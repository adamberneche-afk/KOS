## LESSON 07: ETHICAL DILEMMAS DISCUSSION

**Unit:** Professionalism and Business Ethics

**Duration:** 1 Class Period

**Points:** 20

### I. COMPETENCY ALIGNMENT

**Duty Areas:** Addressing Ethical Issues

**Specific Competencies Covered:**

  - **004:** Demonstrate integrity.
  - **006:** Demonstrate conflict-resolution skills.
  - **020:** Demonstrate professionalism.
  - **029:** Explain the nature of business ethics.
  - **031:** Demonstrate ethical behavior in business relationships.

### II. TEACHER INSTRUCTIONS

**Bell Ringer (The Do Now):**

"You find a wallet with 100 dollars cash and an ID inside in the school parking lot. No one saw you pick it up. What do you do? Be honest."

**The Hook (Classroom Opener):**

"Warren Buffett said it takes 20 years to build a reputation and five minutes to ruin it. In business, you will often face choices where the profitable thing or the easy thing is not the right thing. Today, we are going to practice making those calls before real money and jobs are on the line."

**Quick Grading Tip:**

This is a Discussion Board assignment.

  - **Initial Post (10 points):** Did they choose a scenario and explain their reaction professionally?
  - **Peer Replies (10 points):** Did they reply to **two** classmates with constructive feedback?

**Checkout Slip (Exit Ticket):**

"Which scenario was the hardest for you to decide on? Why is it sometimes hard to do the right thing when friends or supervisors are involved?"

### 

### III. CANVAS ASSIGNMENT TEXT (STUDENT VIEW)

**Title:** Discussion: Ethical Dilemmas in the Workplace

**Description:**

In the business world, the "right answer" is not always easy to find. Ethical leaders cannot stand back and let unethical behavior take place.

**The Task:**

  - **Select:** Pick **one** of the four scenarios below.
  - **Post:** Tell us which scenario you chose. Then, outline how you would react. Be sure to maintain professionalism in your planned response.
  - **Reply:** Once you have posted your response, reply to **two** of your classmates.

**Scenario 1: The Server**

Overhearing coworkers making derogatory comments about customers. Do you confront them?

**Scenario 2: The Hiring Manager**

Considering hiring a qualified friend. Is this favoritism?

**Scenario 3: The Treasurer**

Asked to fudge numbers in the budget to cover costs. Everyone else agrees. What do you do?

**Scenario 4: The Bystander**

Witnessing intimidation of a younger student in the hallway. How do you intervene?

**Submission:**

Post your initial response and two replies in the Discussion Board.
