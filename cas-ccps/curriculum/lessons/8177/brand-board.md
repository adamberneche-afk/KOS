# **Lesson Plan: The Brand Board Project**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

Students will define the concept of "Brand" as the reputation of an organization or venue formed by consistent actions. They will apply these principles by creating a "Brand Board" for a startup in the Sports/Entertainment industry. This board serves as the visual and ideological foundation for an event's identity, ensuring that "Atmospherics" and marketing messages are interconnected.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 1:** Demonstrate creativity and innovation (Designing the visual identity).
  - **Competency 11:** Demonstrate big-picture thinking (Understanding how a font or color choice impacts the entire event's reputation).
  - **Competency 21:** Demonstrate reading and writing skills (Defining brand principles and emotional adjectives).
  - **Competency 81:** Identify the elements of a brand (Defining the core visual and ideological components of a sports/entertainment entity).
  - **Competency 85:** Explain the components of venue management (Understanding "Atmospherics"—how colors and themes create a physical environment).
  - **Competency 93:** Describe factors that motivate consumers to attend sports, entertainment, and event occasions (Using brand identity to create emotional pull).
  - **Competency 97:** Develop a marketing plan for an event (The Brand Board serves as the "Visual Identity" section of the plan).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Define a "brand" as a reputation managed through consistent visual and ethical standards.
2.  Select a cohesive color palette and typography that aligns with venue atmospherics.
3.  Synthesize visual elements (colors, fonts, logos, images) into a coherent Brand Board that motivates consumer attendance.
4.  Explain how visual design serves as a silent "team member" in managing a sports or entertainment venue.

## **4. Materials & Resources**

  - Graphic Design Tool (Canva, Adobe Express, or Google Slides).
  - CHHS Student Handbook (for ensuring school-appropriate imagery).
  - Case studies of "Venue Rebranding" (e.g., a stadium renovation and its new visual identity).

## **5. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook (10 Minutes)**

  - **The Management Perspective:** I’ll share how one of my companies had to completely change its "look" because our customers didn't feel the "vibe" we were selling.
  - **Discussion:** "If this classroom were a stadium, what colors and music would make fans feel excited vs. relaxed?"

### **B. Direct Instruction: Branding for Events (15 Minutes)**

  - **Atmospherics (Comp 85):** How branding moves from a computer screen to the physical walls of a venue.
  - **Motivation (Comp 93):** The psychology of color in sports (e.g., why "Power" colors like Black and Gold are used for aggressive brands).

### **C. Guided Application: The Identity Persona (20 Minutes)**

  - Students brainstorm a startup event or venue (e.g., an E-sports Arena or a Music Festival).
  - Reflection: Students select **3 emotional adjectives** that act as the "North Star" for their management decisions.

### **D. Independent Practice: Building the Board (40 Minutes)**

Students create their boards including:

  - **Colors:** 5 signature colors (The "Venue Palette").
  - **Typography:** One specific font that conveys the event's "voice."
  - **Logos:** Two versions for different applications (e.g., one for a jersey, one for a digital ticket).
  - **Principles:** The 3 adjectives.
  - **Imagery:** 4 images representing the "Fan Experience."

## **7. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Professionalism (Faculty 4.2):** As we mentor, we treat every student's creative vision with respect, even if their "brand" is unconventional.
  - **Student Conduct:** No imagery depicting prohibited items per the CHHS handbook.
  - **Flexibility:** As always, the "Human First" policy applies—if a student struggles with the tech, we provide alternative ways to show their brand vision.

## **8. Reflection & Connection**

Closing Question: "If a fan saw your Brand Board but didn't know the name of your company, would they still know what kind of 'experience' they were buying a ticket for?"
