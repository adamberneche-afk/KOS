# **Lesson Plan: Game Day Takeover (Running an Event & Thanking the Team)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

Students have planned events on paper all year. In this lesson they run one. The class manages a Cavalier Shop pop-up at a CHHS home game: they build the run of show, staff it, sell the products they made in class, handle the money, and close it out. Afterward they do what professional event managers do and most first-timers forget: thank everyone who made it possible, and judge the event against its goals. If a live game-day event isn't approved, the class runs the store's busiest regular shift as the event instead.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 10:** Collaborate with team members (Running the pop-up as a crew with assigned roles).
  - **Competency 58:** Demonstrate customer-service techniques (Serving game-day customers quickly and politely).
  - **Competency 95:** Analyze personnel needs for an event (Building the staffing chart and shift schedule).
  - **Competency 99:** Develop follow-up activities to recognize and thank participants after an event (Writing thank-you notes and a public thank-you to everyone who made the event possible).
  - **Competency 100:** (Optional) Manage a sports, entertainment, and event occasion (Running the Cavalier Shop pop-up at a home game from setup to closeout).
  - **Competency 101:** (Optional) Evaluate the event’s overall success (Comparing sales, customers and wait times against the goals set before the event).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Build a run of show and a staffing chart for a real event.
2.  Manage an event on the day: setup, sales, customer service, problem-solving and closeout.
3.  Identify everyone who contributed to an event and recognize them with professional thank-you notes and a public thank-you.
4.  Evaluate an event against goals set in advance and recommend what to keep and what to change.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

This lesson runs over two class periods with the event in between. **Day 1** plans the event (prompt Parts 1–2). **Event day** is the pop-up itself. **Day 2** is the follow-up: thank-yous and the evaluation (Parts 3–5).

#### **Day 1: The Plan (65 Minutes)**

### **A. The Hook: What Does an Event Manager Do All Night? (10 Minutes)**

  - **Discussion:** Ask: *"When you walk into a game, what had to happen before you got there, and what happens after you leave?"* Build a quick before/during/after list on the board. Point out that the "after" column is usually the shortest, and that's a mistake.

### **B. The Run of Show & Staffing (25 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 100):** A run of show is the event's minute-by-minute schedule: setup, doors, peak times (pregame, halftime), closeout and cleanup. Build the pop-up's run of show together, using the game schedule.
  - **Staffing (Comp 95):** Assign roles from the Employee Handbook: shift lead, cashier, sales floor/runner, inventory and setup crew. Every role needs a backup.
  - **Goals:** Set the targets now so Day 2 has something to measure: items sold, revenue, number of customers and the longest acceptable wait.

### **C. The Manager's Checklist (30 Minutes)**

  - **Group Work:** Students complete Parts 1–2 of the prompt: the run of show, staffing chart, supply list (inventory count, starting cash and card reader, signage, table), and one risk with a backup plan (rain, a long line, a card reader that won't connect).

#### **Event Day: The Pop-Up**

  - **Manage It (Comp 100 & 58):** Students run the pop-up with the run of show and staffing chart. Shift leads keep a short event log: sales counts, problems and how they were solved. Closeout follows the Employee Handbook's cash and Z-out procedures.

#### **Day 2: The Follow-Up (65 Minutes)**

### **D. Who Made This Happen? (15 Minutes)**

  - **Discussion (Comp 99):** List everyone the event depended on: the athletic director who approved it, custodians who set up tables and cleaned up, administrators, teachers who covered or volunteered, parent helpers, and the customers. Ask: *"Which of these people will we need again next time?"* (All of them.)

### **E. Saying Thank You (25 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 99):** Professional follow-up happens within a few days. A good thank-you is specific (what the person did and why it mattered), sincere and short. Recognition can be private (a handwritten note) or public (a social post or a morning-announcement shout-out).
  - **Independent Work:** Students write one handwritten thank-you note to a staff member and draft one public thank-you to customers (Part 3).

### **F. Was It a Success? (25 Minutes)**

  - **Evaluation (Comp 101):** Students compare the event log to the Day 1 goals and complete Parts 4–5: what the numbers show, one thing to keep and one thing to change for the next event.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Game Day Takeover**

Students submit the student prompt's five parts:

1.  **The Run of Show:** The event timeline and each student's role and shift.
2.  **The Manager's Checklist:** The staffing chart, supply list, goals, and one risk with a backup plan.
3.  **The Thank-Yous:** A list of everyone who contributed, one handwritten thank-you note, and one public thank-you.
4.  **The Results:** The event's numbers compared with its goals.
5.  **Keep and Change:** One thing to keep and one thing to change, backed by the results.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Approval First:** A game-day pop-up needs administration and athletic department approval before it is scheduled. Sales and money handling follow CCPS financial guidelines and the Employee Handbook; students do not take money home or leave the cash drawer unattended.
  - **Photos and Privacy:** A public thank-you post shows products and the store, not students, unless the students in the photo have a media release on file. Never post a customer's name without permission.
  - **Integrity:** Event logs and results report what actually happened. Rounding sales up to meet a goal is an integrity violation, not good marketing.
