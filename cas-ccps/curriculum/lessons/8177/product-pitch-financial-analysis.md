# **Lesson Plan: The Venue Buyer's Product Pitch**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

In this lesson, students will take on the role of a Merchandise Buyer for a sports/entertainment venue (The Cavalier Shop). They are tasked with identifying, sourcing, and pitching a single new SKU (Stock Keeping Unit) to be added to the store's inventory. To successfully "sell" their idea to management, they must back up their creative concept with hard financial data, including margin, break-even, and projected profit.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 2:** Demonstrate critical thinking and problem solving (Evaluating suppliers and financial viability).
  - **Competency 7:** Demonstrate listening and speaking skills (Delivering a persuasive, professional pitch).
  - **Competency 19:** Apply mathematical skills to job-specific tasks (Calculating retail margins, turnover, and break-even points).
  - **Competency 93:** Describe factors that motivate consumers to attend sports, entertainment, and event occasions (Explaining *why* the product will sell to the CHHS fan base).
  - **Competency 96:** Analyze the financial resources required for each event activity (Determining the upfront capital needed for the initial supplier order and the expected ROI).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Source a viable product and supplier for a niche retail venue.
2.  Calculate key retail metrics: margin, break-even point, and expected profit.
3.  Formulate a pricing strategy that balances consumer demand with venue profitability.
4.  Deliver a persuasive, data-driven business pitch to a management team.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Buyer's Dilemma (10 Minutes)**

  - **Discussion:** What is the difference between a product we *like* and a product that makes *business sense*?
  - **Concept:** Introduce the "SKU" (Stock Keeping Unit) and the concept of shelf space as "rent" – every item must earn its keep.

### **B. Retail Math Crash Course (20 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 19):** Walk through the formulas for:

  - **Margin:** (Retail Price - Cost) / Retail Price
  - **Break-Even Point:** Fixed Costs (or Total Order Cost) / Profit Per Unit
  - **Turnover Rate:** How fast the inventory will sell out during the sports season/semester.

### **C. Sourcing & Strategy Lab (40 Minutes)**

  - **Independent Work:** Students research potential products online (e.g., custom rally towels, limited edition spirit pins, specialized stadium seats).
  - Students use Google Sheets/Excel to calculate their order costs and projections.

### **D. The Pitch (30 Minutes)**

  - **Presentations:** Students have 3 minutes to present their SKU to "Management" (Mr. Berneche and the class).

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Product Pitch**

Each student's pitch (whether presented via slide deck or formal memo) **must** include:

1.  **The Product:** Name and detailed description of the SKU.
2.  **The "Why":** A compelling argument for why it will sell well to the CHHS community (Fan Motivation).
3.  **The Source:** A verified supplier/vendor for this product (including links/contact info).
4.  **Pricing Strategy:** Your suggested retail price and the psychological/economic strategy behind it.
5.  **Turnover Rate:** The expected timeframe to sell through the initial order.
6.  **Financial Breakdown:** The mathematical calculations for the profit margin, the break-even point on the initial order, and the expected total profit.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Core Values (Accountability & Integrity):** Students must use real, accurate numbers from actual suppliers. Plagiarizing costs or making up math violates the integrity expectation.
  - **Appropriateness:** All pitched products must strictly adhere to the CHHS Student Handbook (no weapons, inappropriate language, or prohibited substances/paraphernalia).
