## Prompt

Assignment: The Brand Board

Invent a new event or venue (for example, an esports arena, a music festival or a minor-league team) and build a brand board for it: one page that shows exactly how it should look and feel.

Part 1: The Idea. In two or three sentences, describe your event or venue and who it is for.

Part 2: Three Words. Choose three adjectives that describe how you want customers to feel. Explain why you chose each one.

Part 3: The Board. Build your board in Canva, Adobe Express or Google Slides, and add it to this document (an image or a link). It must have:

- 5 colors (your color palette)
- 1 font that matches your brand's voice
- 2 versions of your logo, one for a jersey or sign and one for a digital ticket or app
- your three words
- 4 images that show the experience a customer will have

Part 4: Explain It. For your colors, your font and your images, explain how each choice matches your three words. Then explain how your colors and logo would show up inside the actual venue.

Use these terms accurately: brand, brand identity, color palette, typography, logo, atmospherics.

## Background

A brand is more than a logo. It's what people think and feel when they hear your name, built by everything you do the same way, every time. A logo, colors and a font are the visual part of a brand. They're how customers recognize you and what tells them what kind of experience to expect.

Why consistency matters: when every ticket, sign, jersey and social post uses the same colors, font and logo, the brand feels professional and trustworthy. When every item looks different, customers can't tell what you stand for.

How the pieces work:

- Colors: colors set a mood. Many brands that want to feel bold and powerful use black, gold or red, while brands that want to feel calm or friendly often use blue or green. A good palette has one or two main colors, a couple of supporting colors, and at least one neutral (white, gray or black) so text is easy to read.
- Typography (fonts): a font has a personality. Thick, blocky letters feel strong and loud. Thin, rounded letters feel modern or friendly. Pick one that sounds like your brand.
- Logo versions: a logo has to work everywhere. A detailed logo may look great on a banner but turn into a blur on a phone screen, so most brands have a full version and a simple version.
- Atmospherics: in a venue, the brand comes off the screen and onto the walls. The paint colors, lighting, music and signs make customers feel the brand the moment they walk in.

A brand board is the one-page guide a design team follows, so everyone who makes a sign, a shirt or a social post keeps the brand consistent.

## Key terms

- Brand: the reputation and feeling people connect with a business, built by how it looks and how it acts.
- Brand identity: the visual parts of a brand: logo, colors, fonts and images.
- Color palette: the small set of colors a brand always uses.
- Typography: the fonts a brand uses and how they're arranged.
- Logo: the symbol or design that identifies a brand.
- Atmospherics: the colors, lighting, music and decor that create the feel of a physical space.
- Brand board: a one-page guide that shows a brand's colors, fonts, logos and images together.

## Example

A short sample for an imaginary esports arena called "The Grid."

- Three words: electric, competitive, welcoming.
- Colors: neon green and electric purple (electric), black (competitive), plus white and light gray so text is easy to read.
- Font: a bold, squared-off font that looks like a video game scoreboard.
- Logos: a full logo with "THE GRID" and a lightning-bolt grid for banners and jerseys, and just the lightning bolt for the app icon and digital tickets.
- Images: a packed crowd cheering under purple lights, players at their stations, a family playing together in the free-play zone, a team lifting a trophy.
- In the venue: purple lighting over the stage, neon-green lines on the floor leading to the seats, and the lightning bolt on every screen between matches.
