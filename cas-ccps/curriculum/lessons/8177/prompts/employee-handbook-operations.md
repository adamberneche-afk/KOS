## Prompt

Assignment: The Cavalier Shop Operations Brief

Using the handbook summary above, show that you can run the shop.

Part 1: The Team. For each role (General Manager, Shift Manager, Sales Associate, Inventory Specialist), give one responsibility and explain what goes wrong if nobody does it.

Part 2: Open and Close. List the four opening procedures in order. Then explain the closing "Z-Out" in your own words: what gets counted, what gets subtracted, and what it's checked against.

Part 3: Integrity and Shrink. Explain the "Two-Person Rule" and the "scan everything" rule. For each one, describe a real problem it prevents.

Part 4: The Rush. A wave of customers comes in just as the register paper jams. You're the Shift Manager. Who does what, and what exactly do you say to the customers waiting in line?

Use these terms accurately: SOP, float, Z-Out, shrink, FIFO, facing.

## Background

Why does a Starbucks in Richmond run exactly like one in Seattle? Every store follows the same written steps, called Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). With SOPs, a store runs the same way every shift, no matter who is working. The Cavalier Shop runs on SOPs too. They are in its Employee Handbook, summarized here.

The Team:

- General Manager (the teacher): runs the whole store, does the final money count and the final security check.
- Shift Manager (a student leader): assigns tasks, handles customer problems, checks inventory counts and fills out the Shift Summary Report.
- Sales Associate (cashier and greeter): runs the register, greets every customer and keeps the checkout area clean.
- Inventory Specialist: restocks shelves from the back room, faces the merchandise and tells the Shift Manager when stock is running low.

Opening, in order:

- Step 1, Safety: unlock the doors and make sure the 36-inch walkways are completely clear of boxes. This is required by ADA and fire code.
- Step 2, Count the float: the Shift Manager and the General Manager count the starting cash in the drawer and sign the daily log.
- Step 3, Set the mood: turn on the store lights and the approved music.
- Step 4, Get the shelves ready: face every product, make sure size tags show, and turn on the signs and register iPads.

Closing:

- Sweep the store: check every corner so all customers are out.
- Restock: fold clothing, replace sold items from the back room and sweep the floor.
- Z-Out: count the drawer, subtract the float, and match the total against the register's daily sales report.
- Lock up: turn off the electronics, hand the bank bag to the General Manager and lock every door.

Money and Inventory Rules:

- Never leave the cash drawer open between sales. If the count is off, tell the General Manager right away.
- Scan everything that leaves the store, even a free giveaway.
- Two-Person Rule: two people always count cash and high-value stock together.

On the Sales Floor:

- Wear your Cavalier Shop polo or lanyard so customers know you work there.
- No personal phones on the sales floor. For an emergency, step into the back room.
- Greet every customer: "Welcome to the Cavalier Shop!" Use professional language, not slang.

Hours: all four lunch blocks (11:30 AM to 1:30 PM), and some home football and basketball games. Arrive 10 minutes before your shift to help open.

## Key terms

- SOP (Standard Operating Procedure): a written, step-by-step way to do a task, so everyone does it the same way.
- Float: the cash in the register drawer at the start of a shift, used to make change.
- Z-Out: the end-of-day register report and cash count that closes out the day's sales.
- Shrink: stock lost to theft, damage or mistakes, so there is less on the shelves than the records say.
- FIFO (First In, First Out): sell the oldest stock first, so nothing sits until it is outdated or damaged.
- Facing: pulling products to the front edge of the shelf with labels forward, so the shelves look full and neat.

## Example

A Z-Out with sample numbers. The drawer started the day with a $100 float. At closing it holds $340 in cash. Take away the float: $340 − $100 = $240 in cash sales. The register's daily sales report also says $240 in cash sales, so the drawer balances. If the report said $250, the drawer would be $10 short, and the Shift Manager would tell the General Manager right away.
