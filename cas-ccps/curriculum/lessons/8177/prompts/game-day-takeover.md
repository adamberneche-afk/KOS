## Prompt

Assignment: The Game Day Takeover

The Cavalier Shop is taking over a home game. Our class will run a pop-up shop at the game, selling the products we made in class, from setup to cleanup. Then we'll do the part most first-time event managers forget: thank the people who made it happen and find out whether it worked.

This assignment takes two days with the event in between. Day 1: Parts 1–2. After the event: Parts 3–5.

Part 1: The Run of Show. Build the pop-up's timeline, from setup to cleanup, using the game schedule your teacher shares. Mark the busy times (before the game and at halftime). Then write your role and your shift.

Part 2: The Manager's Checklist. Complete:

- a staffing chart: every role (shift lead, cashier, sales floor/runner, inventory and setup), who fills it, and who backs them up
- a supply list: inventory count, starting cash and card reader, signs, table and anything else the shop needs
- the event's goals: items sold, revenue, number of customers, and the longest wait you'd accept
- one risk and your backup plan (for example: rain, a long line, or a card reader that won't connect)

Part 3: Say Thank You. After the event:

- List everyone who helped make the event happen, and what each person or group did.
- Write a handwritten thank-you note to one staff member on your list. Be specific about what they did and why it mattered. Type the note's words into this document before you deliver it.
- Draft a public thank-you to our customers, for social media or the morning announcements.

Part 4: The Results. Compare what happened with the goals from Part 2. Use the real numbers from the event log. Then describe your own shift: what you did, one customer you served, and one problem that came up and how it was handled.

Part 5: Keep and Change. Name one thing to keep and one thing to change for the next event, and explain each using the results.

Use these terms accurately: event manager, run of show, staffing chart, closeout, follow-up, recognition, goal.

## Background

Every event has three parts: before, during and after. Fans only see the middle. Event managers are judged on all three.

Before: the plan.

- A run of show is the event's schedule, minute by minute: when setup starts, when doors open, the busy times, when sales stop, and when cleanup is done.
- A staffing chart lists every job, who does it, and who covers it if that person is out. On a busy night, every role needs a backup.
- Goals are set before the event, not after, so you can honestly judge how it went.

During: running it.

- Follow the run of show, but expect surprises. A good manager solves the problem and writes down what happened.
- Customer service still counts at a fast-moving table: greet people, keep the line moving, and thank every customer.
- Closeout follows the Employee Handbook: count the money, run the Z-out report, count the inventory that's left, and pack up.

After: the follow-up.

- Recognize people. Events depend on people the crowd never sees: the athletic director who approved it, custodians who set up and cleaned, teachers and parents who helped, and the customers who showed up.
- Thank them within a few days. A good thank-you is specific ("Thank you for setting up our table before the gates opened"), sincere and short.
- Recognition can be private, like a handwritten note, or public, like a social post or a shout-out on the morning announcements.
- People who feel appreciated say yes again. A thank-you is how you get help for the next event.
- Evaluate it. Compare the results with your goals, then decide what to keep and what to change.

Public posts show the shop and the products. Don't post photos of students without a media release on file, and don't name customers without their permission.

## Key terms

- Event manager: the person responsible for planning, running and following up on an event.
- Run of show: a minute-by-minute schedule for an event.
- Staffing chart: a list of every job at an event, who does it, and who backs them up.
- Closeout: the steps that end a shift or event, like counting money and inventory.
- Follow-up: what an event team does after the event, including thanking people and evaluating results.
- Recognition: publicly or privately showing appreciation for someone's work.
- Goal: a specific target set before the event, used to judge its success.

## Example

A sample handwritten thank-you note.

"Dear Mr. Lewis, thank you for setting up our tables and power cord before the gates opened on Friday. Because everything was ready early, we started selling during warm-ups and sold 40 items before kickoff. We couldn't have done it without you. Sincerely, the Cavalier Shop team"
