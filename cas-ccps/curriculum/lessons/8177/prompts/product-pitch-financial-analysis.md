## Prompt

Assignment: The Buyer's Product Pitch

You are the merchandise buyer for the Cavalier Shop. Find one new product the store should sell, and write a pitch to the General Manager that proves it will make money.

Part 1: The Product. Name the product and describe it: what it is, what it looks like, and what makes it different from what the store already sells.

Part 2: The Why. Explain why CHHS students, families and fans will buy it. Who is it for, and when will they want it (a season, a big game, a spirit week)?

Part 3: The Source. Name a real supplier, with a link. Give the cost per item, the minimum order, and any setup or shipping fees.

Part 4: The Price. Give your retail price and explain the strategy behind it.

Part 5: The Math. Show every calculation: profit per item, margin, total order cost, break-even point, expected profit if the whole order sells, and how long you expect it to take to sell out (turnover).

Use real numbers from a real supplier. Your product must follow the CHHS Student Handbook.

Use these terms accurately: SKU, supplier, cost, retail price, margin, break-even point, turnover.

## Background

A store has limited shelf space, so every product has to earn its spot. Think of shelf space like rent: if a product doesn't sell, it's taking up space a better product could use. A buyer's job is to find products customers want and that make money for the store. A product you like isn't automatically a good business decision. The numbers have to work.

How a buyer decides:

- Know the customer. Who will buy it, and why? School pride, a big rivalry game, cold weather and gift-giving season all drive what sells.
- Find a supplier. A supplier (or vendor) sells products to stores in bulk at a lower price. Suppliers often have a minimum order, and custom items may have a setup fee.
- Set the price. The retail price has to cover the cost and leave a profit, but it also has to be a price students will actually pay. Common strategies: ending a price in .99 so it feels lower ($9.99 instead of $10), pricing to match similar items, or bundling two items for a deal.
- Run the numbers before ordering. The store pays the supplier up front, before a single item sells, so the buyer needs to know how many must sell just to get that money back.

The formulas:

- Profit per item = Retail price − Cost per item
- Margin = (Retail price − Cost per item) ÷ Retail price, written as a percent
- Total order cost = (Cost per item × Number ordered) + setup and shipping fees
- Break-even point = Total order cost ÷ Profit per item. This is how many you must sell to get the store's money back.
- Expected profit = (Retail price × Number ordered) − Total order cost
- Turnover: how fast the order sells out, for example "in about five weeks."

## Key terms

- SKU (Stock Keeping Unit): the code a store uses to track one specific product, like one color and size of one shirt.
- Buyer: the person who chooses which products a store sells and orders them.
- Supplier (vendor): a company that sells products to stores in bulk.
- Cost: what the store pays the supplier for each item.
- Retail price: what the customer pays the store.
- Margin: the share of the retail price the store keeps as profit, as a percent.
- Break-even point: the number of items the store must sell to earn back what it spent on the order.
- Turnover: how quickly a product sells and has to be restocked.
- ROI (return on investment): the profit compared with what was spent, as a percent.

## Example

A rally towel pitch, with the math. The supplier charges $3.00 per towel, with a 100-towel minimum and a $50 setup fee for the CHHS logo.

- Total order cost: ($3.00 × 100) + $50 = $350
- Retail price: $8.00, less than a ticket, so it's an easy add-on at a game
- Profit per towel: $8.00 − $3.00 = $5.00
- Margin: $5.00 ÷ $8.00 = 62.5%
- Break-even: $350 ÷ $5.00 = 70 towels. After the 70th towel sells, the rest is profit.
- Expected profit if all 100 sell: ($8.00 × 100) − $350 = $450
- ROI: $450 ÷ $350 ≈ 129%
- Turnover: at about 20 towels a week during football season, the order sells out in about 5 weeks.
