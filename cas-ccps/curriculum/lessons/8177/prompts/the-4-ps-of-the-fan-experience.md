## Prompt

Assignment: The Signature Item

Your marketing team is inventing a signature item for a local venue: a new concession food or a piece of merchandise fans can only get there (for example, a new hot dog for the Richmond Flying Squirrels, or a VIP merch bundle for a concert at The National). Your team will pitch it to the class. In this document, write up your team's plan in your own words.

Part 1: Product. Describe the item and what makes it special to this team or venue.

Part 2: Price. Give the price, the estimated cost to make it, and the profit on each one. Explain why fans will pay that price.

Part 3: Place. Explain exactly where fans can buy it, and why you chose that spot (only in one section of the stadium, only in the team app, only on game days).

Part 4: Promotion. Describe one specific way you will launch it and get fans talking.

Part 5: How They Connect. Pick one P and explain what would happen to the other three if you changed it. For example, what happens if you double the price?

Part 6: Your Part. Name the P you will present in the team pitch.

Use these terms accurately: marketing mix, product, price, place, promotion, perceived value.

## Background

A bottle of water costs about 20 cents at the grocery store. At an NFL game it can cost $5 or more. Same water. What changed is where it's sold, how it's priced and how it's presented, and that's the marketing mix at work.

The marketing mix is the set of four decisions every business makes about anything it sells. They're called the 4 Ps:

- Product: what you're selling. In sports, that's more than the game itself: the food, the merchandise, the halftime show and the whole experience.
- Price: what the customer pays. Think ticket levels, premium seats, value meals and bundle deals. Price has to cover the cost and leave a profit, and it has to match what fans think the item is worth.
- Place (also called distribution): where and how customers can buy it. Tickets on Ticketmaster or at the box office; merchandise in the stadium, in the team store or online.
- Promotion: how customers find out about it and why they want it now. Bobblehead giveaway nights, social media posts, ads on the video board, influencer videos.

The 4 Ps work together. Change one and the others have to change too. A higher price needs a more special product and stronger promotion to justify it. Selling only in one spot (place) can make an item feel rare, which can support a higher price. When the 4 Ps line up, customers believe the item is worth it. That belief is called perceived value.

Profit on each item = price − cost to make it.

## Key terms

- Marketing mix: the four decisions (product, price, place, promotion) a business makes about what it sells.
- Product: the good, service or experience being sold.
- Price: what the customer pays.
- Place (distribution): where and how the customer can buy the product.
- Promotion: how a business tells customers about a product and makes them want it.
- Perceived value: how much a customer believes something is worth, which may be more or less than it cost to make.
- Scarcity: when something is limited or hard to get, which can make people want it more.

## Example

A short sample plan for a minor-league baseball team.

- Product: the "Grand Slam Dog," a foot-long hot dog topped with mac and cheese and crushed chips, served in a mini souvenir batting helmet.
- Price: $14. It costs about $4.50 to make, including the helmet, so the profit is $9.50 each. Fans pay more because the helmet is a keepsake.
- Place: sold only at one stand behind home plate, so finding it feels like part of the game.
- Promotion: the team mascot eats one on the video board during the third inning, and the first 100 fans to post a photo with the team's hashtag win a free one.
- How they connect: if the price went up to $20, the team would need a bigger promotion, like a limited-edition helmet color each month, to keep fans buying.
