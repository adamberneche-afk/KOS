## Prompt

Assignment: The Fan Persona Profile

Why would anyone pay to sit in the cold when they could watch the game for free on a big TV at home? Your job is to figure out what gets one specific type of person off the couch, and how to win their money for a local event.

Choose one persona:

- The Parkers: a family of four in Chesterfield with kids ages 5 and 8. Entertainment budget: about $200 a month.
- Jordan: a 25-year-old single professional who just moved to Richmond for work. Entertainment budget: about $300 a month.
- The Hendersons: a retired couple in their late 60s. Entertainment budget: about $150 a month.

Pick a real local event for them: a Richmond Flying Squirrels game, a VCU basketball game, a concert at the Altria Theater, or another Richmond-area event.

Part 1: The Persona. Describe your persona: who they are, what a typical week looks like for them, and how much of their budget one outing might cost.

Part 2: The Motivator. What would make them want to leave the house? Pick their top motivator from the five in the background, and explain why it fits them.

Part 3: The Competition. Name one specific local indirect competitor that could win their money on a Friday night instead (for example, Topgolf, Dave & Buster's, Kings Dominion, a movie theater, Richmond Raceway). Explain why it's a real threat for this persona.

Part 4: The Winning Strategy. Describe one specific promotion or experience the event could offer that would make your persona choose it over the competitor. Explain how it matches their motivator and their budget.

Put your profile on one page in Canva or Google Slides and add it (or its link) to this document.

Use these terms accurately: discretionary income, motivator, direct competition, indirect competition, consumer persona.

## Background

A big 4K TV has a better view, cheaper snacks and no traffic. So why do thousands of people still buy tickets? Because sports and entertainment don't really sell a game or a show. They sell an experience and a memory you can't get from your couch.

The money they're fighting for: discretionary income is what's left after paying for needs like housing, food and gas. It's the money people choose how to spend, and every movie theater, restaurant, theme park and team is competing for the same dollars.

Five common reasons people go to live events (motivators):

- Socialization: hanging out with friends; the game is the backdrop.
- Entertainment: the excitement of the event itself (the action, the music, the show).
- Family bonding: making memories together and giving kids something to do.
- Status and business: being seen at the big event, or hosting clients in a suite.
- FOMO (fear of missing out): not wanting to be the only one who wasn't there when everyone posts about it.

The same event attracts different kinds of fans:

- The die-hard: cares about the score and the win.
- The socializer: there for the friends and the photos, and may not know the score.
- The corporate client: there to build a business relationship in a premium seat.

Two kinds of competition:

- Direct competition: the same kind of entertainment, like two concerts on the same night.
- Indirect competition: a different kind of entertainment competing for the same time and money, like a baseball game vs. a night at Topgolf.

A consumer persona is a made-up but realistic profile of one type of customer. Marketers use personas to step outside their own point of view and think about what someone different from them actually wants.

## Key terms

- Discretionary income: money left after paying for necessities, which people can spend however they choose.
- Motivator: the reason a person decides to do something, like attend an event.
- Consumer persona: a realistic profile of one type of customer, used to plan marketing.
- Direct competition: businesses offering the same kind of product to the same customers.
- Indirect competition: businesses offering a different product that competes for the same customer's time or money.
- FOMO: fear of missing out on an experience others are having.

## Example

A short sample profile for a persona not on the list: Maya, a 16-year-old high school junior with about $40 a month to spend.

- Motivator: socialization. She goes where her friends go, and wants photos to post.
- Competition: a movie and food in Short Pump costs her friend group about the same as a ticket.
- Winning strategy: a student night with $8 tickets for anyone with a school ID, a photo booth with the team mascot, and a section just for students, so going to the game becomes the social plan, not just the backdrop.
