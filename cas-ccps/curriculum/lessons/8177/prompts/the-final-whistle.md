## Prompt

Assignment: The Free Agent Portfolio

The season's over. Like a front office after the final game, you'll evaluate an event, then evaluate yourself, and get ready to apply for a job in sports and entertainment.

Part 1: The Post-Event Evaluation. Pick a real school event you attended or helped with (a game, a dance, a spirit week, the Cavalier Shop's busiest day). Write a SWOT analysis: at least two strengths, two weaknesses, two opportunities and two threats. Then name one thing you'd measure next time to judge success (attendance, profit, survey ratings, social media comments).

Part 2: Your Career Paths. Name three different jobs in sports, entertainment or event management that interest you. For each, write one sentence on what the job involves and one sentence on which project from this class prepared you for it.

Part 3: Your Resume Bullets. Write five resume bullet points based on projects from this class. Each one must start with an action verb, describe a specific task, and include a result or number where you can. Link your full one-page resume if you have one.

Part 4: Your STAR Stories. Write three STAR stories from this year (group projects, the Cavalier Shop, survey fieldwork, a negotiation) that you could use to answer interview questions like "Tell me about a time you solved a problem."

Part 5: Your Network. Name one professional organization (like DECA) you could join and one person or organization you could ask for an informational interview. Explain how each could help your career.

Everything on a resume must be true. Never exaggerate what you did.

Use these terms accurately: SWOT analysis, KPI, ROI, resume, action verb, STAR method, networking, informational interview.

## Background

Every big event ends with an evaluation. The front office looks at the numbers, the problems and the feedback, and decides what to do differently next time. You can do the same thing with your own skills.

How to judge whether an event succeeded:

- Financial: did it hit its budget and profit goal? Return on investment (ROI) compares what you earned with what you spent.
- Operational: did things run smoothly, or were there long lines, bottlenecks or safety issues?
- Reputational: what are people saying afterward, in surveys and on social media?

The numbers you choose to track are called key performance indicators (KPIs): attendance, revenue, wait time, survey rating.

A SWOT analysis sorts what you learned into four boxes:

- Strengths: what went well (internal, under your control).
- Weaknesses: what went poorly (internal).
- Opportunities: outside chances to do better next time (a new sponsor, a better date).
- Threats: outside risks (bad weather, a competing event the same night).

You don't have to be an athlete to work in sports. Front-office careers include ticket sales, marketing, social media, event operations, sponsorship sales, public relations, facility management, merchandising and data analysis. Professional organizations like DECA, FBLA and the Public Relations Society of America (PRSA) help you build skills, compete and meet people in the industry.

Networking means building relationships with people in the field. An informational interview is a short, polite conversation with someone who has a job you're curious about, where you ask about their career. You're not asking for a job, just advice. A professional online presence (like a LinkedIn profile, when you're old enough) helps people find you.

A strong resume bullet = action verb + specific task + result.

- Weak: "Did a pricing project."
- Strong: "Calculated ticket revenue at five price points for three game scenarios and recommended the most profitable price for each."

The STAR method answers interview questions with a real story:

- Situation: where were you, and what was going on?
- Task: what did you need to do?
- Action: what exactly did you do?
- Result: what happened, and what did you learn?

## Key terms

- SWOT analysis: an evaluation of strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats.
- Key performance indicator (KPI): a number used to measure success, like attendance or revenue.
- Return on investment (ROI): the profit from something compared with what it cost.
- Resume: a one-page summary of your skills, experience and education.
- Action verb: a strong verb that starts a resume bullet, like organized, calculated or designed.
- STAR method: a way to answer interview questions with a story: situation, task, action, result.
- Networking: building professional relationships that can help your career.
- Informational interview: a short conversation with someone in a job you're curious about, to learn about their career.

## Example

A sample STAR story.

- Situation: "During survey fieldwork in my sports management class, our class needed 323 responses, and halfway through the week we had fewer than 100."
- Task: "I had to collect 15 responses myself, and our team needed a better plan."
- Action: "I wrote a 10-second pitch, set up at the cafeteria during two lunch blocks, and asked students at tables outside my friend group."
- Result: "I collected 17 responses, and when I shared my pitch with my team, our class reached its goal two days early."
