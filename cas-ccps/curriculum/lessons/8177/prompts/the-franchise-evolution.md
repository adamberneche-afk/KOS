## Prompt

Assignment: The Brand Revival Pitch

The Richmond Kickers, Richmond's pro soccer team, have had flat ticket sales for three years. The brand is stuck. You're the new brand manager, and your job is to create three new ways to make money that don't depend on selling more regular tickets.

Part 1: The Bundle. Design a bundle for one specific group of fans (college students, families, young professionals). List everything in it, the price of each item on its own, the bundle price, and how much the fan saves. Explain why this group would buy it.

Part 2: The Brand Extension. Propose one new product or service the Kickers could launch that has nothing to do with playing soccer (a summer camp, a co-branded local food item, a podcast). Explain how it uses the Kickers brand and who would buy it.

Part 3: The Naming Rights. Pick one area of the stadium (the main gate, the family zone, a VIP club) and a real local business that should pay to put its name on it. Explain why that business's brand fits that area. No alcohol, tobacco, vaping or gambling companies.

Part 4: The Life Cycle. In two or three sentences, explain which stage of the product life cycle the Kickers are in and how your three ideas help them grow again.

If you use real logos in a mockup, label it "For educational purposes only."

Use these terms accurately: product life cycle, product mix, bundling, brand extension, licensing, naming rights, royalty.

## Background

Why does a plain green t-shirt cost $5, while a green Philadelphia Eagles t-shirt costs $45? The cotton is the same. The difference is the brand. People don't just buy the shirt; they buy what it says about them. A strong brand lets a business charge more, and it can be used to sell things that have nothing to do with the original product.

The product life cycle: most products go through four stages.

- Introduction: the product is new, few people know about it, and sales are slow.
- Growth: word spreads and sales climb quickly.
- Maturity: sales level off. Most people who want it already have it.
- Decline: sales start to fall as interest fades.

A business in maturity has to find new ways to grow, or it slides into decline. The NFL has been around for more than 100 years and is deep in maturity, but it still grows its revenue every year using tools like these:

- Bundling: packaging several products together for one price. A family 4-pack (four tickets, four hot dogs, four drinks for $100) sells food before the fan even walks in.
- Brand extension: using a famous brand to launch a new kind of product. WWE makes movies; minor-league teams open year-round restaurants; the NBA helped launch the WNBA.
- Licensing: letting another company use your logo on its products in exchange for a royalty, a percentage of every sale. A team doesn't make video games, but it gets paid when its logo appears in one.
- Naming rights: a company pays to put its name on a stadium or part of one, like Capital One Arena in Washington, D.C. Naming rights cost far more than a banner because the name shows up everywhere: tickets, TV broadcasts, maps and news stories.

The product mix is everything a business sells: tickets, food, merchandise, camps, licensed products. Growing the product mix creates more ways to make money from the same fans.

## Key terms

- Product life cycle: the four stages a product goes through: introduction, growth, maturity and decline.
- Product mix: all the products and services a business offers.
- Bundling: selling several products together for one price.
- Brand extension: using an existing brand name to launch a new kind of product.
- Licensing: allowing another company to use your brand on its products for a fee.
- Royalty: the percentage of each sale a licensing company pays the brand owner.
- Naming rights: the right to put a company's name on a venue or part of one, for a fee.

## Example

A sample bundle for a different team.

"The Date Night Bundle: two tickets ($18 each), two drinks ($6 each), one shared nachos ($9) and a souvenir photo at the stadium photo booth ($10). Bought separately, that's $67. The bundle is $55, a $12 savings. It's aimed at couples in their 20s, who want a fun, affordable night out and a photo to post, and it gets them spending on food and photos before the game even starts."
