## Prompt

Assignment: The International Expansion Proposal

You are the Vice President of International Development for a U.S. sports team or entertainment brand (an NBA team, WWE, a music festival). Plan one major event in another country, and build a short proposal (Google Slides or Canva, linked or added to this document) that answers each part below.

Part 1: The Destination. Which brand, and which foreign city? Explain why there's a market for your brand there: fans, popularity of the sport, population, or something else you found in your research.

Part 2: The Money. Name the local currency. Set a general admission ticket price in that currency, then convert it to U.S. dollars using today's real exchange rate. Show your math, and name the website where you found the rate (Google Finance or another financial site).

Part 3: The Culture. Describe one specific way you will change your event or marketing to fit the local culture: translating your slogan, changing the food at concessions, partnering with a local star, or adjusting the start time.

Part 4: The Challenge. Describe one real problem of bringing a whole team or event overseas (shipping equipment through customs, jet lag and time zones, tax rules) and how you'd handle it.

Show respect for the culture you're marketing to. Stereotypes or jokes about another culture will not be accepted.

Use these terms accurately: global trade, export, currency, exchange rate, cultural adaptation, time zone.

## Background

Why would the NFL schedule a game to start at 9:30 AM on a Sunday in the U.S.? Because it's being played in London, where it's 2:30 in the afternoon. The NFL has played regular-season games in London since 2007, and more recently in Germany. The NBA, WWE and major music tours do the same thing. In the U.S., most people who would become fans already have. The biggest room to grow is overseas.

Global trade is buying and selling between countries. Sports are full of it:

- A team jersey is often made in Southeast Asia or Central America, shipped to the U.S., and then sold to fans around the world.
- Games and broadcasts are exported too. Selling the rights to show NBA games in another country is selling a U.S. product overseas.

Currency and exchange rates: every country (or group of countries) has its own money: the British pound (£), the euro (€), the Japanese yen (¥). The exchange rate is how much one currency is worth in another, and it changes every day.

- To convert to dollars, multiply the foreign price by the exchange rate (how many dollars one unit of that currency is worth).
- A "strong dollar" buys more foreign currency. That makes U.S. merchandise more expensive for foreign fans, and money earned overseas turns into fewer dollars back home.
- A "weak dollar" does the opposite: U.S. products look cheaper overseas, and foreign earnings turn into more dollars.

Cultural adaptation: you can't copy and paste an American ad into Tokyo or Madrid. You may need to translate the language (and check that your slogan still makes sense), adjust the food, schedule around local customs and time zones, and partner with local stars fans already love. Respect is the rule: a campaign that stereotypes a culture will fail, and it should.

## Key terms

- Global trade: buying and selling goods and services between countries.
- Export: a product or service sold to another country.
- Import: a product or service bought from another country.
- Currency: the money used in a country, like the dollar, pound, euro or yen.
- Exchange rate: how much one currency is worth in another.
- Cultural adaptation: changing a product or message to fit the values and customs of a different culture.
- Time zone: a region that uses the same clock time; London is usually 5 hours ahead of Richmond.

## Example

Currency math with sample rates (yours must use today's real rates).

- A sweatshirt sells for £80 at a game in London. Sample rate: 1 pound = 1.25 dollars. £80 × 1.25 = $100.
- A ticket costs ¥8,000 at a show in Tokyo. Sample rate: 100 yen = 0.65 dollars, so 1 yen = 0.0065 dollars. ¥8,000 × 0.0065 = $52.
- A cultural change: for a Tokyo event, a U.S. festival might add local favorites to the food stands and invite a popular Japanese artist to open the show, so local fans feel the event was made for them, too.
