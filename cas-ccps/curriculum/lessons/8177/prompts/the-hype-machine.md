## Prompt

Assignment: Pack the House

Clover Hill is hosting a Friday night playoff game, but attendance at big games has been low. You're the new event promoter, and you have a $5,000 budget to fill the stands. Build a campaign storyboard (in Canva or right here in this document) with one piece from each part of the promotional mix.

Part 1: The Ad (paid). Create a mockup of an Instagram or TikTok ad, or a poster. Say exactly who it's aimed at, where it will run, and how much of your $5,000 it costs.

Part 2: The PR Stunt (earned). Pitch a newsworthy event before the game that NBC12 or the school newspaper would want to cover for free. Explain why it's news, not just an ad.

Part 3: The Sales Promotion (incentive). Create a short-term deal that gets people to buy tickets early: a contest, a giveaway, a flash sale. Include the deadline.

Part 4: The Budget. List how you'd spend the $5,000, item by item. It must add up to $5,000 or less.

Part 5: Keep It Honest. Explain how your campaign avoids misleading anyone. Would every promise in it hold up on game night?

If you build the storyboard in Canva, add its link here and also type each part into this document: the ad's words, audience, placement and cost; the stunt; the deal and its deadline; the budget lines.

Use these terms accurately: promotional mix, advertising, public relations, publicity, sales promotion, personal selling.

## Background

A great event means nothing if nobody knows about it. Promotion is how a business gets the word out and makes people want to come now. Teams, movie studios and concert promoters build "hype" using a set of tools called the promotional mix.

The promotional mix:

- Advertising (paid): you pay to put your message in front of people. Sponsored Instagram posts for a music festival, a billboard for the Richmond Kickers, a TV commercial. You control the message, but it costs money.
- Public relations and publicity (earned): getting people to talk about you for free. A star athlete visiting a children's hospital ends up on the local news. You don't pay for it, but you also don't control what reporters say.
- Sales promotion (incentives): short-term deals that push people to act now. "First 5,000 fans get a free t-shirt," "Use code CAVALIERS for 20% off," a halftime contest.
- Personal selling (one-on-one): a real person selling directly, like a ticket rep calling a local business to sell a group package, or students selling tickets at a lunch table.

Paid vs. earned: an ad is something you buy. Publicity is something you earn by doing something interesting enough that others want to share it. A strong campaign uses both.

Why campaigns use several tools at once: someone might see your ad, hear about your PR stunt from a friend, and finally buy because of the early-bird deal. Each piece does a different job, and together they reach more people.

Stay honest. Ads and promotions can't make false promises. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) has truth-in-advertising rules, and a "bait and switch" (advertising one thing, then delivering something worse) breaks both the law and your customers' trust.

## Key terms

- Promotion: everything a business does to tell people about its product and make them want it.
- Promotional mix: the four main promotion tools: advertising, public relations, sales promotion and personal selling.
- Advertising: paid messages in media like social media, billboards and TV.
- Public relations (PR): building a good image and relationships with the public and the media.
- Publicity: free media attention a business earns, like a news story.
- Sales promotion: a short-term incentive, like a giveaway or discount, that pushes people to buy now.
- Personal selling: one-on-one selling by a real person.

## Example

A short sample campaign for a charity concert in the school auditorium.

- The Ad: an Instagram story ad aimed at CHHS students and parents within 10 miles, showing the student bands and "Tickets $5 — all proceeds to the food bank." Cost: $400 for two weeks.
- The PR Stunt: the bands play a surprise 15-minute set in the cafeteria during lunch the week before, and invite the school paper and NBC12 to film it.
- The Sales Promotion: everyone who buys a ticket before Wednesday gets entered to win front-row seats and a signed setlist.
- Keep It Honest: the ad says "proceeds to the food bank" only because the money really goes there, and the drawing winner is announced publicly.
