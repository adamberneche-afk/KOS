## Prompt

Assignment: The NIL Activation

You're a brand manager for the Cavalier Shop. Pick one product our class makes (or one CHHS event) and plan a digital endorsement deal with a real public figure to promote it.

Part 1: The Match. Choose a real public figure: a pro or college athlete, a musician, or a well-known local personality. Explain who their followers are, and why that audience matches the people who would buy your product or attend your event. Explain why they're a better fit than someone with more followers.

Part 2: Macro or Micro? Is your choice a macro-influencer or a micro-influencer? Explain one advantage and one risk of that choice.

Part 3: The Post. Design a mockup of the sponsored post (an Instagram post, Reel cover or TikTok frame) in Canva and add it to this document. It must include:

- engaging copy that sounds like the influencer, not like an ad
- a call to action you can measure, like a promo code ("Use code CAVS26 at the Cavalier Shop")
- a clear #ad or #sponsored disclosure that's easy to see

Part 4: Measure It. Explain how you'll know whether the deal worked. What numbers will you track?

Choose someone whose public image fits CHHS's core values: no one known for explicit content or for promoting alcohol, tobacco, vaping or gambling. Label any mockup that uses a real person or logo "For educational purposes only."

Use these terms accurately: endorsement, NIL, influencer, macro-influencer, micro-influencer, call to action, FTC disclosure.

## Background

Why would a company pay an athlete thousands, or even millions, for one social media post instead of buying a billboard? Because fans trust the athlete. When someone they admire uses a product, the product borrows some of that trust. That's what an endorsement is: a well-known person publicly supporting a product.

NIL stands for name, image and likeness. Since 2021, NCAA college athletes have been allowed to earn money from endorsements, and many states now allow some high school athletes to do the same, under their own rules. NIL turned athletes of every size into potential marketing partners.

Digital endorsements work differently from TV ads:

- Promo codes and affiliate links track exactly how many sales each influencer brings in.
- Sponsored posts appear right in fans' feeds, in the influencer's own voice.
- Takeovers let an influencer run a brand's account for a day.

Macro vs. micro:

- A macro-influencer has a huge following (hundreds of thousands to millions). They reach a lot of people, cost a lot, and their audience may not care about a local product.
- A micro-influencer has a smaller following (often a few thousand to tens of thousands), but their followers are closely connected and highly engaged. For a local business, a respected local figure can bring a better return than a celebrity.

The right fit beats the biggest number. A small, loyal audience that matches your customers is worth more than a huge audience that doesn't.

It's the law: the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) requires sponsored posts to be clearly labeled, with #ad or #sponsored placed where people will see it, not buried at the end of a long caption. Hiding a paid endorsement misleads followers.

## Key terms

- Endorsement: a well-known person publicly supporting a product or brand.
- NIL (name, image and likeness): an athlete's right to earn money from their name, picture and identity.
- Influencer: a person whose online following can affect what their followers buy.
- Macro-influencer: an influencer with a very large following.
- Micro-influencer: an influencer with a smaller, highly engaged following.
- Call to action: the part of a message that tells people exactly what to do next.
- FTC disclosure: the label (#ad or #sponsored) the law requires on a paid post.

## Example

A short sample for a made-up person, Coach Dana Reyes, a popular Richmond youth soccer coach with about 8,000 local followers.

- The match: her followers are local soccer families, the same people who buy team gear and go to school games.
- The post: a photo of her team in custom Cavalier-style rally towels, with the caption "My crew is ready for the playoffs! These towels were designed and printed by Clover Hill students. Use code COACHDANA at the Cavalier Shop for $2 off. #ad"
- Measure it: count how many times the code COACHDANA is used, and compare towel sales that week with the week before.
