## Prompt

Assignment: The Richmond Festival Blueprint

You are the Lead Event Director. Richmond needs a new signature event for next year, and you have this week to plan it. Answer every question specifically: "A field" is not a location, and "people" is not a crowd.

Phase 1: The Vision

- Name Your Event: give it a catchy name.
- The Theme: what is it (music, food, sports, gaming, art)?
- The Crowd: who exactly are you trying to attract (for example, "high school students who love rap" or "families with kids under 10")?
- The Vibe: why would someone pay to come? Describe the experience.

Phase 2: The Logistics

- Location: where in Richmond will you hold it, and why there? Consider parking, size and noise.
- The Team: list 5 specific jobs you need to hire for (for example, security guard, audio tech), and what each one does.
- Risk Management: name one thing that could go wrong (a rainstorm, a power outage) and explain exactly how you'll prepare for it.

Phase 3: The Business

- Money In: how will the event make money (tickets, food sales, sponsors)?
- Money Out: what will cost the most (talent, venue rental, insurance, security)?
- The Pitch: write a 3-sentence social media post announcing that tickets are on sale. Include a hashtag.

Your event must follow the CCPS Student Code of Conduct (no events centered on restricted substances or unsafe activities).

Use these terms accurately: target market, logistics, risk management, revenue, expenses, sponsor.

## Background

Richmond already has signature events, like the Richmond Folk Festival and Dominion Energy Riverrock, that bring in thousands of visitors every year. Cities want events like these because visitors spend money at hotels, restaurants and shops, which creates jobs and tax revenue. That's why event directors who can pull one off are in demand.

Anyone can come up with a fun party idea. An event director has to figure out how to pay for it and keep everyone safe. Event planning usually happens in three phases.

Phase 1, the vision: what the event is and who it's for. The more specific the crowd, the easier every other decision gets. A festival for families with young kids needs shade, bathrooms, strollers and an early end time. A festival for college students needs late-night music and food trucks.

Phase 2, the logistics: the practical details of making it happen.

- Location: big enough for your crowd, with parking or bus access, and somewhere loud music won't break noise rules.
- Personnel: the staff you need. Security, ticket scanners, audio and lighting techs, vendor coordinators, first aid, cleanup crews.
- Risk management: planning for what could go wrong before it does. A rain plan (tents, a backup date, a refund policy), a power backup (generators), and a first-aid station and emergency exits.

Phase 3, the business: the money.

- Money in (revenue): ticket sales, food and drink sales, merchandise, sponsors who pay to have their names on the event, and vendor fees from food trucks that pay for a spot.
- Money out (expenses): the performers (often the biggest cost), venue rental, staging and sound, security, insurance, permits and marketing.
- If money out is bigger than money in, the event loses money.

## Key terms

- Signature event: an event a city becomes known for, which draws visitors year after year.
- Target market: the specific group of people an event is designed to attract.
- Logistics: the practical details of running an event: location, staff, equipment and schedule.
- Risk management: identifying what could go wrong and planning how to prevent it or handle it.
- Revenue: money coming in.
- Expenses: money going out.
- Sponsor: a company that pays to have its name connected to an event.
- Vendor: a business that sells at an event, like a food truck.

## Example

A short sample, for a different event than yours should be.

- Name and theme: "RVA Retro Arcade Night," a vintage video game festival.
- The crowd: adults ages 25–40 who grew up with 1990s video games.
- Location: a warehouse event space in Scott's Addition, because it's large, has nearby parking, and is surrounded by restaurants.
- Risk management: a power outage would shut down every game, so we'll rent two backup generators and test them the day before.
- Money in: $25 tickets, a local credit union as the main sponsor, and $150 per food truck spot. Money out: game rentals and the venue cost the most.
- The pitch: "Grab your quarters, RVA. Retro Arcade Night is coming to Scott's Addition with 100 classic games. Tickets are on sale now! #RVARetroNight"
