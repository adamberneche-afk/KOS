## Prompt

Assignment: The Sales Management Playbook

You've just been promoted to director of ticket sales for the River City Blades minor-league hockey team. Your team needs to sell $60,000 in ticket plans next month. The average plan costs $750. You manage four sales reps. Here's how many plans each sold last month:

- Jordan: 24 plans
- Sam: 19 plans
- Alex: 15 plans
- Taylor (started two weeks ago): 6 plans

Part 1: The Quota. How many plans does the team need to sell to reach $60,000? Set a quota for each rep, and show your math. Explain why each quota is challenging but possible. (You don't have to give everyone the same number.)

Part 2: The Sales Contest. Next month is usually slow. Design a contest to motivate the team: how reps win, what the prize is, and why it will work. Include at least one prize that isn't money.

Part 3: The Ethics Policy. Write a three-rule code of ethics for your team, and explain the problem each rule prevents.

Part 4: Support a Struggling Rep. Alex is behind on quota again this month. Write a short paragraph explaining two support activities you'll provide to help Alex succeed.

Use these terms accurately: sales quota, commission, sales motivation, sales training, sales support, consultative selling, ethics.

## Background

Being great at selling and being great at managing salespeople are different jobs. A sales manager doesn't just say "sell more." They set fair goals, train the team, keep them motivated, give them the tools they need, and make sure they sell honestly.

Setting a quota is math, plus judgment. A quota is the sales target each rep is expected to reach.

- Start with the goal: total revenue needed ÷ average sale = total sales needed.
- Then divide the work, considering each rep's experience and track record.
- Too high, and reps get discouraged and quit. Too low, and the team misses its goal. The best quotas are challenging but reachable.

For example: a team needs $100,000 and the average plan is $1,000, so it needs 100 plans. With 5 reps, that's about 20 plans each.

Keeping a team motivated:

- Financial rewards: commission (a percentage of each sale) and bonuses for hitting goals.
- Non-financial rewards: recognition ("Salesperson of the Month"), a reserved parking spot, a team lunch, a day off.
- Contests can make a slow month fun, but they should reward the whole team as well as the top seller, so no one gives up.

Training and support help reps succeed:

- Sales training: teaching product knowledge (the arena map, pricing, packages) and how to handle common objections.
- Sales support: giving reps good leads (like past ticket buyers to call), marketing materials, CRM software and practice role-plays with a manager.

Selling ethically: the stereotype of a pushy, dishonest salesperson comes from high-pressure selling. Consultative selling is the opposite: the rep learns what the fan wants and recommends a plan that fits their budget. Unethical tactics break trust, can break the law, and lose repeat customers. Examples of what not to do:

- Hiding taxes and fees until the last moment
- Promising things you can't guarantee, like a player meet-and-greet
- Misrepresenting what a seat's view is like
- Pressuring someone with fake deadlines ("This price ends in 10 minutes!")

## Key terms

- Sales quota: the sales target a rep is expected to reach in a set time.
- Commission: pay based on a percentage of each sale.
- Sales motivation: the rewards and recognition that encourage reps to perform.
- Sales training: teaching reps the product knowledge and skills to sell.
- Sales support: the tools and help a company gives reps, like leads and marketing materials.
- Consultative selling: selling by understanding the customer's needs and recommending what fits.
- Ethics: rules about what's right and fair in how a business treats people.

## Example

A sample ethics rule.

"Rule 1: Tell every customer the full price, including all taxes and fees, before taking any payment. This prevents customers from feeling tricked at checkout, which leads to angry calls, refund requests and fans who never buy from us again."
