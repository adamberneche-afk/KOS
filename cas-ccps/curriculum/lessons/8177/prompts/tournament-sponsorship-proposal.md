## Prompt

Assignment: The Sponsorship Letter

Our tournament needs sponsors. Find one real local business whose customers match our tournament's crowd, and write a professional letter asking it to sponsor the event. This is a real letter: the best ones will be signed and mailed to the businesses by Mr. Berneche.

Use the tournament details your teacher gives you (name, date, expected attendance, and what a sponsor receives).

Part 1: Research. Name one real business in the Richmond or Chesterfield area. Find the name and title of the person who decides on sponsorships (the owner, general manager or marketing director) and the business's mailing address. Explain in two or three sentences why this business's customers are the same people who will be at our tournament.

Part 2: Write the Letter. Write a one-page formal business letter with these four paragraphs:

- The Hook: introduce Clover Hill High School, our program and the tournament.
- The Connection: explain why this business is a perfect fit for our audience.
- The Value: say exactly what the sponsor gets (for example, its logo on the event t-shirt, a banner in the gym, an announcement over the PA system).
- The Call to Action: thank them and say that Mr. Berneche will follow up next week.

Part 3: Proofread. Read it out loud, check every name and address, and fix every error. Letters with slang or mistakes won't be mailed.

Never collect money or agree to anything with a business yourself. All sponsorship money goes through Mr. Berneche and the school bookkeeper.

Use these terms accurately: sponsorship, donation, target market, value proposition, call to action.

## Background

Businesses don't sponsor events just to be nice. A donation is a gift: the business gives money and expects nothing back. A sponsorship is a deal: the business pays to get its name in front of a specific crowd. When you ask for a sponsorship, you're not asking for a favor. You're offering a marketing opportunity.

Who wants to reach our crowd? If a tournament brings 500 teenagers and their parents into a gym, the businesses that want to reach those people include driving schools, sporting goods stores, pizza and fast-casual restaurants, orthodontists, phone stores and car dealerships. Match the business to the crowd.

The value proposition is the answer to the sponsor's question: "What do I get for my money?" Be specific: how many people will see their logo, where and how often.

Why a real name matters: a letter addressed "To Whom It May Concern" usually ends up in the trash. A letter addressed to the actual owner or marketing director gets read. Check the business's website, its "About" or "Contact" page, or LinkedIn.

Formal business letter format, top to bottom:

- Our address (the school's), then the date
- The business contact's name, title, business name and address
- Greeting: "Dear Ms. Lopez:" (use their last name and a colon)
- The four paragraphs, single-spaced, with a blank line between them
- Closing: "Sincerely," then space for a signature, then the name

## Key terms

- Sponsorship: a deal in which a business pays to connect its name to an event and reach its audience.
- Donation: a gift of money given without expecting anything in return.
- Target market: the specific group of people a business wants as customers.
- Value proposition: a clear statement of what the sponsor gets for its money.
- Call to action: the part of a message that tells the reader what happens next.
- Philanthropy: giving money to help a cause, without expecting a business benefit.

## Example

The connection and value paragraphs from a sample letter to an invented business.

"Our tournament will bring more than 500 Chesterfield high school students and their families to Clover Hill High School over two days. Many of these students are 15 and 16 years old and are about to get their learner's permits, which makes them exactly the customers Rapid Road Driving School serves.

As our Gold Sponsor, Rapid Road Driving School would have its logo printed on the back of every tournament t-shirt worn by all 120 players, a banner displayed in the gym for the entire event, and a thank-you announcement over the PA system before every game."
