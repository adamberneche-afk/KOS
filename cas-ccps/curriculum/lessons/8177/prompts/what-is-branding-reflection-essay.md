## Prompt

Assignment: What Is Branding?

Imagine you are the teacher. Write an essay that explains branding to someone who has never taken a marketing class: a younger sibling, a grandparent, a new student. Use what you have learned and made in this class as your examples.

Part 1: Plan It. Before you write, list three projects or examples from this class that taught you something about branding (for example, your Brand Board, the Cavalier Shop company description, the 4 Ps signature item, or your franchise scouting report). Next to each, write one sentence about what it taught you.

Part 2: Write It. Write an essay of at least 500 words that:

- opens with a clear, simple definition of branding in your own words
- uses at least three examples, each in its own paragraph, to show branding in action
- explains any marketing word you use in plain language, the way a teacher would
- ends by explaining why branding matters to a business, and to you

Part 3: Check It. Read your essay as if you were the beginner. Underline any sentence a beginner wouldn't understand, then rewrite it.

This must be your own writing. An essay written by an AI tool violates the CHHS Academic Integrity policy and earns a zero.

Use these terms accurately, and explain each one: brand, brand identity, logo, target market, brand loyalty.

## Background

You have been working with branding all quarter, even when the assignment didn't say "branding." The Brand Board was about how a brand looks. The company description was about what makes a brand different. The 4 Ps were about how a brand shows up in what it sells, what it charges, where it sells and how it promotes. This essay is where you pull it all together.

Explaining something simply is harder than explaining it with big words. If you can teach it to a beginner, you really understand it.

How to turn marketing words into plain language:

- Instead of "target demographic," say "the people most likely to buy it."
- Instead of "brand identity," say "the look a brand always uses: its logo, colors and fonts."
- Instead of "brand loyalty," say "when people keep choosing one brand over others, even when they could buy something cheaper."
- Instead of "perceived value," say "how much people think something is worth."

A simple essay plan:

- Introduction: hook the reader with a question or a familiar brand, then give your definition of branding.
- Body paragraph 1: your first example. What did you make or study, and what does it show about branding?
- Body paragraph 2: your second example.
- Body paragraph 3: your third example.
- Conclusion: why branding matters, to businesses and to the people who buy from them.

How to reach 500 words the right way: add details, not filler. For each example, say what you did, what choices you made, why you made them, and what you learned. Don't repeat the same point in different words.

## Key terms

- Brand: the reputation and feeling people connect with a business, built by everything it does the same way, every time.
- Brand identity: the visual parts of a brand: logo, colors, fonts and images.
- Logo: the symbol or design that identifies a brand.
- Target market: the specific group of people a business is trying to reach.
- Brand loyalty: when customers keep choosing the same brand over its competitors.
- Unique selling proposition (USP): the one thing a business offers that its competitors don't.

## Example

One sample body paragraph, using an invented pizza shop rather than a class project.

"Think about your favorite local pizza place. Maybe it's called Tony's, and every box has the same red-and-white checkered pattern. You could spot that box from across a parking lot. That pattern is part of Tony's brand identity, the look it always uses so you recognize it instantly. But the brand is more than the box. If Tony's pizza is always hot, the staff always remembers your order, and they sponsor your little brother's soccer team, you start to feel like Tony's is your place. That feeling is the real brand. It's why you drive past two other pizza shops to get there. Marketers call that brand loyalty: choosing the same business again and again because of how it makes you feel."
