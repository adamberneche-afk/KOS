# **Lesson Plan: Secure the Perimeter (Venue Security & Emergency Preparedness)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

The most brilliant marketing campaign and the most profitable pricing strategy mean absolutely nothing if an event is unsafe. In this lesson, students will shift from the role of Promoters to Directors of Security. They will learn the fundamentals of crowd control, emergency preparedness, and the integration of security personnel and technology. The lesson culminates in a "Tabletop Exercise" (TTX) where students must react to a simulated crisis at a major live event.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 87:** Explain the nature of venue/event security (Understanding the overarching goal of protecting life, property, and brand reputation).
  - **Competency 88:** Describe emergency preparedness procedures (Developing evacuation, shelter-in-place, and medical emergency protocols).
  - **Competency 89:** Explain the importance of crowd control (Managing ingress/egress, preventing crowd crush, and directing foot traffic).
  - **Competency 90:** Explain the use of security personnel (Differentiating between uniform police, private event staff, and specialized crowd managers).
  - **Competency 91:** Explain the use of technology in venue security (Analyzing the use of metal detectors, biometric scanners, CCTV, and communication radios).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Identify the primary physical and environmental threats to a large-scale entertainment event.
2.  Differentiate between the roles of technology (CCTV, wands) and human personnel in a comprehensive security plan.
3.  Design a basic egress and crowd control map to prevent bottlenecks and crowd surges.
4.  Formulate an Emergency Action Plan (EAP) response during a simulated crisis scenario.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook: When Things Go Wrong (15 Minutes)**

  - **Case Study Analysis:** Discuss historical examples of event management failures and successes (e.g., severe weather evacuations at outdoor music festivals, or the flawless emergency medical response when an athlete collapses on the field).
  - **Discussion:** *What is the financial and reputational cost to a venue if they fail to keep their patrons safe?*

### **B. The Security Matrix: Tech vs. Personnel (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction:** Break down how modern stadiums protect fans.

  - *Technology (Comp 91):* Clear bag policies, magnetometers (metal detectors), facial recognition, and ticketing barcodes.
  - *Personnel (Comp 90):* Why do venues hire friendly "Guest Services" in bright yellow polos to manage crowds, but use armed local Police Officers at the gates? (De-escalation vs. Enforcement).

### **C. The Tabletop Exercise (TTX) Lab (60 Minutes)**

  - **Group Work (Comp 88 & 89):** Divide students into "Venue Management Teams." Hand them a blueprint of the CHHS Football Stadium or Gymnasium during a sold-out rivalry game.
  - **The Injection:** Present a sudden crisis scenario (e.g., "A severe lightning storm has just touched down, and the game is delayed. You have 3,000 fans in the metal bleachers. Go.").
  - **The Task:** Teams must map out their immediate crowd control response, evacuation routes, and staff communication protocol.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Emergency Action Plan (EAP)**

Each team must submit a 1-page **Incident Response EAP** based on their tabletop exercise. It must include:

1.  **Crowd Flow Map:** A visually annotated map showing the safest routes for crowd egress (evacuation) without causing a bottleneck at the exits.
2.  **Personnel Deployment:** Where are you sending your security guards vs. your guest services staff during the crisis?
3.  **The PA Announcement:** A written, 3-sentence script that the Public Address announcer will read to the crowd to keep them calm and direct them to safety.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **CCPS Safety Protocols:** This is the perfect time to connect classroom learning to the real world. Relate event emergency preparedness to the CHHS fire drills, lockdown procedures, and tornado drills they practice every year. Explain that the school is simply a "venue" and the administration is executing an EAP.
  - **Core Values (Accountability):** Emphasize that in the event industry, security is not a joke. Taking the tabletop exercise seriously reflects the CHHS Core Value of Accountability.
