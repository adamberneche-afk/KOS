# **Lesson Plan: Survey Results Analysis & Management Decisions**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

After successfully collecting a statistically significant sample size of data from the Clover Hill High School student body, students will transition from researchers to venue managers. In this lesson, students will analyze the raw survey data to identify trends, extract three key data points, and compose a 250-word executive summary. Most importantly, they will use this data to justify a concrete suggestion for the physical organization or daily operation of The Cavalier Shop.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 2:** Demonstrate critical thinking and problem solving (Synthesizing raw data into actionable insights).
  - **Competency 21:** Demonstrate reading and writing skills (Composing a professional 250-word analytical summary).
  - **Competency 43:** Analyze market research data (Reviewing the survey responses to identify trends and key metrics).
  - **Competency 44:** Describe the use of research to make management decisions (Using the data to justify an operational change).
  - **Competency 85:** Explain the components of venue management (Applying data to make suggestions about the store's physical organization or daily operations).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Interpret quantitative and qualitative data from a market research survey.
2.  Extract and explain three statistically significant data points.
3.  Formulate a strategic venue management recommendation based strictly on data rather than personal preference.
4.  Compose a clear, professional 250-word executive analysis citing specific metrics.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The "Data Dump" (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction:** Display the compiled Google Form results on the board (pie charts, bar graphs, and common qualitative themes).
  - **Discussion:** "Numbers don't lie, but they don't speak for themselves either." Discuss the difference between *seeing* a data point (e.g., "70% said X") and *understanding* what it means for the business.

### **B. Finding the Story in the Data (15 Minutes)**

  - **Guided Practice (Comp 43 & 44):** Walk through an example. If 80% of respondents said they get their news from Instagram, and only 10% from Morning Announcements, what management decision should we make about our promotional budget and time?
  - **Operational Impact (Comp 85):** How do qualitative responses (like "the store is too crowded") translate into operational changes (like moving the register or limiting capacity)?

### **C. The Executive Analysis Lab (45 Minutes)**

  - **Independent Work:** Students are given access to the raw data output.
  - Students draft their 250-word analysis, ensuring they follow the required deliverables.
  - *Teacher circulates to assist with proper data citation (e.g., teaching them to write "According to the survey, 65% of juniors prefer..." instead of "A lot of people want...").*

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Data Analysis Report**

Each student will submit an executive summary of the market research. The submission **must** include:

1.  **Length:** A minimum of 250 words.
2.  **Three Data Points:** Clear identification and analysis of three distinct, interesting data points from the survey results (can be a mix of quantitative metrics and qualitative feedback).
3.  **Operational Suggestion:** One specific, actionable suggestion for how The Cavalier Shop should be organized physically or operated daily.
4.  **Data Citation:** The operational suggestion must be directly justified by citing one or more of the survey data points.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Academic Integrity (CHHS Core Values):** Students must analyze the actual data provided. Fabricating statistics to support a pre-existing idea or personal preference is an academic integrity violation.
  - **Professional Communication:** The analysis must be written in a professional, business-appropriate tone, suitable for presenting to school administration or a potential investor.
