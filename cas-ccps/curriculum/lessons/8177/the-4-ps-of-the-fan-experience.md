# **Lesson Plan: The 4 Ps of the Fan Experience (Marketing Mix)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

The "Marketing Mix" (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) is the foundation of all marketing strategies. In the sports and entertainment industry, these four elements must work in perfect harmony to create a memorable fan experience. In this lesson, students will learn the definitions of the 4 Ps and work collaboratively in small groups to design and pitch a brand-new, unique concession item or merchandise product for a local sports team or venue.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 10:** Collaborate with team members (Working in small groups to develop a cohesive pitch).
  - **Competency 42:** Explain the concept of the marketing mix (Understanding how the 4 Ps interrelate).
  - **Competency 46:** Explain the nature and scope of product/service management (Designing the physical item and its packaging).
  - **Competency 56:** Explain the nature and scope of distribution/place (Determining where in the venue or online the product will be sold).
  - **Competency 63:** Explain the role of promotion as a marketing function (Designing the advertising strategy to launch the product).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Define the 4 Ps of the Marketing Mix: Product, Price, Place, and Promotion.
2.  Analyze how altering one "P" (e.g., raising the price) affects the other three "Ps".
3.  Collaborate with peers to develop a comprehensive marketing strategy for a new sports/entertainment product.
4.  Deliver a persuasive "pitch" explaining their marketing mix strategy to the class.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook: The $100 Bottle of Water (10 Minutes)**

  - **Discussion:** Hold up a standard plastic bottle of water. Ask the class: *"How much does this cost at Food Lion?"* (About $0.20). *"How much does this cost at a music festival or an NFL game?"* ($5.00+). *"How could I market this exact same water so that someone would willingly pay $100 for it?"*
  - **Transition:** Explain that the water (Product) hasn't changed, but by manipulating the Place, Price, and Promotion, the perceived value skyrockets.

### **B. Defining the 4 Ps (20 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction:** Provide clear, sports-industry examples for each element:

<!-- end list -->

  - **Product:** Not just the game itself, but the merchandise, the food, the halftime show.
  - **Price:** Ticket tiers, premium seating, value meals. (Tie back to the previous Dynamic Pricing lesson).
  - **Place (Distribution):** Selling tickets on Ticketmaster vs. the Box Office; selling merch in the stadium vs. online.
  - **Promotion:** Bobblehead giveaway nights, social media hype, TV commercials.

### **C. Collaborative Lab: "The Signature Item" (60 Minutes)**

  - **Group Work (Competency 10):** Divide students into "Marketing Teams" of 3-4.
  - **The Task:** Teams must invent a "Signature Item" for a local venue (e.g., a crazy new hot dog for the Richmond Flying Squirrels, or an exclusive VIP merch bundle for a concert at The National). They must map out the entire Marketing Mix for this item.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The 4 Ps Pitch Deck**

Each team will present a 4-slide presentation (Google Slides) or a Canva Poster that clearly defines their strategy:

1.  **Product:** A visual mockup and description of the item. What makes it unique to the team/venue?
2.  **Price:** The retail price, the estimated cost to make it, and the profit margin.
3.  **Place:** Exactly where fans can buy it (e.g., "Only available at Section 112," creating scarcity, or "Available exclusively on the team app").
4.  **Promotion:** One specific promotional tactic to launch the product (e.g., an influencer unboxing video, an in-stadium jumbotron game).

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Cavalier Expectations (Empathy & Respect):** Because this is a group project, emphasize the CHHS Cavalier Expectations: *"Valuing the background, experiences, and classroom contributions of others,"* and *"Listening to learn when others are speaking."* \* **Accountability:** To ensure equal participation, each team member must be responsible for presenting at least one "P" during the final pitch.
