# **Lesson Plan: The Back Room (Distribution & Inventory Management)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

In the event management industry, a brilliant marketing campaign means nothing if the physical product isn't available when the fans arrive. In this lesson, students will look behind the curtain of retail and venue operations to understand the "Supply Chain." They will learn how merchandise travels through channels of distribution, the specific technologies used to track it, and the rigorous "Receiving" process required to prevent shrinkage and loss in The Cavalier Shop.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 57:** Explain the nature and scope of channel management (Understanding the flow of goods from manufacturer to consumer).
  - **Competency 58:** Explain the nature of channels of distribution (Direct vs. Indirect channels, wholesalers, and retailers).
  - **Competency 59:** Describe the use of technology in the channel management function (Barcodes, RFID tags, and automated reordering).
  - **Competency 60:** Explain the receiving process (Checking packing slips against Purchase Orders).
  - **Competency 61:** Explain equipment used in receiving (Hand trucks, pallet jacks, box cutters, and scanning guns).
  - **Competency 62:** Explain the nature of inventory control systems (Point of Sale software and physical stock counts).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Map the channel of distribution for a piece of CHHS sports merchandise (from the textile factory to the fan).
2.  Differentiate between a Purchase Order (what we asked for) and a Packing Slip (what actually arrived).
3.  Execute a simulated "Receiving" process, identifying discrepancies such as damaged goods or missing items.
4.  Explain how a Point of Sale (POS) system acts as a real-time inventory control system to prevent retail "shrink."

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook: The "Sold Out" Nightmare (10 Minutes)**

  - **Discussion:** Imagine it's the 3rd quarter of the Homecoming Game. The concession stand completely runs out of bottled water. *Whose fault is it?* \* **Concept:** Introduce the "Supply Chain." It might be the supplier's fault (Distribution), or the manager's fault for not ordering enough (Inventory Control).

### **B. Channels & Technology (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 57, 58, 59):** Map out the journey of a Cavalier hoodie on the whiteboard:

<!-- end list -->

  - *Manufacturer (Factory) -\> Wholesaler/Custom Printer -\> Retailer (Cavalier Shop) -\> Consumer (Fan).*

<!-- end list -->

  - **The Tech:** Explain how we don't count everything by hand anymore. Discuss UPC Barcodes, QR codes, and RFID (Radio Frequency Identification) tags used by massive venues (like NFL stadiums) to track thousands of jerseys instantly.

### **C. Operations: The Receiving Process (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 60, 61, 62):** Walk students through the back room of the Cavalier Shop (or use photos).
  - **The Tools:** Discuss the safe use of receiving equipment (box cutters, dollies) and digital tools (inventory scanning guns).
  - **The Golden Rule of Receiving:** *Never sign the delivery driver's paperwork until you have opened the box and verified the Packing Slip matches the actual physical items.*

### **D. The "Broken Pallet" Simulation Lab (50 Minutes)**

  - **Group Work:** Divide the class into "Receiving Teams." Hand each team a sealed box (can be filled with random classroom supplies, old t-shirts, etc.).
  - **The Scenario:** They are handed a "Purchase Order" (what Mr. Berneche ordered) and a "Packing Slip" (taped to the outside of the box).
  - **The Task:** Teams must physically unbox the items, inspect them for "damage," and count them. They will inevitably find that the box is short by two items, and one item is the wrong color.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Receiving Reconciliation Report**

Students will submit a formal operations report containing:

1.  **The Discrepancy Log:** A detailed list noting exactly where the Packing Slip failed to match the Purchase Order.
2.  **The Inventory Update:** A data-entry exercise where students input the *actual* received viable quantities into a mock digital inventory spreadsheet (Google Sheets), demonstrating their understanding of digital inventory control.
3.  **The Supplier Email:** A professionally written, draft email to the "vendor" explaining the shortage and requesting a credit or replacement for the missing/damaged goods.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Workplace Safety (OSHA/School Guidelines):** When discussing receiving equipment (Comp 61), emphasize safety. Students are strictly prohibited from using sharp tools (like box cutters) or heavy lifting equipment without direct, hands-on faculty supervision. Emphasize proper lifting techniques (lift with the knees).
  - **Integrity (Handling Merchandise):** Emphasize that "shrink" (stolen or lost inventory) often happens in the back room before items even hit the sales floor. Handling inventory requires absolute honesty and accountability to the CHHS student body whose funds purchased the goods.
