# **Lesson Plan: The Battle for the Entertainment Dollar (Consumer Motivation & Competition)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

Why do fans spend $150 to freeze in a stadium when they could watch the same game in high definition from their warm living room for free? In this lesson, students will explore the psychological and social factors that motivate consumers to attend live events. Furthermore, students will analyze "Competitive Factors"—understanding that a sports franchise isn't just competing against other teams; they are competing against movies, theme parks, restaurants, and streaming services for a family's limited discretionary income.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 2:** Demonstrate critical thinking and problem solving (Analyzing complex consumer behaviors).
  - **Competency 93:** Describe factors that motivate consumers to attend sports, entertainment, and event occasions (Exploring FOMO, socialization, status, and family bonding).
  - **Competency 94:** Explain competitive factors in attendance (Evaluating direct vs. indirect competition for discretionary income).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Define "Discretionary Income" and explain its importance to the entertainment industry.
2.  Identify the top 5 psychological/social motivators for event attendance (Socialization, Entertainment, Family Bonding, Status/Corporate, and FOMO).
3.  Distinguish between direct competitors (two concerts on the same night) and indirect competitors (a baseball game vs. Topgolf).
4.  Profile a specific "Consumer Persona" and develop a strategy to win their entertainment dollar.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook: The Couch vs. The Stadium (10 Minutes)**

  - **Discussion:** Project a picture of a massive 85-inch 4K TV next to a picture of the "nosebleed" section of a stadium. Ask the class: *"If the TV view is better, the food is cheaper, and there's no traffic, why do 70,000 people still choose to go to the stadium?"*
  - **The Reveal:** Introduce the concept that the sports/event industry doesn't sell a "game"; it sells an *experience* and a *memory*.

### **B. Motivators & Competitors (20 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 93):** Break down the fan motivators.

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 94):** Discuss the "Entertainment Dollar." If a family in Chesterfield has a $200 entertainment budget for the month, how does the Richmond Flying Squirrels organization convince them to spend it at the diamond instead of at Kings Dominion or the movie theater?

### **C. The Persona Profile Lab (60 Minutes)**

  - **Small Group Work:** Divide students into groups and hand each a "Consumer Persona Card" (e.g., A family of four with young kids, a 25-year-old single professional, a retired couple).
  - **The Task:** Groups must analyze what motivates their specific persona, identify the biggest local competitor for their time/money, and create a targeted strategy to get them to attend a specific local event (e.g., a VCU basketball game or a concert at the Altria Theater).

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Fan Persona Pitch**

Each group will submit a 1-page visual **Fan Persona Profile** (via Canva or Google Slides) that includes:

1.  **The Demographic:** A brief description of their assigned consumer and their estimated discretionary budget.
2.  **The Primary Motivator:** Why would this person want to leave their house? (e.g., "To exhaust their toddlers," or "To post on Instagram").
3.  **The Chief Competitor:** Name one specific, local *indirect* competitor that might steal this consumer's money on a Friday night (e.g., Dave & Buster's, Richmond Raceway).
4.  **The Winning Strategy:** One specific venue amenity or promotion you would offer to guarantee they choose your event over the competitor (e.g., a "Kids Run the Bases" post-game event).

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Cavalier Core Values (Empathy & Respect):** This project requires students to step outside their own teenage perspective and empathize with different demographics (like parents or corporate executives). Emphasize that great marketers must have the *Empathy* to understand what people different from themselves value.
  - **Accountability:** Group roles must be clearly defined so that every student is accountable for a specific section of the final Persona Profile presentation.
