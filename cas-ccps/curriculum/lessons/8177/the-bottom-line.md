# **Lesson Plan: The Bottom Line (Event Budgeting & Finance)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

An event can have brilliant marketing, a perfect venue, and massive crowd demand, but if the expenses exceed the revenue, the event is a failure. In this lesson, students will transition into the role of a Chief Financial Officer (CFO). They will learn how to read basic financial statements, differentiate between fixed and variable costs, and build a comprehensive line-item budget for a mid-sized sporting event.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 16:** Demonstrate information security (Understanding the confidentiality of vendor contracts, payroll, and corporate financial statements).
  - **Competency 99:** Explain the nature of budgets (Understanding the purpose of projecting income and capping expenses).
  - **Competency 100:** Explain the use of financial statements (Reading a basic Profit & Loss / Income Statement).
  - **Competency 101:** Prepare a budget for an event (Allocating funds for marketing, venue rental, security, and talent).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Differentiate between fixed costs (e.g., arena rental) and variable costs (e.g., concession food inventory).
2.  Analyze a basic Profit & Loss (P\&L) statement to determine if an event operated in the red or the black.
3.  Understand the ethical and legal requirements of keeping financial data (like sponsor contracts and employee pay) secure and confidential.
4.  Prepare a balanced, line-item budget for a hypothetical $10,000 local sporting event.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook: The Fyre Festival Disaster (15 Minutes)**

  - **Case Study:** Briefly discuss the infamous 2017 Fyre Festival. While the *marketing* (influencers, models, hype) was an A+, the *budgeting* and financial reality were an F.
  - **Discussion:** What happens when an event organizer promises a $5,000-per-ticket luxury experience but runs out of money to pay for tents, food, and musical acts? (Bankruptcy, fraud charges, and ruined brand reputation).

### **B. Financial Statements 101 (20 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 99 & 100):** Display a simplified Event Income Statement on the board.

<!-- end list -->

  - *Top Line (Revenue):* Ticket Sales, Sponsorships, Merch, Concessions.
  - *Middle (Expenses):* Venue Rental, Security Personnel, Marketing Ads, Insurance, Talent/Referees.
  - *Bottom Line (Net Profit/Loss):* Revenue minus Expenses.

<!-- end list -->

  - **Information Security (Comp 16):** Explain that in the real world, this document is highly confidential. If a rival promoter sees your P\&L, they know exactly how much you pay your security guards and can outbid you.

### **C. The "$10,000 Tip-Off" Budget Lab (55 Minutes)**

  - **Independent or Pair Work (Comp 101):** Students are given $10,000 in "Seed Money" to host a weekend charity basketball tournament at the Clover Hill High School gymnasium.
  - **The Task:** Using Google Sheets or Microsoft Excel, students must build a zero-based budget. They are provided a "Vendor Price List" (e.g., Referees cost $50/hour, CHHS Custodians cost $30/hour, T-shirts cost $8 to print). They must project their expected revenue (from team registration fees and ticket sales) and allocate their expenses so that they generate at least a 15% profit margin for the charity.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Financial Packet**

Students will submit a final **Financial Packet** containing:

1.  **The Line-Item Budget (Spreadsheet):** An Excel/Google Sheet clearly categorizing Projected Revenue and Projected Expenses. Formulas must be used to calculate the "Net Profit."
2.  **The CFO Memo:** A 1-page professional memo to the "Board of Directors" explaining where the biggest financial risks are in their budget (e.g., "If we don't sell at least 200 tickets, our variable costs for concessions will cause us to lose money").

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Technology Acceptable Use (Competency 16 Integration):** Remind students that building spreadsheets and sharing documents on CCPS Google Drives leaves a digital footprint. Discuss the importance of not sharing sensitive files with unauthorized users (a key component of Information Security).
  - **Core Values (Accountability):** Math doesn't lie. Emphasize that taking accountability for your work means double-checking your spreadsheet formulas. A single misplaced decimal point in a budget can ruin an event.
