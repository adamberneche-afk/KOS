# **Lesson Plan: The Business of the Game (Franchise Analysis)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

Students often view the sports industry strictly from a fan's perspective (wins, losses, and player stats). In this lesson, students will strip away the "game" and examine a professional sports franchise strictly as a corporate entity. Students will select one professional sports franchise and conduct an industry analysis to determine its ownership structure, primary revenue streams, community impact, and major competitors for discretionary income.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 15:** Demonstrate information-literacy skills (Researching corporate financial data and ownership records).
  - **Competency 23:** Examine aspects of an industry (Analyzing the multi-billion dollar sports/entertainment industry ecosystem).
  - **Competency 24:** Explain the role of business in society (Evaluating how a sports franchise impacts its local city's economy and community).
  - **Competency 25:** Describe types of business ownership (Identifying whether the franchise is a sole proprietorship, partnership, or corporation).
  - **Competency 26:** Explain the concept of profit (Breaking down revenue streams: media rights, gate receipts, merchandising, vs. operating expenses).
  - **Competency 27:** Explain the concept of competition (Understanding that a sports team competes not just with other teams, but with movies, concerts, and streaming services for discretionary income).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Differentiate between a sports franchise's on-field performance and its corporate financial performance.
2.  Identify the legal ownership structure of a major sports or entertainment entity.
3.  Categorize the primary revenue streams that generate profit in the sports industry.
4.  Define direct and indirect competition as it relates to consumer discretionary income.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The "Fan vs. Owner" Mindset Shift (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction:** Ask the class: *How does a sports team make money?* (Most will say "ticket sales").
  - **The Reveal:** Show a breakdown of the NFL's or NBA's revenue (highlighting that National Media/TV broadcasting rights dwarf ticket sales). Discuss how player salaries (the product) are the biggest expense.

### **B. Ownership Structures in Sports (15 Minutes)**

  - **Guided Practice:** Explain the difference between types of ownership. For example, the Green Bay Packers are community-owned (corporation with shareholders), while the Dallas Cowboys are owned by one family (sole proprietorship/LLC).

### **C. Franchise Analysis Lab (50 Minutes)**

  - **Independent Work (Competencies 23-27):** Students select a professional sports franchise or major entertainment corporation (e.g., WWE, Live Nation). They must use their Chromebooks to research the business side of the entity, focusing strictly on its corporate operations, ownership, and revenue generation.

## **5. Project Deliverables: "The Corporate Scouting Report"**

Students must produce a 1-2 page visual "Scouting Report" (using Canva, Google Docs, or Google Slides) answering the following business questions about their chosen franchise:

1.  **The Entity:** Name of the franchise/company and its current valuation (e.g., Forbes lists).
2.  **The Ownership:** Who owns it? What type of business structure is it?
3.  **The Profit Matrix:** What are their top three sources of revenue? (e.g., Local TV deals, naming rights, ticket sales).
4.  **The Society Impact:** What is one specific way this business impacts its local community (e.g., charity foundations, local job creation, tax subsidies for stadiums)?
5.  **The Competition:** Who is their biggest *indirect* competitor for their target market's entertainment money on a Friday night?

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Technology Acceptable Use Policy:** Students must use their CCPS-issued Chromebooks for researching financial data. Remind students that searching for unauthorized streaming sites or off-topic sports highlight videos violates the acceptable use policy.
  - **Citation Standards:** Because students are researching financial data and ownership records, they must cite their sources (e.g., Forbes, Sports Business Journal) to maintain Academic Integrity and practice proper information-literacy skills.
