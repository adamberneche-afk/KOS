# **Lesson Plan: The Cloud Behind the Game (Data Centers, AI & the Community)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

Every streamed game, mobile ticket, fantasy app and esports tournament runs through a data center: a building full of computers that store and move the world's data. Virginia has more data centers than anywhere else on Earth, and new ones are being proposed across the state, including around Richmond. Supporters point to tax revenue and construction jobs; opponents point to electricity, water, noise and land use, and AI is making those demands grow fast. In this lesson, students act as a community advisory panel evaluating a (made-up) data center proposal in Chesterfield County. They will trace how the sports and entertainment industry depends on data centers, weigh the pros and cons with and without AI, read news coverage for bias, and design a small business that turns the data center into an opportunity, complete with an organizational chart.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 15:** Demonstrate information-literacy skills (Fact-checking each claim in a news story against its source, and checking where a statistic comes from before repeating it).
  - **Competency 44:** Document the different roles in sports, entertainment, and event management using an organizational chart (Building an org chart for a sports or event startup at launch and as it grows).
  - **Competency 48:** Analyze the effect of niche or emerging events in the sports, entertainment, and event industries (How esports and streaming-only events depend on data centers).
  - **Competency 55:** Describe the entrepreneurial mindset (Spotting a problem the data center creates, or a need it opens up, as a business opportunity).
  - **Competency 57:** Describe how small businesses drive job creation, income generation, and economic growth (Comparing a data center's few permanent jobs with the local small businesses and jobs that grow around it).
  - **Competency 64:** Investigate how social and environmental concerns affect decisions in the sports, entertainment, and event industries (Weighing electricity, water, noise and land use, with and without AI).
  - **Competency 65:** Examine the effect of media bias on public perception and opinion (Showing that two accurate stories built from the same facts can leave opposite impressions through selection, framing, headlines and quotes).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Explain how streaming, ticketing, analytics and esports depend on data centers, and which of those uses rely on AI.
2.  Evaluate the economic, social and environmental pros and cons of a data center, and how AI changes them.
3.  Fact-check news coverage against a source, and explain how accurate stories can still be biased through selection, framing, headlines and quotes.
4.  Describe the entrepreneurial mindset and use it to design a small business connected to sports, entertainment or events.
5.  Build an organizational chart for that business and explain how small businesses create jobs.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook: Where Does the Game Go? (10 Minutes)**

  - **Discussion:** Ask: *"When you stream a game or pull up a mobile ticket, where is that data actually stored?"* Most students will say "the cloud." Show a photo of a data center campus in Loudoun County and explain: the cloud is a building like this, full of servers, fans and cooling systems.
  - **The Hook Stat:** Northern Virginia is the largest data center market in the world. You may also hear that "70% of the world's internet traffic passes through Northern Virginia." That number gets repeated constantly, but it is very hard to verify. Ask: *"Who would benefit from repeating it?"* (Preview of Comp 65 and Comp 15.)

### **B. The Industry Runs on Data (15 Minutes)**

  - **Without AI:** Streaming games (Thursday Night Football streams on Amazon Prime Video), mobile tickets, cashless concessions, fantasy apps and social media all run on ordinary data center computing.
  - **With AI:** Automated highlight clips, AI-generated commentary (IBM's AI has narrated highlights at Wimbledon), dynamic ticket pricing software, chatbots on team websites, and player-tracking analytics.
  - **Emerging Events (Comp 48):** Esports tournaments fill arenas and draw millions of online viewers, and some events exist almost entirely online. None of them work without data centers.

### **C. The Pros & Cons (20 Minutes)**

  - **The Pros:** Large local tax revenue, with little demand on schools or roads; hundreds of construction jobs while it's built; the computing power that streaming, ticketing and AI need.
  - **The Cons (Comp 64):** Huge electricity use (which can raise the need for new power plants and power lines), water for cooling, constant fan noise, diesel backup generators, and large amounts of land.
  - **The AI Factor:** AI data centers pack in more powerful chips that use more electricity and give off more heat. In 2025, the International Energy Agency projected that data centers' electricity use worldwide could roughly double by 2030, with AI the biggest driver.
  - **The Jobs Question (Comp 57):** Once built, a data center employs relatively few permanent workers. Compare that with the small businesses that grow around it: electricians, HVAC and security contractors, restaurants and suppliers. Small businesses create most new jobs in the U.S.

### **D. Fact-Check the News (20 Minutes)**

  - **Media Bias (Comp 65 & 15):** Display the two news stories from the student prompt. Every number in both stories comes from the fact sheet, so both are accurate, yet one reads like an ad and the other like a warning.
  - **The Fact-Check:** Students trace each claim in both stories back to the fact sheet and mark it true, true but missing context, or not on the fact sheet. Let them discover that everything checks out.
  - **The Discussion:** *"If both stories are true, why do they make you feel so differently?"* Pull out the techniques: which facts each story selects or leaves out, framing the same number two ways ("$25 million a year" vs. "about 2.5% of the budget"), comparisons ("more than every home in the county"), headlines, loaded words ("boost," "massive"), and quotes that are opinions rather than facts.
  - **The Rewrite:** Students write a balanced headline and three-sentence story using facts from both sides.

### **E. The Startup Lab (30 Minutes)**

  - **Entrepreneurial Mindset (Comp 55):** Entrepreneurs see problems as opportunities. Example: in England, a startup used the heat from a small data center to warm a public swimming pool. Discuss the mindset: spotting needs, taking calculated risks, resourcefulness and resilience.
  - **Org Charts (Comp 44):** Model an org chart on the board for a small sports streaming company (owner at the top; production, sales, marketing and tech below). Show how the chart changes as the business grows from 5 people to 15.
  - **Independent Work:** Students design their own startup and org chart (Part 4 of the prompt).

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Data Center Decision**

Students submit the student prompt's five parts:

1.  **Follow the Game:** How one sports or entertainment experience depends on data centers, and a sort of uses into "needs AI" and "doesn't need AI."
2.  **The Pros & Cons:** A T-chart from the fact sheet, plus how the picture changes for an AI data center.
3.  **Fact-Check the News:** A claim-by-claim fact-check of both stories, how two accurate stories create opposite impressions, and a balanced headline and story.
4.  **The Startup:** A small-business idea tied to sports, entertainment or events, the mindset behind it, an org chart at launch and in year three, and the jobs it creates.
5.  **The Recommendation:** Support, oppose or support with conditions, backed by evidence.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Respectful Debate:** Data centers are a live local issue, and some students' families may work in the industry or live near a proposed site. Keep the discussion on evidence and trade-offs, and respect differing views.
  - **Information Literacy:** The proposal's numbers are practice numbers for this lesson. Students must use the fact sheet and label any outside statistic with its source; repeating an unsourced number is an information-literacy error, not evidence.
  - **Responsible AI Use:** If students use AI tools, they follow CCPS acceptable use rules and the teacher's directions for the assignment. Their analysis and headline rewrite must be their own.
