# **Lesson Plan: The Economics of the Arena (Dynamic Pricing)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

Why does a ticket to a Tuesday night baseball game cost $15, but a ticket to see the same team on a Saturday night against their biggest rival costs $85? In this lesson, students will explore the core economic principles of scarcity, supply, and demand. Students will analyze the controversial practice of "Dynamic Ticket Pricing" used by companies like Ticketmaster and professional sports franchises, and then use mathematical modeling to determine the optimal ticket price to maximize revenue for a hypothetical event.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 2:** Demonstrate critical thinking and problem solving (Determining the "sweet spot" where price and consumer willingness to pay meet).
  - **Competency 19:** Apply mathematical skills to job-specific tasks (Calculating projected revenue using the formula: Price × Quantity = Total Revenue).
  - **Competency 28:** Explain economic principles and their impact on business (Understanding macro/micro economic concepts like supply, demand, and scarcity).
  - **Competency 45:** Explain the nature and scope of pricing (Analyzing how sports teams shift from fixed pricing to dynamic, demand-based pricing).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Define supply, demand, and scarcity in the context of venue capacity.
2.  Explain the concept of "Dynamic Pricing" and why event managers use it to maximize profit.
3.  Calculate projected gross revenue across multiple price points.
4.  Evaluate the ethical considerations of price gouging versus fair market value in the entertainment industry.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Ticketmaster Hook (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction:** Open with a highly relatable, real-world case study: The Taylor Swift *Eras Tour* or a recent major sporting event (like the Super Bowl).
  - **Discussion:** Ask students: *If a stadium holds exactly 65,000 people (Fixed Supply), but 2 million people want to go (High Demand), what happens to the price of the ticket on the secondary market?* Introduce the concept of **Scarcity**.

### **B. Fixed vs. Dynamic Pricing (20 Minutes)**

  - **Guided Practice:** Explain the traditional "Fixed Pricing" model (e.g., all lower-bowl tickets are $50 all season long).
  - **The Pivot:** Introduce "Dynamic Pricing" algorithms. Explain how teams now change prices daily based on the weather, the opposing team's record, or whether a superstar player is playing.

### **C. The Revenue Math Lab (55 Minutes)**

  - **Independent Work (Competency 19 & 28):** Distribute the "Dynamic Pricing Model" worksheet. Students act as the Director of Ticketing for a minor league hockey team. They are given different game scenarios (e.g., Opening Night, a snowy Tuesday game, a Championship playoff game) and a projected demand curve. They must calculate the mathematical revenue at different price tiers to find the most profitable ticket price for each specific game without pricing out the fans.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Pricing Strategy Brief**

Students will submit their completed Revenue Math Lab along with a short, written **Pricing Strategy Brief** justifying their math.

  - **The Calculations:** Accurate completion of the Revenue = Price × Quantity models for three different event scenarios.
  - **The Ethical Defense:** A single-paragraph defense answering the following prompt: *"Is it ethical for a sports team to charge a fan $100 for a ticket on a Saturday, but charge a different fan $20 for the exact same seat on a Tuesday? Why or why not?"*

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Core Values - Integrity & Empathy:** When discussing the ethics of pricing (Ticketmaster fees, scalping, price gouging), tie the conversation back to the CHHS Core Values. How does a business balance making a maximum profit with treating its loyal fan base with empathy and fairness?
  - **Classroom Engagement (Accountability):** The math portion of this lab requires sustained focus. Remind students of the Cavalier Expectation: *"Take ownership of my learning."* Students must attempt the calculations independently before asking for teacher intervention.
