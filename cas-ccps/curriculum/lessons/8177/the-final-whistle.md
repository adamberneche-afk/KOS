# **Lesson Plan: The Final Whistle (Evaluating Success & Career Readiness)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

Just as every major sporting event ends with a post-game press conference and a front-office evaluation, the end of the school year requires a "post-mortem" of the students' skills. In the first half of this capstone lesson, students will learn how to formally evaluate the success of an event using KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) and ROI (Return on Investment). In the second half, students will evaluate *themselves*, translating the projects they've completed this year into professional resume bullet points, exploring professional networking, and preparing for a mock job interview in the sports/entertainment sector.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 12:** Demonstrate career- and life-management skills.
  - **Competency 13:** Demonstrate continuous learning and adaptability.
  - **Competency 32:** Explain the benefits and responsibilities of membership in the student organization as a student and in professional/civic organizations as an adult (e.g., DECA, FBLA).
  - **Competency 43:** Evaluate career opportunities in sports, entertainment, and event management (Identifying at least three career paths in sports and event management beyond being an athlete).
  - **Competency 102:** Identify career and employment-related information sources (Job boards like TeamWork Online and LinkedIn, team and venue careers pages, and the Occupational Outlook Handbook).
  - **Competency 101:** (Optional) Evaluate the event’s overall success (Conducting a post-event SWOT analysis).
  - **Competency 103:** Identify career opportunities at different levels in sports, entertainment, and event management.
  - **Competency 104:** Describe education and training needed for a sports, entertainment, and event management career (Degrees, internships, game-day jobs and industry certifications).
  - **Competency 105:** (Optional) Identify barriers to employment and strategies to overcome them (Naming what could stand between a student and a first job in sports or events, like no experience, transportation or event-night schedules, and a plan for each).
  - **Competency 106:** Identify professional development opportunities in the sports, entertainment, and event industries (Building a digital presence like LinkedIn).
  - **Competency 107:** Collect information about a prospective employer (Preparing for a job interview by researching the employer).
  - **Competency 108:** Develop a career plan (Building a professional resume and portfolio).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Define and measure "Event Success" using financial data (ROI) and qualitative data (fan surveys).
2.  Identify at least three distinct career paths within the sports/event management industry, where to research them, and the education and training each requires.
3.  Translate classroom projects into professional, action-oriented resume bullet points.
4.  Develop a basic networking strategy and identify professional organizations that can accelerate career growth.
5.  Answer behavioral interview questions using the S.T.A.R. (Situation, Task, Action, Result) method.
6.  Identify barriers to getting a first job in the industry and a strategy to overcome each.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Post-Mortem: Evaluating the Event (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 101):** How do we know if an event was actually successful? It's not just "Did people have fun?" It is:

<!-- end list -->

  - *Financial:* Did we hit our profit margin?
  - *Operational:* Were there security bottlenecks?
  - *Reputational:* What are people saying on social media today?

### **B. Career Pathways & Professional Organizations (15 Minutes)**

  - **Exploration (Comp 43 & 103):** Break the myth that you have to be a professional athlete to work in sports. Highlight "Front Office" careers.
  - **Research the Job (Comp 102):** Show students where real career information lives: **TeamWork Online** (the sports industry's main job board), **LinkedIn** and **Indeed**, the careers pages of local teams and venues (Richmond Flying Squirrels, VCU Athletics, Richmond Raceway), and the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics' **Occupational Outlook Handbook** for pay, outlook and required education. Model one search live.
  - **Get the Training (Comp 104):** Most front-office jobs ask for a bachelor's degree (sports management, business, marketing or communications), but the real separator is experience: internships, part-time game-day jobs (ticket taker, usher, concessions) and volunteering at events. Some careers add industry certifications later, like the Certified Meeting Professional (CMP) for event planners or the Certified Facilities Executive (CFE) for venue managers.
  - **Organizations:** Discuss how joining organizations like DECA, FBLA, or the Public Relations Society of America (PRSA) provides a massive competitive advantage on a resume.

  - **Clearing the Path (Comp 105):** Ask: *"What could stop you from getting your first job in sports or events?"* List the answers on the board: no experience yet, no way to get there, event jobs that run nights and weekends, age limits on some roles, not knowing anyone in the industry, and internships that don't pay. Pair each one with a strategy: volunteering and school events for experience, carpools and planning a route, picking roles that fit a school schedule, DECA and informational interviews to build a network, and looking for paid internships or ones that offer school credit.

### **C. Building Your Roster: Networking (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 106):** Introduce the concept that "Your network is your net worth."
  - **Application:** Discuss the basics of setting up a professional LinkedIn profile and the concept of the "Informational Interview" to connect with local sports executives (e.g., reaching out to a manager at the Richmond Flying Squirrels).

### **D. The "Free Agent" Resume Workshop (30 Minutes)**

  - **Guided Practice (Comp 108):** Teach the formula for a strong resume bullet point: *Action Verb + Specific Task + Quantifiable Result*.
  - **Translation Exercise:** "Calculated dynamic ticket pricing models based on supply and demand, projecting a 15% increase in total game-day revenue."

### **E. The S.T.A.R. Interview Prep (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 107):** Teach the S.T.A.R. method for answering behavioral questions. Students will draft 2-3 S.T.A.R. stories based on their group projects this year.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The "Free Agent" Portfolio**

Students will submit their final capstone portfolio, which includes:

1.  **The Post-Event Evaluation:** A 1-page SWOT analysis of a hypothetical or real school event.
2.  **The Professional Resume:** A formatted, 1-page resume highlighting the hard skills learned in the 8177 course, including any professional organization memberships (like DECA).
3.  **Mock Interview S.T.A.R. Sheet:** A pre-filled worksheet detailing three specific stories they can use in an upcoming mock interview.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Core Values (Integrity):** A resume is a legal document in the professional world. Emphasize the CHHS Core Value of Integrity: students must never lie, exaggerate, or plagiarize on their resumes.
  - **Professional Attire:** If conducting live mock interviews, remind students of the CHHS dress code while also discussing what constitutes "Business Professional" vs. "Business Casual" in the modern sports workplace.
