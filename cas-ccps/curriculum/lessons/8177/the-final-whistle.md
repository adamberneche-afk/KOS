# **Lesson Plan: The Final Whistle (Evaluating Success & Career Readiness)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

Just as every major sporting event ends with a post-game press conference and a front-office evaluation, the end of the school year requires a "post-mortem" of the students' skills. In the first half of this capstone lesson, students will learn how to formally evaluate the success of an event using KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) and ROI (Return on Investment). In the second half, students will evaluate *themselves*, translating the projects they've completed this year into professional resume bullet points, exploring professional networking, and preparing for a mock job interview in the sports/entertainment sector.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 12:** Demonstrate career- and life-management skills.
  - **Competency 13:** Demonstrate continuous learning and adaptability.
  - **Competency 98:** Explain the nature of event evaluation (Conducting a post-event SWOT analysis).
  - **Competency 102:** Identify career opportunities in sports, entertainment, and event management.
  - **Competency 103:** Explain the role of professional organizations in the industry (e.g., DECA, FBLA).
  - **Competency 104:** Develop a professional resume/portfolio.
  - **Competency 105:** Prepare for a job interview.
  - **Competency 106:** Explain the importance of networking (Building a digital presence like LinkedIn).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Define and measure "Event Success" using financial data (ROI) and qualitative data (fan surveys).
2.  Identify at least three distinct career paths within the sports/event management industry.
3.  Translate classroom projects into professional, action-oriented resume bullet points.
4.  Develop a basic networking strategy and identify professional organizations that can accelerate career growth.
5.  Answer behavioral interview questions using the S.T.A.R. (Situation, Task, Action, Result) method.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Post-Mortem: Evaluating the Event (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 98):** How do we know if an event was actually successful? It's not just "Did people have fun?" It is:

<!-- end list -->

  - *Financial:* Did we hit our profit margin?
  - *Operational:* Were there security bottlenecks?
  - *Reputational:* What are people saying on social media today?

### **B. Career Pathways & Professional Organizations (15 Minutes)**

  - **Exploration (Comp 102 & 103):** Break the myth that you have to be a professional athlete to work in sports. Highlight "Front Office" careers.
  - **Organizations:** Discuss how joining organizations like DECA, FBLA, or the Public Relations Society of America (PRSA) provides a massive competitive advantage on a resume.

### **C. Building Your Roster: Networking (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 106):** Introduce the concept that "Your network is your net worth."
  - **Application:** Discuss the basics of setting up a professional LinkedIn profile and the concept of the "Informational Interview" to connect with local sports executives (e.g., reaching out to a manager at the Richmond Flying Squirrels).

### **D. The "Free Agent" Resume Workshop (30 Minutes)**

  - **Guided Practice (Comp 104):** Teach the formula for a strong resume bullet point: *Action Verb + Specific Task + Quantifiable Result*.
  - **Translation Exercise:** "Calculated dynamic ticket pricing models based on supply and demand, projecting a 15% increase in total game-day revenue."

### **E. The S.T.A.R. Interview Prep (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 105):** Teach the S.T.A.R. method for answering behavioral questions. Students will draft 2-3 S.T.A.R. stories based on their group projects this year.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The "Free Agent" Portfolio**

Students will submit their final capstone portfolio, which includes:

1.  **The Post-Event Evaluation:** A 1-page SWOT analysis of a hypothetical or real school event.
2.  **The Professional Resume:** A formatted, 1-page resume highlighting the hard skills learned in the 8177 course, including any professional organization memberships (like DECA).
3.  **Mock Interview S.T.A.R. Sheet:** A pre-filled worksheet detailing three specific stories they can use in an upcoming mock interview.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Core Values (Integrity):** A resume is a legal document in the professional world. Emphasize the CHHS Core Value of Integrity: students must never lie, exaggerate, or plagiarize on their resumes.
  - **Professional Attire:** If conducting live mock interviews, remind students of the CHHS dress code while also discussing what constitutes "Business Professional" vs. "Business Casual" in the modern sports workplace.
