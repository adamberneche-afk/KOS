# **Lesson Plan: The Franchise Evolution (Life Cycles, Bundling & Licensing)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

Every product—from a new style of athletic shoe to a mega-music festival—goes through a lifecycle: Introduction, Growth, Maturity, and Decline. But what happens when a sports franchise or entertainment brand hits the "Decline" phase? In this lesson, students will act as Brand Managers tasked with reviving a stagnating local sports property. They will learn how to inject new life and revenue into a mature brand through **Product Bundling**, **Brand Extensions**, **Licensing**, and selling **Naming Rights**.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 47:** Explain the concept of product mix (Analyzing all the different product lines a venue offers).
  - **Competency 48:** Describe the nature of product bundling (Packaging tickets, parking, and food for one price).
  - **Competency 49:** Describe the product life cycle (Introduction, Growth, Maturity, Decline).
  - **Competency 50:** Explain the nature of product extension in the sports/entertainment industry (Launching a spin-off product, like a mascot-themed cereal).
  - **Competency 51:** Describe the role of product/service branding (How a strong brand identity allows for higher profit margins).
  - **Competency 54:** Explain the role of naming rights (Selling the title of a stadium or specific venue zone to a corporate sponsor).
  - **Competency 55:** Explain the role of licensing (Renting the team logo to third-party manufacturers like Nike or EA Sports).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Chart the four stages of the Product Life Cycle (PLC).
2.  Differentiate between a *Brand Extension* (creating a new product category) and a *Product Bundle* (packaging existing products together).
3.  Explain the financial mechanics of *Licensing* (where the team gets paid royalties for letting someone else make the product).
4.  Propose a comprehensive "Brand Revival Strategy" for a mature or declining sports property.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook: The $1,000 Foam Finger (10 Minutes)**

  - **Discussion:** Hold up a picture of a generic green t-shirt and a picture of a green Philadelphia Eagles t-shirt. *Why does one cost $5 and the other cost $45?* \* **Concept (Comp 51):** Introduce the power of **Branding**. People don't buy the cotton; they buy the identity.

### **B. The Life Cycle & The "Maturity" Problem (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 49):** Draw the Product Life Cycle bell curve on the board (Intro, Growth, Maturity, Decline).
  - **The Problem:** Ask the class: "The NFL has been around for over 100 years. It's heavily in the 'Maturity' phase. Everyone who wants to watch football already does. How do they keep making *more* money every year if they can't find new fans?"

### **C. The Growth Strategies (20 Minutes)**

  - **Bundling (Comp 48):** "The Family 4-Pack" (4 tickets, 4 hot dogs, 4 sodas for $100). It guarantees concession sales before the fan even arrives.
  - **Brand Extensions (Comp 50):** The WWE creating movies; the NBA creating the WNBA; a local minor league baseball team opening a year-round restaurant.
  - **Licensing & Naming Rights (Comp 54 & 55):** Explain the difference between *Sponsorship* (paying for a banner) and *Naming Rights* (paying $20 million to call it "Capital One Arena"). Explain *Licensing*: The team doesn't make video games, but they license their logo to EA Sports for *Madden* and collect a 15% royalty on every copy sold.

### **D. The Brand Manager Lab (45 Minutes)**

  - **Group Work:** Students are given a scenario: *The Richmond Kickers (local pro soccer team) have seen flat ticket sales for three years. They are stuck in the "Maturity" phase. You are the new Brand Managers tasked with generating three new revenue streams without relying on ticket sales.*

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Brand Revival Pitch**

Groups will submit a visual 3-slide presentation containing:

1.  **The Ultimate Fan Bundle:** Design a creative "Bundle" aimed at a specific demographic (e.g., "The College Night Bundle: Ticket, Uber Voucher, and exclusive scarf"). Include the mathematical pricing breakdown.
2.  **The Brand Extension:** Propose one brand new product or service the team should launch that has nothing to do with playing the sport (e.g., A branded summer camp, a co-branded local coffee roast).
3.  **The Naming Rights Target:** Identify a specific zone of the stadium (e.g., the VIP Club, the Family Zone, the main gate) and pitch a local corporate sponsor who should buy the naming rights, explaining *why* their brand aligns with that zone.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Copyright & Academic Integrity:** When discussing Licensing, remind students of the legal ramifications of trademark infringement. If they mock up designs using real corporate logos for this project, it must be explicitly labeled as "For Educational Purposes Only" to respect Intellectual Property laws.
  - **Appropriate Partnerships:** All proposed Naming Rights partners must adhere to CCPS and CHHS guidelines (e.g., students cannot pitch alcohol, tobacco, or gambling companies for stadium naming rights).
