# **Lesson Plan: The Front Lines (Customer Service & Sales)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

In the sports and entertainment industry, the "product" is an experience. Therefore, exceptional customer service and effective sales techniques are critical to a venue's survival. In this interactive lesson, students will learn the 6-Step Selling Process tailored for premium ticket sales (like luxury suites). They will also learn how to de-escalate conflicts and recover frustrated fans using the T.H.A.N.K.S. customer service method.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 9:** Demonstrate customer service skills (Applying the T.H.A.N.K.S. method for service recovery).
  - **Competency 69:** Explain the nature and scope of the selling function (Understanding the difference between B2B and B2C sales in sports).
  - **Competency 70:** Explain the selling process (Mastering the 6 steps from approach to close).
  - **Competency 71:** Describe the nature of customer relations (Understanding how retention is cheaper than acquisition).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Outline the 6-Step Selling Process (Approach, Determine Needs, Present Product, Overcome Objections, Close, Suggestion Selling).
2.  Differentiate between selling features (e.g., "This suite has 12 seats") and benefits (e.g., "This suite is perfect for entertaining your top clients").
3.  Apply the T.H.A.N.K.S. method to de-escalate an upset customer.
4.  Perform a live sales or customer service scenario in a professional role-play.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook: The "Obstructed View" (10 Minutes)**

  - **Scenario:** Project a picture of a terrible stadium seat (e.g., right behind a massive concrete pillar). Tell the class: *"A fan just paid $150 for this seat. They are marching down to the box office, and they are furious. You are working the window. What is your very first sentence to them?"*
  - **Discussion:** Let students share their immediate reactions. Introduce the concept that *how* you handle the problem matters more to brand loyalty than the problem itself.

### **B. The 6-Step Selling Process (20 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction:** Walk through the 6-step process from the perspective of an Account Executive selling a VIP Season Ticket Package to a local business owner:

<!-- end list -->

1.  **Approach:** The warm intro.
2.  **Determine Needs:** Asking open-ended questions (*"Who do you usually bring to games?"*).
3.  **Present Product:** Translating features into benefits.
4.  **Overcome Objections:** Handling the "It's too expensive" complaint.
5.  **Close:** Asking for the sale.
6.  **Suggestion Selling:** Upselling VIP parking or food/beverage packages.

### **C. Service Recovery: The T.H.A.N.K.S. Method (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction:** Teach the NRF-standard customer service recovery framework:

<!-- end list -->

  - **T**hank the customer for bringing the issue to your attention.
  - **H**ear them out (active listening, no interrupting).
  - **A**pologize for the inconvenience (empathy).
  - **N**eed more information (ask clarifying questions).
  - **K**now a solution (empowerment to fix it).
  - **S**olve the problem.

### **D. The Role-Play Gauntlet (45 Minutes)**

  - **Interactive Lab:** Pair students up. Give each pair two scenario cards—one Sales scenario and one Customer Service scenario.
  - Students get 10 minutes to prep, then must perform their role-plays live. The teacher circulates and acts as the "Manager" evaluating their performance.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Role-Play Assessment**

Students are assessed using a live **Role-Play Performance Rubric**. They must successfully demonstrate:

  - Use of professional language and tone.
  - Accurate application of the 6-Step Selling Process (if the seller).
  - Accurate application of the T.H.A.N.K.S. method (if the box-office rep).
  - Active listening and adaptability when the "customer" throws a curveball.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Cavalier Expectations (Respect & Empathy):** This lesson leans heavily into the CHHS core values of Respect ("Using positive and appropriate language when communicating with others") and Empathy. Handling an angry fan requires regulating your own emotions and validating theirs.
  - **Employability Skills:** Remind students that these role-play skills translate directly to *any* future job interview or workplace conflict, not just sports marketing.
