# **Lesson Plan: The Global Expansion (International Sports & Marketing)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

Why does the NFL play regular-season games in London and Germany? Why does the NBA heavily scout and market in Europe and Asia? Because the domestic market is saturated, and the real financial growth lies overseas. In this capstone lesson, students will examine how major sports and entertainment brands navigate the complexities of international business. They will explore global trade, the impact of currency exchange rates on profitability, and how to adapt a domestic marketing campaign to respect and appeal to a foreign culture.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the final macro-economic and marketing competencies from the **8177** framework, achieving 100% course coverage:

  - **Competency 39:** Explain the nature of global trade (Understanding how merchandise is manufactured globally and how sports products/broadcasts are exported to foreign markets).
  - **Competency 40:** Describe the determinants of exchange rates and their effects (Calculating how a strong or weak US Dollar affects the profit margin of a jersey sold in Euros, Pounds, or Yen).
  - **Competency 41:** Explain marketing and its importance in a global economy (Adapting brand messaging, event times, and promotional strategies to fit different cultural norms and global time zones).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Identify the reasons why US-based sports and entertainment leagues expand internationally.
2.  Calculate basic foreign exchange rate conversions to determine the US Dollar ($) profit of international merchandise sales.
3.  Analyze cultural differences to successfully adapt a domestic marketing campaign for a foreign target audience.
4.  Develop an "International Expansion Proposal" for a chosen sports franchise or entertainment brand.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook: The 9:30 AM Kickoff (10 Minutes)**

  - **Discussion:** Ask the class: *"Why on earth would the NFL schedule a football game to start at 9:30 AM on a Sunday in the US?"* (Because it's being played in London, where it is 2:30 PM).
  - **Concept:** Explain that the NFL, NBA, and WWE have essentially maxed out their US fanbases. To grow their multi-billion dollar revenues, they must export their "product" (the games and broadcasts) overseas.

### **B. Global Trade & The Exchange Rate Math (20 Minutes)**

  - **Global Supply Chains (Comp 39):** Discuss where a typical team jersey is actually made (often Southeast Asia or Central America), shipped to the US, and then sold globally. That is the definition of global trade.
  - **The Currency Factor (Comp 40):** If an NFL team sells a sweatshirt at the London game for £80 (British Pounds), how much is that in US Dollars?

  - *Mini-Lab:* Put current exchange rates on the board (e.g., 1 GBP = 1.25 USD, 1 Euro = 1.08 USD, 100 Yen = 0.65 USD). Have students calculate the US revenue of foreign merchandise sales. Discuss how a "strong dollar" actually makes US goods more expensive for foreign fans to buy.

### **C. The International Expansion Lab (60 Minutes)**

  - **Cultural Marketing (Comp 41):** You can't just copy/paste an American ad in Tokyo or Madrid. You have to translate the language, adjust for cultural values, and change the pricing strategy.
  - **Independent/Pair Work:** Students act as the VP of International Development for a major US sports team, music festival, or entertainment brand (e.g., WWE, a specific NBA team, Coachella). They must plan a single major event in a foreign country of their choice.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Expansion Proposal**

Students must submit a digital presentation (Google Slides or Canva) detailing their international expansion event. It must include:

1.  **The Destination:** Which brand are you expanding, and what specific foreign city are you hosting your event in? *Why* is there a market for it there?
2.  **The Exchange Rate:** Identify the local currency. Price a standard general admission ticket in that local currency, and show the math converting it back to US Dollars based on today's exchange rate.
3.  **The Cultural Adaptation:** Describe one specific way you must change your event or marketing to fit the local culture (e.g., translating the slogan, altering the food concessions to match local tastes, or partnering with a local foreign celebrity/athlete).
4.  **The Global Trade Challenge:** Identify one logistical challenge of bringing an entire US sports team/event overseas (e.g., transporting equipment through customs, jet lag/time zones, tax laws).

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Global Citizenship & Respect:** Adapting marketing for foreign cultures requires empathy and cultural awareness. Remind students of the CHHS Core Value of Respect. Stereotyping, using offensive caricatures, or mocking foreign cultures in their marketing proposals will result in a failing grade and a disciplinary referral.
  - **Mathematical Integrity:** For the exchange rate calculations, students must use the actual, current conversion rates found via Google Finance or a reputable financial site, rather than guessing or making up numbers.
