# **Lesson Plan: The Hype Machine (Promotional Campaigns)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

While having a great product and the right price is essential, it means nothing if the target market doesn't know it exists. In this lesson, students will transition from general marketing strategy to specific, tactical promotion. Students will deconstruct the "Promotional Mix" and analyze how sports franchises, movie studios, and event promoters build hype. They will then apply these concepts by designing an integrated promotional campaign for an upcoming local or fictional event.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 64:** Explain the types of promotion (Differentiating between institutional and product promotion).
  - **Competency 65:** Identify the elements of the promotional mix (Advertising, Public Relations, Sales Promotion, and Personal Selling).
  - **Competency 66:** Explain the use of advertising in the sports, entertainment, and event industries (Analyzing paid media like billboards, social media ads, and TV commercials).
  - **Competency 67:** Explain the use of publicity/public relations (Understanding how to generate free media coverage and manage brand image).
  - **Competency 68:** Explain the use of sales promotion (Designing short-term incentives like giveaways, contests, and discount codes).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Define the four elements of the Promotional Mix.
2.  Differentiate between paid advertising and earned public relations/publicity.
3.  Analyze a real-world sports or entertainment campaign (e.g., a Super Bowl commercial or a viral sneaker drop) to identify its promotional strategies.
4.  Design a cohesive, three-pronged promotional campaign to drive ticket sales for a specific event.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook: The Viral Moment (15 Minutes)**

  - **Video Analysis:** Show 2-3 short clips of famous promotional moments (e.g., a highly anticipated movie trailer, a viral Super Bowl commercial, or a crazy minor league baseball promotion like "Disco Demolition Night" or a bobblehead giveaway).
  - **Discussion:** Ask the class: *"Which of these cost the team millions of dollars, and which of these generated 'free' hype on the news?"* Introduce the difference between paid Advertising and earned PR.

### **B. Deconstructing the Promotional Mix (20 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction:** Break down the four pillars with industry examples:

<!-- end list -->

  - **Advertising (Paid):** Instagram sponsored posts for a music festival, a billboard for the Richmond Kickers.
  - **Public Relations (Earned):** A star athlete visiting a local children's hospital (generating goodwill and local news coverage).
  - **Sales Promotion (Incentives):** "First 5,000 fans get a free t-shirt," or "Use promo code CAVALIERS for 20% off."
  - **Personal Selling (1-on-1):** A ticketing agent calling a local business to sell a corporate suite.

### **C. Campaign Creation Lab: "Pack the House" (55 Minutes)**

  - **Independent or Pair Work:** Students are given a scenario: Clover Hill High School is hosting a massive Friday Night playoff game (or a major charity concert in the auditorium), and attendance has been low historically. They are the new Event Promoters and have a $5,000 budget to "Pack the House."

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Campaign Storyboard**

Students must submit a "Campaign Storyboard" (using Canva or Google Docs) that includes one distinct element from each of the following promotional categories:

1.  **The Advertisement (Paid):** Design a visual mockup of an Instagram/TikTok ad or a physical poster. Detail exactly who the target audience is and how much of the $5,000 budget will be spent here.
2.  **The PR Stunt (Earned):** Pitch an idea for a "Public Relations" event leading up to the game/concert that will get the local news (NBC12) or the school newspaper to write a story about it for free.
3.  **The Sales Promotion (Incentive):** Create a short-term incentive to get people to buy tickets *early* (e.g., a halftime contest, a merchandise giveaway, or a flash sale).

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Core Values - Integrity in Advertising:** Discuss the ethical boundaries of promotion. Relate this to the CHHS Core Value of **Integrity** ("Completing my work to the best of my ability, Think before I act"). Emphasize that making false claims in advertising or creating deceptive PR stunts (bait-and-switch) violates both federal law (FTC) and ethical business practices.
  - **Technology Acceptable Use:** While researching real-world ads or finding Canva templates, students must adhere to the CCPS Chromebook policies, ensuring all media accessed is school-appropriate and relevant to the assignment.
