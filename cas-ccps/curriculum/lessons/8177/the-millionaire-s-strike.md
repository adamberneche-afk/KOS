# **Lesson Plan: The Millionaire's Strike (Organized Labor & Economics)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

When athletes go on strike, fans just see millionaires arguing with billionaires. But from an event management perspective, a sports lockout is a catastrophic disruption of the local economy. In this lesson, students will learn the fundamentals of macroeconomics by simulating a Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) negotiation between a Players Association (Union) and Team Owners. They will analyze how player specialization gives the union leverage (including the ability to play internationally in the off-season), and how a work stoppage creates a devastating ripple effect of unemployment for hourly venue staff and local businesses.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following macro-economic competencies from the **8177** framework:

  - **Competency 29:** Explain the concept of productivity (How much revenue a player generates vs. their cost).
  - **Competency 30:** Analyze impact of specialization/division of labor on productivity (Why a franchise quarterback is irreplaceable, giving the union extreme negotiating leverage).
  - **Competency 31:** Explain the concept of organized labor and business (Defining Unions, CBA, Strikes, and Lockouts).
  - **Competency 35:** Discuss the impact of a nation's unemployment rates (Analyzing the "collateral damage" of a strike on the hourly venue workers who lose their income).
  - **Competency 38:** Determine the impact of business cycles on business activities (How a prolonged lockout disrupts the economic cycle of a sports city).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Define "Organized Labor" and explain why professional athletes use unions to negotiate with billionaire owners.
2.  Explain how the "Division of Labor" (elite athletic specialization) prevents owners from simply hiring replacement workers.
3.  Analyze the macroeconomic ripple effect of a sports strike on the local community (restaurants, parking, hotels, and hourly venue staff).
4.  Negotiate a mock Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) balancing revenue sharing, worker safety, and international labor rights.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook: The Highest Paid Union Workers (10 Minutes)**

  - **Discussion:** Write "UNION WORKER" on the board. Ask students to name professions that belong to a union. (They will say teachers, plumbers, auto workers). Then, show a picture of the NFL Players Association logo.
  - **Concept (Comp 31):** Explain that every major pro sports league has a union. Athletes negotiate their healthcare, minimum salaries, and safety rules exactly like factory workers do.

### **B. Leverage & The Ripple Effect (20 Minutes)**

  - **Specialization (Comp 30):** Why can't the NFL just fire the striking players and hire guys off the street? (Because the product drops in quality, and fans won't pay to watch amateurs).
  - **Global Labor Mobility:** What happens if there is a lockout? Discuss how elite athletes (like US NBA players) can simply take their highly specialized skills to European or Asian leagues to continue earning income during a strike or the off-season. The owners, however, cannot move their physical stadiums, giving the players a unique upper hand in the global market.
  - **The Collateral Damage (Comp 35 & 38):** If the NBA locks out its players, the players are fine (they are rich or playing overseas). Who actually suffers? *The usher. The hot dog vendor. The bartender across the street from the arena.* Discuss how a strike creates localized unemployment and disrupts the business cycle of an entire city.

### **C. The CBA Negotiation Simulation (60 Minutes)**

  - **Group Work:** Divide the class evenly into two groups: **The Owners** and **The Players Association (Union)**.
  - **The Scenario:** The current CBA has expired. The sport is facing a lockout. They must negotiate a new 4-point contract before the end of the block.
  - **The Issues:** 1. *Revenue Sharing:* Owners want a 55/45 split. Players want a 50/50 split of all TV money.  
    2\. *Season Length:* Owners want to add 2 extra games to the season to make more money. Players refuse, citing health and safety risks.  
    3\. *Minimum Salary:* Players want the lowest-paid rookies to make a minimum of $1,000,000. Owners want to cap it at $750,000.  
    4\. *Off-Season Overseas Play:* Owners want to ban players from playing in European/Asian leagues during the off-season to protect their multi-million dollar investments from injury. Players demand the right to play globally to maximize their earnings and brand.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Bargaining Agreement**

At the end of the simulation, the two sides must come together and submit:

1.  **The Signed CBA:** A finalized, signed document detailing the exact compromises made on the four issues. (If they fail to reach an agreement, the "season is canceled," and both sides fail the negotiation grade).
2.  **The "Collateral Damage" Report (Individual):** Each student must submit a 1-page reflection identifying three specific local businesses or hourly job roles in the Richmond area that would suffer financial hardship if a local team (e.g., the Richmond Flying Squirrels or Kickers) went on strike, explaining *why* their business cycle is tied to the team.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Professional Conflict Resolution:** Negotiations can get heated. Remind students that professional business negotiations require respect and active listening (Workplace Readiness Competencies). Yelling, insulting the opposing side, or refusing to compromise is unprofessional and will result in a deduction of the daily participation grade.
  - **Empathy & Community Awareness:** The Collateral Damage Report is designed to foster SEL (Social-Emotional Learning). Ensure students treat the reality of hourly worker unemployment with the maturity and empathy expected of CHHS students.
