# **Lesson Plan: The NIL Deal (Endorsements & Digital Sponsorships)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

The days of relying solely on television commercials to sell sports merchandise are over. Today, brands leverage the "Name, Image, and Likeness" (NIL) of athletes and digital influencers to reach niche audiences directly on their phones. In this lesson, students will act as Brand Managers for The Cavalier Shop. They will learn the mechanics of endorsements, the legal requirements of digital sponsorships, and how to match a brand's target demographic with the correct digital influencer.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 52:** Explain the role of endorsements in sports/entertainment (Why brands pay individuals to represent their products).
  - **Competency 83:** Explain the use of specialized sponsorships (Creating unique, event-specific, or personality-driven sponsorship packages).
  - **Competency 84:** Explain the use of digital endorsements/sponsorships (Social media influencer campaigns, affiliate links, and sponsored content).
  - **Competency 20:** Demonstrate professionalism (Understanding the FTC disclosure rules for digital marketing).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Define the concept of an "Endorsement" and explain how NIL has changed the sports marketing landscape.
2.  Differentiate between macro-influencers (millions of followers) and micro-influencers (highly engaged niche audiences).
3.  Evaluate potential digital endorsers based on brand alignment rather than simply follower count.
4.  Design a digital sponsorship activation (a mock sponsored social media post) that complies with FTC disclosure guidelines.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook: The $1 Million Instagram Post (10 Minutes)**

  - **Discussion:** Show a picture of a famous athlete (e.g., Cristiano Ronaldo or Caitlin Clark) posting about a brand on Instagram. Ask: *Why does Nike pay them millions for one picture instead of buying a billboard?*
  - **Concept (Comp 52):** Introduce "Borrowed Equity." Brands borrow the trust and coolness of the athlete to sell their products to the athlete's fans.

### **B. The Rise of NIL & Digital Sponsorships (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 83 & 84):** Explain the recent shift in NCAA and High School sports regarding Name, Image, and Likeness (NIL).
  - **The Digital Shift:** Discuss how digital sponsorships work mechanically (e.g., promo codes, affiliate links, TikTok brand takeovers) versus traditional TV endorsements.
  - **Micro vs. Macro:** Explain why a local business in Midlothian would get a better Return on Investment (ROI) sponsoring a popular CHHS senior than they would sponsoring a global celebrity.

### **C. The Matchmaker Lab (15 Minutes)**

  - **Guided Practice:** Present three different products sold at The Cavalier Shop (e.g., A premium Nike sideline jacket, a novelty foam finger, a specialized track & field accessory).
  - **The Task:** Have students brainstorm which specific "type" of student or local micro-influencer should endorse each product and *why* their specific audience is the right fit.

### **D. The Activation Project (50 Minutes)**

  - **Independent Work:** Students step into the role of the Talent Agency. They must select a real public figure (athlete, musician, or local influencer) to sign a digital endorsement deal for a specific CHHS event or Cavalier Shop product.
  - They will use design software (Canva) to mock up the deliverables.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The NIL Activation Package**

Students will submit a two-part digital portfolio:

1.  **The Rationale (1 Paragraph):** A strategic explanation of *why* they chose this specific influencer. What is the influencer's target demographic, and how does it align with the CHHS community or the product being sold?
2.  **The Digital Mockup:** A visual mockup of the sponsored social media post (e.g., an Instagram Reel frame or a TikTok post).

  - *Requirement:* The post must include engaging promotional copy.
  - *Requirement:* The post must include a measurable "Call to Action" (e.g., "Use promo code CAVS26 at the school store").
  - *Requirement:* The post must legally comply with FTC guidelines by visibly including \#Ad or \#Sponsored.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Digital Citizenship & Ethics:** The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) requires all digital endorsements to be clearly disclosed. Emphasize that failing to put \#Ad on a sponsored post is not just bad etiquette; it is illegal.
  - **Appropriate Representation (CHHS Core Values):** Students must select influencers who align with the school's core values. Influencers known for controversy, explicit content, or promoting prohibited items (tobacco, alcohol, etc.) cannot be used for this school-based simulation.
