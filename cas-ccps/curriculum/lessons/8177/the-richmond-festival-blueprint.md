# **Lesson Plan: The Richmond Festival Blueprint (Event Planning Simulation)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

In this immersive simulation, students will step into the role of Lead Event Director. Tasked with creating a new, signature festival for the city of Richmond in 2026, students will architect an event from the ground up. This project breaks the event planning process into three distinct phases: The Vision (theme and target market), The Logistics (venue, personnel, and risk management), and The Business (revenue, budgeting, and promotion).

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 1:** Demonstrate creativity and innovation (Conceptualizing a unique festival theme and vibe).
  - **Competency 86:** Discuss risk-management strategies for a venue and/or event (Identifying potential threats like weather or power outages and planning mitigations).
  - **Competency 92:** Explain the process for planning an event (Structuring the blueprint from vision to execution).
  - **Competency 95:** Analyze personnel needs for an event (Identifying 5 specific job roles necessary to operate the festival).
  - **Competency 96:** Analyze the financial resources required for each event activity (Determining "Money In" revenue streams and "Money Out" major expenses).
  - **Competency 97:** Develop a marketing plan for an event (Crafting the social media pitch).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Conceptualize a thematic event and identify a highly specific target demographic.
2.  Evaluate local venue options based on logistical constraints (parking, noise, capacity).
3.  Forecast necessary personnel and operational risks associated with large-scale events.
4.  Outline basic financial structures (revenue vs. expenses) for a pop-up entertainment venture.
5.  Write compelling, concise promotional copy tailored for social media.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The "Signature Event" Discussion (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction:** What makes a "signature event"? Discuss current Richmond staples (e.g., The Richmond Folk Festival, Dominion Energy Riverrock, Watermelon Festival).
  - **Discussion:** What do these events do for the local economy? Why do cities want them?

### **B. The Blueprint Breakdown (15 Minutes)**

  - **Guided Instruction:** Walk the students through the three phases of the project.
  - **Focus Area:** Emphasize the "Risk Management" and "Business" sections. Anyone can come up with a fun party idea, but an Event Director has to know how to pay for it and keep people safe.

### **C. The Event Director's Lab (60 Minutes)**

  - **Independent Work (Comp 92):** Students act as Lead Event Directors to build their blueprints. They may use digital tools (Google Docs/Slides) to format their proposals professionally.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Richmond Festival Blueprint**

Students must submit a comprehensive proposal answering the following prompts:

**Project Title:** The Richmond Festival Blueprint

**Your Role:** You are the Lead Event Director.

**The Challenge:** Richmond needs a new signature event for 2026. You have this week to plan it.

**Phase 1: The Vision**

  - **Name Your Event:** Create a catchy name.
  - **The Theme:** What is it? (Music, Food, Sports, Gaming, Art?)
  - **The Crowd:** Who are you trying to attract? Be specific (e.g., "High school students who love rap" or "Families with young kids").
  - **The "Vibe":** Why would someone pay money to come here? What is the experience?

**Phase 2: The Logistics**

  - **Location:** Where in Richmond will you hold it? Why there? (Consider parking, size, noise).
  - **The Team:** List 5 specific job roles you need to hire to run this event (e.g., Security Guard, Audio Tech).
  - **Risk Management:** What is one thing that could go wrong? (e.g., Rain storm, power outage). How will you prepare for it?

**Phase 3: The Business**

  - **Money In:** How will you make money? (Tickets? Food sales? Sponsors?)
  - **Money Out:** What will cost the most money? (Talent? Venue rental? Insurance?)
  - **The Pitch:** Write a 3-sentence social media post to announce tickets are on sale. Include a hashtag\!

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Accountability (CHHS Core Values):** Students must take ownership of their learning by producing highly specific, realistic answers. Vague responses (e.g., Location: "A field") do not demonstrate the accountability required of a venue manager.
  - **Safe and Appropriate Themes:** Remind students that all proposed events, themes, and social media pitches must adhere to the CCPS Student Code of Conduct (e.g., no events centered around restricted substances, inappropriate entertainment, or unsafe activities).
