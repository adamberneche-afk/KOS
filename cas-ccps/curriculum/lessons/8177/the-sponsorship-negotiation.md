# **Lesson Plan: The Sponsorship Negotiation (Sales & SEL Integration)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

In the sports and entertainment industry, ticket sales barely cover the electric bill; the real revenue comes from Corporate Sponsorships. In this lesson, students will act as the Director of Corporate Partnerships for a CHHS athletic team or event. To succeed, students must rely heavily on Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) skills: using **initiative** to prospect leads, embracing **diversity** to find brands that match the student body, navigating CRM **IT systems**, and utilizing **conflict resolution** to handle sales objections during a live negotiation.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson integrates hard technical sales skills with vital Workplace Readiness (SEL) skills:

**Workplace Readiness (SEL Focus):**

  - **Competency 3:** Demonstrate initiative and self-direction (Prospecting and cold-calling leads without being hand-fed a list).
  - **Competency 6:** Demonstrate conflict-resolution skills (Navigating contract disputes and handling the word "No" professionally).
  - **Competency 8:** Demonstrate respect for diversity (Identifying local businesses owned by or catering to the diverse demographics of the CHHS community).
  - **Competency 17:** Demonstrate understanding of current IT systems (Utilizing Customer Relationship Management \[CRM\] software to track sales pipelines).

**Industry/Technical Skills:**

  - **Competency 76:** Explain the use of objection-handling techniques in the selling process.
  - **Competency 77:** Explain the closing process in selling.
  - **Competency 82:** Explain the nature of sponsorship in the sports/event industry.

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Define corporate sponsorship and explain its mutual benefit (ROI) to both the venue and the brand.
2.  Utilize a CRM system (via Google Sheets/Airtable) to input, track, and manage a sales pipeline.
3.  Identify 5 diverse local businesses that align with the demographics and values of the CHHS fan base.
4.  Apply conflict-resolution techniques (like the "Feel, Felt, Found" method) to overcome a sponsor's financial objections.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. Initiative & IT: Building the CRM Pipeline (20 Minutes)**

  - **The Tech (Comp 17):** Introduce students to the concept of a CRM (like Salesforce or HubSpot). Explain that modern sales rely on IT systems to track relationships. Provide a "Mock CRM" template in Google Sheets.
  - **The Hustle (Comp 3):** Students must use their initiative to research and input 5 *real* local businesses into their CRM that they want to target for a $500 banner sponsorship.
  - **The Diversity Check (Comp 8):** Challenge the students: *Does your prospect list reflect the actual community?* Require that at least two of their prospected businesses represent diverse ownership (e.g., minority-owned, women-owned) or cater to diverse cultural segments of the Richmond area.

### **B. The Psychology of "No": Conflict Resolution (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 6 & 76):** In sales, an objection is simply a conflict of priorities. Teach the **"Feel, Felt, Found"** conflict-resolution framework:

<!-- end list -->

  - *"I understand how you* ***feel*** *about the $500 price tag."* (Empathy)
  - *"Other local owners* ***felt*** *the same way initially."* (Validation)
  - *"But what they* ***found*** *was that the banner brought in $1,500 of new student business."* (Resolution/ROI)

### **C. The Negotiation Roleplay (45 Minutes)**

  - **Guided Practice (Comp 77 & 82):** Pair students up. One is the CHHS Sponsorship Director; the other is a local business owner who is highly skeptical and protective of their budget.
  - **The Simulation:** The Director must pitch the $500 sponsorship. The Business Owner is instructed to give at least two hard objections (e.g., "Kids don't have money to spend at my store," or "I already sponsor the rival high school"). The Director must actively resolve the conflict to close the deal.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Signed Contract & CRM**

Students will submit their finalized Sales Portfolio, which includes:

1.  **The CRM Tracker:** Their digital spreadsheet showing their 5 diverse local leads, contact information, and pipeline status.
2.  **The Objection Script:** A written script detailing how they plan to overcome the two most likely objections a local business owner will give them using the "Feel, Felt, Found" method.
3.  **The Post-Negotiation Reflection:** A brief paragraph analyzing their emotional regulation during the roleplay. (e.g., *How did it feel when they said no? How did you pivot?*)

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Community Partnerships (Core Values):** Remind students that when we talk about local businesses, we are talking about our neighbors. All mock pitches and CRM notes must maintain the highest level of professionalism and respect for these real-world business owners.
  - **Digital Citizenship:** Ensure students understand that while they are researching real businesses, they are *not* to actually contact these businesses or send emails on behalf of CHHS without explicit administrative approval.
