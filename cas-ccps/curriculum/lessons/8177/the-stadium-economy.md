# **Lesson Plan: The Stadium Economy (Macroeconomics & Venue Funding)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

When a sports franchise wants a new $2 billion stadium, they usually ask the city to help pay for it. Franchise owners argue the stadium will boost the local economy, but economists often disagree. In this lesson, students will analyze the macroeconomic factors behind venue construction. They will act as City Council Financial Analysts evaluating a proposal for a new sports complex in Richmond, applying concepts like GDP, inflation, interest rates, and the law of diminishing returns to determine if the project is a sound investment.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following macro-economic competencies from the **8177** framework:

  - **Competency 32:** Explain the impact of the law of diminishing returns (Understanding that building a stadium *too* big eventually costs more to maintain than it generates in revenue).
  - **Competency 33:** Discuss the measure of consumer spending as an economic indicator (Tracking how fans spend money at local restaurants/hotels around the venue).
  - **Competency 34:** Explain the concept of Gross Domestic Product (GDP) (Analyzing whether a stadium actually grows the total value of goods/services produced in the city).
  - **Competency 36:** Explain the economic impact of inflation on business (How the rising cost of concrete, steel, and labor drives up stadium construction costs and ticket prices).
  - **Competency 37:** Explain the economic impact of interest-rate fluctuations (Understanding that cities borrow money to build stadiums; high interest rates cost taxpayers millions in extra fees).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Define GDP and Consumer Spending, and explain how sports venues influence local economic growth.
2.  Calculate the hidden costs of venue construction caused by inflation and high interest rates.
3.  Apply the "Law of Diminishing Returns" to venue capacity and ticket pricing strategies.
4.  Evaluate a municipal funding proposal and write a data-driven recommendation to city officials.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook: The $5 Billion Megaproject (10 Minutes)**

  - **Discussion:** Show a picture of SoFi Stadium (Rams/Chargers) or the proposed new stadium for the Chicago Bears. Write the cost on the board: **$5,000,000,000**.
  - **The Question:** *Who actually pays for this? And if the city pays for half of it, is it a smart investment or a scam?*

### **B. The Macro Crash Course (20 Minutes)**

  - **The Pros (Comp 33 & 34):** Explain the argument *for* stadiums. They create construction jobs, and on game days, fans pack local bars and hotels. This drives up **Consumer Spending** and boosts the city's **GDP**.
  - **The Cons (Comp 36 & 37):** Explain the argument *against* stadiums. Cities don't have $1 Billion lying around, so they take out loans (municipal bonds). If **Interest Rates** are high, the city pays double over 30 years. Furthermore, due to **Inflation**, the cost to build is skyrocketing, meaning the team has to charge $150 for a nosebleed ticket just to break even.
  - **The Sweet Spot (Comp 32):** Introduce the **Law of Diminishing Returns**. If a team averages 10,000 fans, building a 15,000-seat arena is smart. Building a 40,000-seat arena is financial suicide. The extra 25,000 empty seats cost millions to build, heat, and clean, but generate zero revenue.

### **C. The City Council Lab (50 Minutes)**

  - **The Scenario:** A minor league sports team wants to build a $300 Million complex in Chesterfield County. They are asking the local government to pay for half ($150M) via a high-interest loan.
  - **Independent Work:** Students act as Financial Analysts for the county. They are provided a mock "Economic Impact Report" detailing the projected GDP boost, current inflation rates on construction materials, and the loan's interest rate.
  - **The Task:** They must draft a formal recommendation to the Mayor approving or denying the funding.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Financial Analyst Recommendation**

Students must submit a 1-page formal business memo to the Mayor containing:

1.  **The Verdict:** A clear "Approve" or "Deny" for the $150M public funding request.
2.  **The GDP & Spending Analysis:** A paragraph explaining how the stadium would impact local consumer spending and GDP.
3.  **The Risk Assessment:** A paragraph detailing why current inflation and interest rates make the city's loan incredibly risky.
4.  **The "Diminishing Returns" Warning:** A specific recommendation on the stadium's size/capacity to ensure the city doesn't overbuild and waste money on empty seats.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Civic Responsibility & Respect:** This lesson touches on taxes and government spending, which can mirror real-world political debates. Remind students to maintain professionalism and respect differing viewpoints, focusing purely on the economic data rather than political ideology.
  - **Mathematical Integrity:** Students must base their recommendations on the provided data packet. Fabricating financial numbers to support a biased conclusion violates academic integrity expectations.
