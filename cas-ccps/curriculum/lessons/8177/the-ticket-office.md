# **Lesson Plan: The Ticket Office (Sales Management, Quotas & Ethics)**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

Moving from an individual contributor to a management role requires a massive shift in mindset. In this lesson, students step into the role of Director of Ticket Sales for a minor league sports team. They will learn that sales management isn't just about yelling "sell more\!"—it requires mathematical strategy (setting quotas), psychological insight (motivating a burned-out team), logistical backing (sales support activities), and strict legal boundaries (ethical selling).

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson successfully completes the Sales block by addressing the following competencies from the **8177** framework:

  - **Competency 72:** Explain the nature of sales management (Overseeing personnel, budgets, and overall sales strategy).
  - **Competency 73:** Explain the nature of sales motivation (Using commission structures, bonuses, and gamification to drive performance).
  - **Competency 74:** Explain legal and ethical considerations in selling (Avoiding high-pressure scams, false promises, and ticket scalping).
  - **Competency 75:** Explain the nature of sales training (Onboarding new reps with product knowledge and objection-handling scripts).
  - **Competency 78 & 79:** Explain the nature and use of sales quotas (Setting realistic revenue or volume targets for reps to hit).
  - **Competency 80:** Explain the nature of sales support activities (Providing reps with leads, marketing materials, and CRM software).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Calculate a realistic monthly sales quota based on overall venue revenue goals.
2.  Design a multi-tiered sales motivation program (financial and non-financial incentives).
3.  Identify and correct unethical sales tactics (e.g., misrepresenting seat views or hiding hidden fees).
4.  Develop a "Sales Support" strategy to help struggling representatives hit their targets.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Hook: The Boiler Room vs. The Pro (10 Minutes)**

  - **Discussion:** Ask the class: *What is the stereotype of a "salesperson"?* (Usually: pushy, dishonest, annoying).
  - **Concept (Comp 74):** Introduce the concept of Consultative Selling vs. High-Pressure Selling. A good sports sales rep doesn't trick a fan into buying season tickets; they find out *why* the fan loves the team and offers a package that fits their budget.

### **B. Quotas & The Math of Management (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 78 & 79):** The team needs to make $100,000 this month. You have 5 sales reps.
  - **The Math:** If average season tickets cost $1,000, how many tickets does each rep need to sell? (20 tickets). *What happens if you set the quota at 50 tickets?* (Reps get discouraged and quit). *What if you set it at 5?* (The team goes bankrupt). Quotas must be challenging but mathematically possible.

### **C. Motivation, Training, & Support (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction (Comp 73, 75, 80):** Once the quota is set, how do you help them hit it?

<!-- end list -->

  - *Training:* Teaching them the stadium map and pricing tiers.
  - *Support:* Giving them a list of past ticket buyers to call (Lead Generation), rather than making them cold-call random phone numbers.
  - *Motivation:* Commission checks, "Salesperson of the Month" parking spots, or a pizza party for hitting the team goal.

### **D. The Sales Manager's Playbook Lab (50 Minutes)**

  - **Independent Work:** Students act as the Director of Ticket Sales. They must draft a comprehensive "Sales Management Playbook" for their new team of reps, tackling quotas, motivation, and ethics.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Integrity in Business:** Heavily emphasize CHHS's core value of Integrity. If a student's "Ethics Policy" relies on deceiving the customer to hit a quota, it will be rejected. Good business is built on repeat customers, which requires ultimate trust.
  - **Professional Empathy:** Managing people requires empathy. Ensure students understand that part of management is *supporting* struggling employees (Comp 80), not just firing them when they miss a target.
