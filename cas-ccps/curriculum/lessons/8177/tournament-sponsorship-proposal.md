# **Lesson Plan: Tournament Sponsorship Proposals**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

In this lesson, students will transition from event planning to event financing. Students will research local businesses in the Richmond/Chesterfield area whose target demographics align with our upcoming tournament. They will then draft a formal, persuasive sponsorship proposal letter outlining the mutual value of the partnership. The most professional and compelling letters will be officially mailed by the instructor to the targeted businesses next week to secure real funding for the event.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 15:** Demonstrate information-literacy skills (Researching local businesses, finding decision-maker contact information, and understanding the sponsor's target market).
  - **Competency 20:** Demonstrate professionalism (Crafting a formal business letter that properly represents the school).
  - **Competency 21:** Demonstrate reading and writing skills (Writing clear, persuasive, and grammatically flawless business correspondence).
  - **Competency 96:** Analyze the financial resources required for each event activity (Understanding how sponsorship dollars offset tournament expenses).
  - **Competency 97:** Develop a marketing plan for an event (Integrating corporate sponsorships into the overall promotional and financial strategy).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Differentiate between "philanthropy" (a donation) and "sponsorship" (a mutually beneficial marketing partnership).
2.  Identify local businesses that share a target market with a high school sports/entertainment tournament.
3.  Structure and write a formal business proposal letter.
4.  Articulate a clear "Value Proposition" (Return on Investment) for a potential sponsor.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. Sponsorship vs. Donation (15 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction:** Explain that businesses aren't just giving us money to be nice; they are buying access to our audience.
  - **Discussion:** If our tournament attracts 500 teenagers and their parents, what types of local businesses want to advertise to that specific crowd? (e.g., Local driving schools, sporting goods stores, fast-casual restaurants).

### **B. The Research Phase (20 Minutes)**

  - **Independent Work (Comp 15):** Students use their Chromebooks to identify *one* specific local business.
  - **The Challenge:** They cannot just address the letter "To Whom It May Concern." They must research the company to find the name of the Owner, General Manager, or Marketing Director.

### **C. Anatomy of the Pitch (45 Minutes)**

  - **Guided Practice (Comp 21):** Walk through the 4-paragraph sponsorship letter structure:

<!-- end list -->

1.  **The Hook:** Introduction of the CHHS program and the specific tournament.
2.  **The Connection:** Why this specific business is a perfect fit for our audience.
3.  **The Value Proposition:** What the sponsor gets (e.g., logo on the back of the t-shirt, banner in the gym, shoutout on the PA system).
4.  **The Call to Action:** Thanking them and stating that Mr. Berneche will follow up next week.

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Sponsorship Letter**

Each student must submit a formal, 1-page business letter.

  - **The Target:** Must be addressed to a specific, real local business and (ideally) a specific person at that organization.
  - **The Alignment:** The letter must explicitly state *why* this partnership makes sense for the business's target market.
  - **The Real-World Stake:** Students must be informed that these are *live* documents. The instructor will review, print, sign, and mail the best letters next week to secure actual event funding.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Representing the Shield (CHHS Core Values):** Because these letters will be read by community business leaders, they must reflect the school's core values of *Accountability* and *Integrity*. Letters containing slang, grammatical errors, or unprofessional formatting will not be mailed and will require a complete rewrite.
  - **Authorized Fundraising:** Students must be reminded that all school fundraising and sponsorships must follow CCPS financial guidelines. Students are *not* permitted to collect money or sign contracts themselves; all financial commitments must be routed through the instructor and the school's bookkeeping office.
