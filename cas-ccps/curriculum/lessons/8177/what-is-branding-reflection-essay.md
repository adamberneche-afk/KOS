# **Lesson Plan: "What is Branding?" Synthesis Essay**

**Course:** Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management (8177)

**Instructor:** Mr. Berneche & Marketing Co-Teacher

**Location:** Clover Hill High School, Richmond, VA

**Target Grade Level:** 10-12

## **1. Lesson Overview**

Throughout the academic year, students have explored the concept of "branding" from multiple perspectives—event promotion, venue management, corporate sponsorships, and personal professional development. In this summative reflection, students will synthesize this year-long curriculum. Stepping into the role of a "Teacher," students will write a comprehensive essay explaining the concept of branding to an audience with zero prior marketing experience.

## **2. VDOE Competency Alignment**

This lesson addresses the following competencies from the **8177: Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management** framework:

  - **Competency 2:** Demonstrate critical thinking and problem solving (Synthesizing various projects and concepts from throughout the year into a single, cohesive definition).
  - **Competency 11:** Demonstrate big-picture thinking (Connecting the abstract concept of branding to practical, real-world business applications).
  - **Competency 21:** Demonstrate reading and writing skills (Composing a formal, well-structured essay of at least 500 words).
  - **Competency 97:** Develop a marketing plan for an event (Reflecting on how brand identity dictates all marketing and promotional efforts).

## **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Formulate a comprehensive, easily understandable definition of "branding" free of complex industry jargon.
2.  Synthesize prior course projects to provide concrete examples of branding in action.
3.  Compose a professional, structured essay meeting a minimum length requirement.
4.  Demonstrate mastery of a subject by successfully adopting an instructional tone.

## **4. Instructional Sequence**

### **A. The Year in Review Brainstorm (15 Minutes)**

  - **Class Discussion:** Put the word "BRANDING" on the whiteboard. Have students call out every project or assignment from the year that involved branding (e.g., The Cavalier Shop logo, The Richmond Festival vibe, personal résumés, sponsorship letters).
  - **Concept Mapping:** Visually connect how "branding" was the underlying foundation of all these different activities.

### **B. The "Teacher" Persona (10 Minutes)**

  - **Direct Instruction:** Explain the prompt's unique constraint: they are writing for someone with *no* marketing experience.
  - **Tone & Vocabulary:** Discuss how to translate marketing jargon. Instead of saying "target demographic segmentation," a teacher might say "figuring out exactly who wants to buy your stuff."

### **C. Writing Workshop (65 Minutes)**

  - **Independent Work (Comp 21):** Students outline, draft, and finalize their essays.
  - *Teacher circulates to help students expand their thoughts, ensuring they reach the 500-word minimum through detailed examples rather than "fluff" or repetition.*

## **5. Project Deliverables: The Branding Essay**

Each student must submit a finalized essay responding to the prompt: *"What is Branding?"*

  - **The Role:** The essay must be written from the perspective of a teacher explaining the concept to a beginner.
  - **The Content:** The essay should draw on different examples and perspectives covered throughout the school year.
  - **The Length:** The final submission must be a **MINIMUM of 500 words**.

## **6. Faculty & Student Handbook Compliance**

  - **Academic Integrity (CHHS Core Values):** A 500-word minimum essay is highly susceptible to the use of Generative AI (e.g., ChatGPT). Students must be explicitly reminded of the CHHS Academic Integrity policy. Work submitted must be entirely their own. Essays suspected of being AI-generated will be subject to a zero and an administrative referral for academic dishonesty.
  - **Focus & Accountability:** During the writing workshop, students must demonstrate accountability by remaining on task and independently managing their time to meet the essay's length and quality requirements.
