ASSIGNMENT: Business Structure & Organization (S1-U2)
COURSE: 8177 Sports Entertainment and Event Management
UNIT OBJECTIVE: Students can explore organizational structures within the industry and explain for-profit vs. nonprofit operation.
PLANNED WORK PRODUCT: Conglomerate structure document: formal description of the business structure — what type of organization it is, how divisions relate to each other, who has authority over what, and how the school store's required status fits into the overall design.

COMPETENCIES ASSESSED: 8177-52, 8177-53

CRITERIA

Criterion 1: Explaining the Structure of Sports, Entertainment, and Event Businesses
- 8177-52 Explore the organizational structures within the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
    Evidence can include (examples, not all required):
    * professional sports leagues
    * sports recreation leagues
    * general business studios
    * venues (public vs. private)
    * governing bodies (e.g., National Collegiate Athletic Association [NCAA], International Olympic Committee [IOC])
    * parks and recreation authorities
    * collegiate athletic programs
- 8177-53 Explain the advantages and disadvantages of operating as a for-profit or nonprofit organization.
    Evidence can include (examples, not all required):
    * advantages of for-profit (e.g., business growth, investment potential)
    * disadvantages of for-profit (e.g., loss of revenue, business risk)
    * advantages of nonprofit (e.g., tax exemptions, more public support)
    * disadvantages of nonprofit (e.g., skepticism, insufficient volunteers, complex regulatory requirements)
    * evaluation of which alternative best aligns with the objectives

Judge each competency only by what this assignment asks the student to do. The lines under a competency are examples of what evidence can look like, not a checklist: one specific, well-done example that fits the task is enough, and a part of a competency the task does not ask for is never required.

## Prompt template

Business Structure & Organization (S1-U2)

Students can explore organizational structures within the industry and explain for-profit vs. nonprofit operation.

What you will make: Conglomerate structure document: formal description of the business structure — what type of organization it is, how divisions relate to each other, who has authority over what, and how the school store's required status fits into the overall design.

Your role: Management students draft the formal org chart with reporting lines, decision authority, and division interdependencies. General Manager candidate writes the first draft of the management charter.

The work: Students examine the conglomerate's structure formally: is it organized correctly? Does the reporting structure make sense? Are the divisions the right size? This is a management design exercise, not just a vocabulary lesson.

Go deeper:

- Organizational structures within the industry (Task 52): flat vs. hierarchical, functional vs. divisional. How does each affect a manager's day-to-day decisions?
- For-profit vs. nonprofit tradeoffs (Task 53): if the school league became a nonprofit, what would change? Students evaluate this as a real strategic question.
- Management specialization students map their own role within the org chart and write a one-paragraph role authority statement: what decisions can I make independently, and what requires approval?

Your work must show evidence of each of these:

- Explore the organizational structures within the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Explain the advantages and disadvantages of operating as a for-profit or nonprofit organization.

Use these terms accurately: Business ownership type, Organizational structure, Vertical integration, For-profit organization, Nonprofit organization, Division.

Write your response under “Your response” below.
