ASSIGNMENT: Economics of Sports, Entertainment & Events (S3-U1)
COURSE: 8177 Sports Entertainment and Event Management
UNIT OBJECTIVE: Students can analyze U.S. economic indicators, examine how sports/events affect local economies, and identify global cultural and political economic factors.
PLANNED WORK PRODUCT: Two deliverables: (1) Economic environment brief — one page per student summarizing current conditions affecting the conglomerate's market: consumer spending trends, material cost trends, and competitive landscape. (2) Wholesale sourcing decision brief — Wholesale Manager produces a one-page document recommending whether to place the first order now or delay, based on economic conditions and cost analysis.
COURSE ROLE: Wholesale & Sourcing Manager leads the sourcing decision brief. General Manager reviews and approves or requests revision. Economics tasks 49–51 are assessed through the management lens — how do these indicators affect operational decisions?
COMPETENCIES ASSESSED: 8177-49, 8177-50, 8177-51

CRITERIA

Criterion 1: Describing the Economics of the Sports, Entertainment, and Event Industries
- 8177-49 Analyze key U.S. economic indicators and their effects on the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
    * Evidence of Inflation
    * Evidence of Employment
    * Evidence of Deflation
    * Evidence of Pricing
    * Evidence of Gross Domestic Product (GDP)
    * Evidence of Productivity
    * Evidence of Gross National Product (GNP)
    * Evidence of Consumer Price Index (CPI)
    * Evidence of Producer Price Index (PPI)
- 8177-50 Examine how sports, entertainment, and events affect local, state, and national economies.
    * Evidence of the local level (e.g., economic impact of the Super Bowl on host city)
    * Evidence of the state level (e.g., horse racing)
    * Evidence of the national level (e.g., food/beverage consumption during the Super Bowl, hosting watch parties, going out to watch the game)
    * Evidence of The international level (e.g., Olympic site selection)
- 8177-51 (Optional) Identify cultural and political environmental factors and their economic impact on the global sports, entertainment, and event industries.
    * Evidence of beliefs
    * Evidence of behaviors
    * Evidence of heritage
    * Evidence of government structure
    * Evidence of economic stability
    * Evidence of terrorism threats/security
    * Evidence of current events
    * Evidence of international agreements

PASSING STANDARD
The work passes when the planned work product shows evidence for every competency listed above, in the student's own words, applied to the class conglomerate (its divisions, school store, events and esports program) or a real organization in sports, entertainment or events, and uses the unit vocabulary accurately (Supply and demand, Business cycle, Opportunity cost, Economic indicator, Direct competition, Economic impact, Consumer confidence). Each criterion needs specific examples, not general statements. Work that restates definitions without applying them, or leaves any listed competency without evidence, does not pass.

Economics of Sports, Entertainment & Events (S3-U1)

Students can analyze U.S. economic indicators, examine how sports/events affect local economies, and identify global cultural and political economic factors.

What you will make: Two deliverables: (1) Economic environment brief — one page per student summarizing current conditions affecting the conglomerate's market: consumer spending trends, material cost trends, and competitive landscape. (2) Wholesale sourcing decision brief — Wholesale Manager produces a one-page document recommending whether to place the first order now or delay, based on economic conditions and cost analysis.

Your role: Wholesale & Sourcing Manager leads the sourcing decision brief. General Manager reviews and approves or requests revision. Economics tasks 49–51 are assessed through the management lens — how do these indicators affect operational decisions?

The work: Before placing the first purchase order, the Wholesale Manager conducts an economic environment check: what is the current cost of raw materials? Has inflation affected blank garment pricing? What is the school community's spending capacity? This unit gives students the vocabulary and framework for that analysis.

Go deeper:

- U.S. economic indicators deep dive (Task 49): students identify three current economic indicators relevant to the conglomerate's cost structure — CPI for apparel, consumer confidence index, unemployment rate among school-age households — and explain how each affects a specific operational decision.
- Economic impact analysis (Task 50): students draft a simple economic impact model for the school esports league's first season — projected store revenue, event revenue, and community spending generated by home matches.
- Global and cultural factors (Task 51, optional): how do global supply chain disruptions affect blank garment availability and pricing? Students trace one product in the catalog from raw material to finished good across the global supply chain.

Your work must show evidence of each of these:

- Analyze key U.S. economic indicators and their effects on the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Examine how sports, entertainment, and events affect local, state, and national economies.
- (Optional) Identify cultural and political environmental factors and their economic impact on the global sports, entertainment, and event industries.

Use these terms accurately: Supply and demand, Business cycle, Opportunity cost, Economic indicator, Direct competition, Economic impact, Consumer confidence.
