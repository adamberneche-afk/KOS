## Rubric

```
ASSIGNMENT: Customer Service Skills (S5-U2)
COURSE: 8175 Sports Entertainment and Event Marketing
UNIT OBJECTIVE: Students can explain customer service importance, compare types of customer interaction, translate technical language for customers, and describe complaint handling procedures.
PLANNED WORK PRODUCT: Three deliverables: (1) Conglomerate Customer Service Standards document — two pages, approved by General Manager. (2) Role-play assessment — each student completes two role-plays: one in-person store scenario and one digital/social media scenario. Assessed by a peer using a provided rubric. (3) Complaint resolution log template — a simple tracking document the Store Manager uses to record every complaint, resolution, and follow-up.
COURSE ROLE: Sales & Customer Experience rotation students lead the Customer Service Standards draft — they are the primary customer-facing role. All 8175 rotation students complete the role-play assessments. Brand & Creative students review the standards for alignment with the brand voice — does the language sound like the brand?
COMPETENCIES ASSESSED: 8175-52, 8175-53, 8175-54, 8175-55

CRITERIA

Criterion 1: Developing Customer Service Skills
- 8175-52 Explain the importance of customer service as it relates to the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
    Evidence can include (examples, not all required):
    * the concept that customer service reflects the ability to balance the customers’ needs and company policy
    * the reasons that customer service is important, including how it
    * establishes customer loyalty
    * increases customer satisfaction
    * encourages customers to return
    * enhances the image of a company
    * generates more revenue
- 8175-53 Compare various types of customer interaction.
    Evidence can include (examples, not all required):
    * self-service
    * limited service
    * full service
    * automation
    * internal customer (e.g., fellow employees, other departments within the organization)
    * external customer (e.g., suppliers, contractors)
    * examples of procedures and practices within each type of customer service
- 8175-54 Translate technical language into commonly understood language.
    Evidence can include (examples, not all required):
    * understanding specialized vocabulary
    * analyzing the audience
    * expressing technical terms and concepts in everyday language
- 8175-55 Describe procedures for handling a customer complaint.
    Evidence can include (examples, not all required):
    * listening
    * receiving feedback
    * giving feedback
    * empathizing
    * maintaining ethical standards
    * displaying a clear understanding of company policies
    * exhibiting patience
    * preventing vs. solving problems

PASSING STANDARD
The work passes when the planned work product does what the assignment asks and shows each competency listed above in at least one specific way, in the student's own words, applied to the class conglomerate (its divisions, school store, events and esports program) or a real organization in sports, entertainment or events, and uses the unit vocabulary accurately (Customer touchpoint, Complaint resolution, Customer retention, Internal customer, Constructive feedback, Service standard, Escalation). Specific examples, not general statements. Work that restates definitions without applying them, or leaves a listed competency with no evidence at all, does not pass. Judge each competency only by what this assignment asks the student to do. The lines under a competency are examples of what evidence can look like, not a checklist: one specific, well-done example that fits the task is enough, and a part of a competency the task does not ask for is never required.

Customer Service Skills (S5-U2)

Students can explain customer service importance, compare types of customer interaction, translate technical language for customers, and describe complaint handling procedures.

What you will make: Three deliverables: (1) Conglomerate Customer Service Standards document — two pages, approved by General Manager. (2) Role-play assessment — each student completes two role-plays: one in-person store scenario and one digital/social media scenario. Assessed by a peer using a provided rubric. (3) Complaint resolution log template — a simple tracking document the Store Manager uses to record every complaint, resolution, and follow-up.

Your role: Sales & Customer Experience rotation students lead the Customer Service Standards draft — they are the primary customer-facing role. All 8175 rotation students complete the role-play assessments. Brand & Creative students review the standards for alignment with the brand voice — does the language sound like the brand?

The work: Students draft the Conglomerate Customer Service Standards — a two-page document covering in-person store interactions, custom order consultations, social media response standards, event-day customer management, and the complaint resolution process. Each division reviews the standards for role-specific applicability.

Go deeper:

- Customer interaction comparison (Task 53): students map the full customer journey through the conglomerate — from first awareness of the school store to completing a custom order — and identify every touchpoint where the customer interaction type changes. Which touchpoints carry the highest reputational risk?
- Technical translation exercise (Task 54): students are given three pieces of internal production language ('the transfer didn't adhere due to substrate temperature,' 'we're out of that colorway in polyester,' 'the file resolution is too low for large format') and must rewrite each as a clear, professional customer-facing explanation.
- Complaint scripts: students write three complete complaint response scripts for the most likely store scenarios — wrong item, delayed order, product quality issue — and present them for class feedback before they are incorporated into the Customer Service Standards.

Your work must show evidence of each of these:

- Explain the importance of customer service as it relates to the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Compare various types of customer interaction.
- Translate technical language into commonly understood language.
- Describe procedures for handling a customer complaint.

Use these terms accurately: Customer touchpoint, Complaint resolution, Customer retention, Internal customer, Constructive feedback, Service standard, Escalation.

Write your response under “Your response” below.
```
