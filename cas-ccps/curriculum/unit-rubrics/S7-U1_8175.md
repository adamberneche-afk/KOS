Promotion, Sponsorship & Endorsements (S7-U1)

Students can explain the promotional mix, identify roles of advertising/publicity/sales promotion/direct marketing/personal selling, describe advertising elements, explain PR and PR tools, explain sponsorship and endorsements with legal restrictions, and describe promotional campaign elements.

What you will make: Four deliverables: (1) Campaign brief — one page defining objective, audience, message, channel mix, timeline, and success metrics. Approved by General Manager before execution begins. (2) Asset library — all promotional materials produced: posters, social graphics, digital signage, press release, event-day signage. Filed in the Creative Studio shared drive. (3) Sponsorship prospectus — a one-page document the conglomerate can present to potential sponsors for the league, defining sponsorship tiers and benefits. (4) Campaign results report — post-match document measuring actual attendance against goal, social reach, and engagement metrics. Honest assessment of what worked and what didn't.

Your role: Promotions & Sponsorship rotation students lead campaign design and execution. Brand & Creative rotation students produce all visual assets using templates established in Stage 2. Sales & Customer Experience students handle event-day promotional activation — giveaways, merch table setup, spectator engagement. Marketing Advisor rotation students write the campaign brief and results report.

The work: Students design and execute a three-week promotional campaign for the first home esports match. Week one: campaign strategy and asset production. Week two: content rollout and community outreach. Week three: urgency push, event-day promotion, and day-of activation. The large format printer runs continuously producing signage, posters, and bracket displays. Every piece of promotional content is approved against the Social Media Policy from Stage 5 before posting.

Go deeper:

- Promotional mix application (Task 72–73): students map every promotional action in their campaign to one of the five promotional mix components. No promotional action is unaccounted for. The campaign brief includes a promotional mix allocation — how much time and resource goes to each component and why.
- Advertisement evaluation (Task 77): students evaluate three pieces of promotional content produced for the campaign against the goals and methods framework — did the poster achieve its awareness goal? Did the social post achieve its engagement goal? Evidence-based assessment, not opinion.
- PR tools in practice (Task 83–84): students pitch a story about the esports league to the school newspaper or morning announcement program. They write the pitch, follow up, and document the outcome — whether the story runs or not. Both outcomes are learning.
- Legal restrictions on endorsements (Task 89): if the conglomerate asks a popular student to promote the school store on their personal social media, what are the legal and ethical requirements? Students research FTC disclosure guidelines for student-age influencers and write a one-paragraph policy for the conglomerate.
- Sponsorship prospectus depth (Tasks 87–91): students research three local businesses that might be logical sponsors for the esports league — gaming cafes, tech retailers, food service — and write a customized one-paragraph outreach pitch for each, connecting the sponsor's audience to the conglomerate's.

Your work must show evidence of each of these:

- Explain promotion and promotional mix.
- Identify the roles of advertising, publicity, sales promotion, direct marketing, and personal selling in the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Identify methods of communication used in sports, entertainment, and event promotion.
- Describe the elements of an advertisement.
- Explain advertising as related to the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Identify the goals of advertising and the methods of evaluating an advertisement’s success.
- Explain public relations as related to the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Describe public relations (PR) tools.
- (Optional) Explain the internal and external aspects of public relations.
- Explain sponsorship and endorsements as they relate to the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Compare the reasons for using sponsorships vs. endorsements.
- Identify legal restrictions placed on endorsements and sponsorships.
- Identify current trends in sponsorship within the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Describe the components and relationships between stakeholders.
- Explain the advantages and disadvantages of using various forms of sales promotion in the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Describe the elements of sports, entertainment, and event promotional campaigns.

Use these terms accurately: Promotional mix, Campaign brief, Public relations (PR), Sponsorship, Endorsement, Sales promotion, Stakeholder, Promotional campaign.

Write your response under “Your response” below.
