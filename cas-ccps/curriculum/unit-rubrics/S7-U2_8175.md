```
ASSIGNMENT: Sales Process & On-Site Merchandising (S7-U2)
COURSE: 8175 Sports Entertainment and Event Marketing
UNIT OBJECTIVE: Students can explain types of selling, product/service bundling, the selling process steps, and on-site merchandising and concessions.
PLANNED WORK PRODUCT: Four deliverables: (1) Merchandise table plan — layout diagram, inventory selection and quantities, pricing and bundle display, signage list, and role assignments for event-day. (2) Sales tracking sheet — a simple transaction log completed during the event: item sold, price, bundle or individual, time of sale. (3) Post-event revenue report — total revenue by SKU, bundle performance, busiest selling window, and three specific recommendations for the next event. (4) On-site revenue stream map (8177) — a one-page document identifying all active revenue streams at the first match, projected vs. actual for each, and variance explanation.
COURSE ROLE: Sales & Customer Experience rotation students own the merchandise table operation entirely — planning, setup, staffing, selling, and post-event reporting. This is the capstone assessment for the Sales & Customer Experience rotation. Marketing Advisor rotation students produce the bundle offer analysis — what bundles to offer, at what price, with what projected uptake based on market research data.
COMPETENCIES ASSESSED: 8175-94, 8175-95, 8175-96, 8175-97

CRITERIA

Criterion 1: Describing the Sales Process
- 8175-94 Explain the types of selling and why they are important in the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
    Evidence can include (examples, not all required):
    * recognizing the importance of selling as part of the marketing concept
    * explaining and making a distinction between
    * personal selling (e.g., salespeople, merchandise sales, interviewing)
    * technology-based selling (e.g., online sales, email marketing, social media marketing)
- 8175-95 Explain the concept of bundling products and/or services.
    Evidence can include (examples, not all required):
    * the definition of bundling —combining multiple products to be sold as one package
    * the concept that there are more venue opportunities through bundling beyond the primary product or service (e.g., licensing, food and beverage)
- 8175-96 Explain the steps of the selling process.
    Evidence can include (examples, not all required):
    * performing pre-approach activities
    * obtaining product knowledge
    * prospecting and/or qualifying
    * planning the approach
    * recognizing the needs of customers
    * presenting and demonstrating
    * overcoming objections
    * closing the deal
    * asking for referrals
    * offering suggestion selling
    * initiating the follow-up (relationship marketing)
- 8175-97 Explain on-site merchandising and concessions as they relate to sports, entertainment, and event facilities.
    Evidence can include (examples, not all required):
    * food/beverage options
    * premium services (e.g., at-seat service, suites, touch screen order delivery)
    * exclusive sponsorships
    * parking
    * ticketing/registration
    * restaurants
    * retail stores and kiosks
    * enhanced activities (e.g. pre-game entertainment, kids’ activities, areas for socializing)
    * merchandising
    * non-game event operations/alternate venue usage

PASSING STANDARD
The work passes when the planned work product does what the assignment asks and shows each competency listed above in at least one specific way, in the student's own words, applied to the class conglomerate (its divisions, school store, events and esports program) or a real organization in sports, entertainment or events, and uses the unit vocabulary accurately (Selling process, Upselling, Bundling, On-site merchandising, Revenue stream, Average transaction value, Post-event reconciliation). Specific examples, not general statements. Work that restates definitions without applying them, or leaves a listed competency with no evidence at all, does not pass. Judge each competency only by what this assignment asks the student to do. The lines under a competency are examples of what evidence can look like, not a checklist: one specific, well-done example that fits the task is enough, and a part of a competency the task does not ask for is never required.
```

```
Your role: Sales & Customer Experience rotation students own the merchandise table operation entirely — planning, setup, staffing, selling, and post-event reporting. This is the capstone assessment for the Sales & Customer Experience rotation. Marketing Advisor rotation students produce the bundle offer analysis — what bundles to offer, at what price, with what projected uptake based on market research data.

The work: Students plan and execute the full on-site sales operation for the first home esports match. Pre-event: merchandise table layout, inventory selection and quantity, signage production, bundle offer finalization, and sales role assignments. Event-day: table setup, active selling throughout the event, transaction tracking. Post-event: revenue reconciliation, unsold inventory return to store, and sales performance report.

Go deeper:

- Selling process role-play (Task 96): before the event, students practice the seven-step selling process in pairs using the actual merchandise as props. The scenario: a student picks up a jersey, looks at the price tag, and starts to put it back. Walk through the full selling process from that moment. Assessed by a peer using a provided rubric before the event happens.
- On-site merchandising theory to practice (Task 97): students research one professional sports or esports organization's merchandise operation — what do they sell at events, how is the table or kiosk set up, what are the signature bundle offers — and apply one specific finding to their merchandise table plan.
- Objection handling: students identify the three most likely customer objections at the merchandise table ('too expensive,' 'I'll think about it,' 'I don't have cash') and script a response to each that moves toward a sale without pressure. Scripts are incorporated into the pre-event briefing for all Sales rotation students.

Your work must show evidence of each of these:

- Explain the types of selling and why they are important in the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Explain the concept of bundling products and/or services.
- Explain the steps of the selling process.
- Explain on-site merchandising and concessions as they relate to sports, entertainment, and event facilities.

Use these terms accurately: Selling process, Upselling, Bundling, On-site merchandising, Revenue stream, Average transaction value, Post-event reconciliation.

Write your response in the response zone below.
```
