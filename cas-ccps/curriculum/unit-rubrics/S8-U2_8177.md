## Prompt template

```
Event Planning & Management (S8-U2)

Students can explain the event planning process, describe consumer motivation factors, explain popularity/competitive factors on attendance, analyze personnel needs, analyze financial resources, develop an event marketing plan and budget, develop post-event recognition, manage a live event, and evaluate overall success.

What you will make: Five deliverables per event, accumulated across the season: (1) Personnel plan — role assignments, backup assignments, and briefing notes for every position. (2) Event budget — projected and actual P&L. (3) Event marketing plan — adapted from the Stage 8 Card 1 framework for each specific match. (4) Run-of-show — completed template with timestamps, activities, owners, and status tracking. (5) Post-event evaluation — attendance, revenue, operational, and promotional assessment with specific recommendations for the next event.

Your role: Events Manager owns the run-of-show and personnel plan. General Manager chairs planning meetings and owns the post-event evaluation. Store Manager owns event P&L. League Manager owns competitive operations. All 8177 students complete the financial resource analysis (Task 96) and post-event recognition activities (Task 99) for every home match. Tasks 100 and 101 (managing and evaluating the occasion) are assessed on the final match of the season as the capstone performance assessment.

The work: Students plan and execute the full sequence of home matches for the league season. Each match is treated as a distinct event with its own planning cycle — personnel analysis, budget, marketing plan, run-of-show, and post-event evaluation. The General Manager chairs the planning meeting for each match. Division managers report their division's readiness status. The run-of-show template above is completed and distributed before every event.

Go deeper:

- Personnel analysis in depth (Task 95): for each home match, the Events Manager produces a formal personnel plan that maps every role, names the person responsible, identifies the backup, and specifies the briefing time. After the match, the plan is evaluated — were there coverage gaps? Which roles were over-resourced? The plan improves with each iteration.
- Motivation and competitive factors (Tasks 93–94): after the first two matches, management students analyze attendance trends — did it grow, hold, or decline? They identify the motivational factors that drove the best-attended match and the competitive factors that hurt the lowest-attended one, and adjust the third match's promotional strategy accordingly.
- Post-event recognition system (Task 99): management students design a standing recognition protocol — what happens after every match to acknowledge players, volunteers, and staff. Consistency matters more than elaborateness. A reliable public acknowledgment after every event builds the culture the league needs to sustain itself.
- Season-end evaluation (Task 101): the final post-event evaluation covers the entire season — total attendance, total revenue, operational improvement across all matches, promotional effectiveness trends, and a formal recommendation for year two. This document is presented to administration alongside the marketing plan.

Your work must show evidence of each of these:

- Explain the process for planning an event.
- Describe factors that motivate consumers to attend sports, entertainment, and event occasions.
- Explain how event popularity and competitive factors relate to attendance.
- Analyze personnel needs for an event.
- Analyze the financial resources required for each event activity.
- Develop a marketing plan for an event.
- Develop a budget for an event.
- Develop follow-up activities to recognize and thank participants after an event.
- (Optional) Manage a sports, entertainment, and event occasion.
- (Optional) Evaluate the event’s overall success.

Use these terms accurately: Run-of-show, Personnel plan, Event P&L, Consumer motivation, Post-event evaluation, Competitive calendar, Recognition protocol.
```
