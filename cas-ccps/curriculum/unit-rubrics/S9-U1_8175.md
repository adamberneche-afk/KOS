## Prompt template

```
Career Development & Job Search (S9-U1)

Students can identify industry occupations, assess personal skills and interests, identify job search resources, create a networking plan, develop a résumé and cover letter, complete a job application, and describe the interview process.

What you will make: Five deliverables: (1) Résumé — experience drawn from division roles and WBL artifacts, formatted professionally. (2) Cover letter — written for a real or realistic job posting in the student's target career cluster. (3) Networking plan — completed template below, minimum five contacts identified and outreach actions documented. (4) Career plan — completed template below, including education path, skill gap analysis, and five-year trajectory. (5) Mock interview or informational interview — Task 113 for 8175 (optional), strongly recommended for all students. Results documented in writing.

Your role: All 8175 rotation students produce the full five-deliverable portfolio. The résumé reflects their rotation history — each rotation track is a distinct role with specific deliverables listed as accomplishments. The cover letter targets a role in their highest-engagement rotation track — the one where they felt most effective and most interested.

The work: Students conduct a reverse audit of their year: every role they held, every deliverable they produced, and every competency they demonstrated. This audit becomes the raw material for the résumé, cover letter, and career plan. Students who held management specializations have deeper documentation of a narrower range. Students who rotated across marketing tracks have broader documentation of multiple functions.

Go deeper:

- Occupation mapping (Task 106): students identify ten specific job titles in their target career cluster — not generic categories but actual titles from real job postings — and map each to a division role or WBL artifact from this program. Which roles are they already qualified for at entry level based on documented experience?
- Résumé accomplishment statements (Task 110): students transform each WBL deliverable into a résumé accomplishment statement using the PAR format — Problem, Action, Result. Example: 'Designed and executed a three-week promotional campaign for a live esports event, achieving 92% of attendance goal with zero paid media budget.'
- Mock interview preparation (Task 112–113): students prepare answers to the five most common behavioral interview questions in sports and entertainment — using documented WBL evidence for every answer. The mock interview is conducted with a peer, a teacher, or a community professional using a structured rubric.
- Digital portfolio development: students assemble a simple digital portfolio — a Google Site, a PDF packet, or a shared Drive folder — containing their top three WBL artifacts, their résumé, and a one-paragraph professional bio. This portfolio is submitted with the cover letter for the target job posting.

Your work must show evidence of each of these:

- Identify occupations within the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Identify personal skills and interests applicable to the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Identify resources for a job search in the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Create a networking plan in the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Develop a résumé and cover letter.
- Complete a job application.
- Describe the job interview process.
- (Optional) Participate in a mock interview or an informational interview with a local employer.

Use these terms accurately: Portfolio, Behavioral interview question, PAR statement, Networking, Career plan, Professional development, Informational interview, Employer research.

Write your response under “Your response” below.
```
