## Prompt template

```
Career Development & Job Search (S9-U1)

Students can identify career information sources, identify career opportunities at multiple levels, describe education/training requirements, identify employment barriers, identify professional development opportunities, research a prospective employer, and develop a career plan.

What you will make: Five deliverables: (1) Résumé — experience drawn from division roles and WBL artifacts, formatted professionally. (2) Cover letter — written for a real or realistic job posting in the student's target career cluster. (3) Networking plan — completed template below, minimum five contacts identified and outreach actions documented. (4) Career plan — completed template below, including education path, skill gap analysis, and five-year trajectory. (5) Mock interview or informational interview — Task 113 for 8175 (optional), strongly recommended for all students. Results documented in writing.

Your role: Management specialization students produce résumés that emphasize their specialization role as a sustained leadership experience rather than a rotation. The General Manager documents cross-divisional coordination and administrative reporting. The career plan (Task 108) is the 8177 capstone document for this unit — connecting a year of management specialization to a specific post-secondary and career pathway.

The work: Students conduct a reverse audit of their year: every role they held, every deliverable they produced, and every competency they demonstrated. This audit becomes the raw material for the résumé, cover letter, and career plan. Students who held management specializations have deeper documentation of a narrower range. Students who rotated across marketing tracks have broader documentation of multiple functions.

Go deeper:

- Career levels in management (Task 103): students map the career ladder in their target management cluster — what is the entry-level role, what is the mid-level role, what is the senior role, and what does the transition between each level require? They identify one person currently in their target mid-level role and research their career path.
- Education and training requirements (Task 104): for the target entry-level role, what credentials are standard — degree, certification, portfolio, or demonstrated experience? Students evaluate their current status against each requirement and identify the gaps they will close in the next two years.
- Employment barriers and strategies (Task 105, optional): students identify one realistic barrier to their target career — geographic, financial, credential-based, or network-based — and develop a specific strategy to address it. This is not pessimism; it is realistic planning that distinguishes prepared candidates from surprised ones.
- Employer research deliverable (Task 107): students research one real employer in their target cluster — an esports organization, a sports marketing agency, a venue management company, or a sports entertainment brand — and produce a one-page employer profile: mission, recent news, organizational structure, open roles, and a paragraph on why this employer specifically aligns with their career plan.
- Professional development pathway (Task 106): students identify three specific professional development actions they will take in the next twelve months — a DECA competitive event registration, a LinkedIn Learning certification, an informational interview with a professional in their target cluster, or a college program visit. Each action has a deadline and a success criterion.

Your work must show evidence of each of these:

- Identify career and employment-related information sources.
- Identify career opportunities at different levels in sports, entertainment, and event management.
- Describe education and training needed for a sports, entertainment, and event management career.
- (Optional) Identify barriers to employment and strategies to overcome them.
- Identify professional development opportunities in the sports, entertainment, and event industries.
- Collect information about a prospective employer.
- Develop a career plan.

Use these terms accurately: Portfolio, Behavioral interview question, PAR statement, Networking, Career plan, Professional development, Informational interview, Employer research.

Write your response in the response zone below.
```
