The Business of the Super Bowl
More Than Just a Game
Image: Photo of a packed football stadium at night

Speaker Notes:
Good morning, everyone. Today I'm going to talk about why the Super Bowl is the biggest marketing event in America. (Nonverbal: I will pause, smile, and make eye contact with different parts of the room before I begin.) Most people think it's just a football game, but for businesses, it's the most important day of the year.

Slide 2
On-slide text:
Why Brands Pay Big
Over 100 million viewers
Live, ad-supported audience
People watch the commercials
Social media buzz for days
Image: Small bar chart of viewership by year

Speaker Notes:
So why do companies care so much? (Verbal: I will explain this point in my own words instead of reading the slide.) The Super Bowl regularly gets more than 100 million viewers, and it's one of the few times people actually want to watch the ads. People talk about the commercials at work and online for days afterward. (Visual: I will point to the bar chart to show how viewership has stayed huge year after year.)

Slide 3
On-slide text:
The Price Tag
About $7 million
For just 30 seconds
Plus production costs
Image: Dollar sign graphic

Speaker Notes:
Now here's the shocking part. A 30-second commercial costs around seven million dollars, and that doesn't even include the cost of making the ad, hiring celebrities, or the music. (Nonverbal: I will hold up my hand with fingers spread to show "thirty seconds" and pause for effect.) That's more than a lot of people make in their entire lifetime, just for half a minute of TV time.

Slide 4
On-slide text:
Is It Worth It?
Massive brand awareness
Viral social media moments
Higher sales after the game
Risk: forgettable ads flop
Image: Two simple icons, a thumbs up and a thumbs down

Speaker Notes:
So is it worth it? For many brands, yes. A great ad can go viral and reach millions of extra people online for free. Brands also see sales go up in the weeks after. But there's a risk. If your ad is boring or confusing, you just spent millions of dollars for nothing. That's why companies spend months planning these commercials.

Slide 5
On-slide text:
Key Takeaway
The game sells the audience
The ads sell the brand
Questions?
Image: Football on a white background

Speaker Notes:
To wrap it up, the Super Bowl shows how sports and marketing work together. The game brings in the audience, and the ads use that audience to build their brands. (Nonverbal: I will step toward the audience and make eye contact as I ask for questions.) Thank you for listening. Does anyone have any questions?

Rule of Six Check
