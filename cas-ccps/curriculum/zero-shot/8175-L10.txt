Service Experience Reflection

The Action: Last summer, I went to a Chick-fil-A with my family during a busy lunch rush, and the service stood out compared to other fast-food restaurants. Even though the line was wrapped around the building, employees came outside with tablets to take orders, which made the line move much faster than I expected. When we got to the window, the worker greeted us with a smile, said "my pleasure" when we thanked her, and even noticed my little brother and gave him an extra cup of ice cream. Inside, another employee walked around offering to refill drinks and clear trays, which is something you almost never see at a fast-food restaurant.

The Emotion: As a customer, this made me feel welcomed and valued instead of feeling like just another order number. I felt like the employees actually cared whether we had a good experience. It made our family relaxed and happy, even though the restaurant was crowded.

The Value: This kind of service increases the perceived value of the purchase. The food cost about the same as other fast-food places, but because the service was so friendly and fast, it felt like we got more for our money. Great service like this is why my family keeps going back and even recommends it to friends, which shows how one employee's actions can affect the whole company's reputation.

The Recovery: A time when service went wrong was when my friend ordered tickets at a movie theater, and the employee accidentally sold her seats for the wrong showtime. When she brought it up, the employee acted annoyed and told her she should have checked the ticket herself. Using the LAST steps, the employee should have handled it differently. First, he should have Listened carefully to her problem without interrupting or getting defensive. Next, he should have Apologized sincerely, saying something like, "I'm so sorry about the mix-up." Then, he should have Solved the problem by exchanging the tickets for the correct showtime or offering a refund, and maybe a free popcorn for the trouble. Finally, he should have Thanked her for bringing it to his attention and for choosing their theater. If he had done this, my friend probably would have left happy, but instead she now goes to a different theater. This shows that courteous and helpful service, which was present at Chick-fil-A and missing at the theater, can decide whether a customer comes back or never returns.
