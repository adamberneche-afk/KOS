Industry Publication: GAME FACE
The Magazine for Young Sports Fans and Future Athletes

Industry: Sports and Athletics
Target Market (Demographic): Teens ages 13-18, mostly high school athletes and sports fans, who follow sports on social media, buy sneakers and gear, and are thinking about playing in college.

Group Roles
Editor-in-Chief: keeps the team on schedule and checks the final layout
Lead Writer: writes the News Piece and helps edit
Opinion Writer: writes the Opinion Piece
Art Director: designs the cover and leads the advertisements

COVER
Title "GAME FACE" in bold red and white letters across the top. A full-page action photo of a high school basketball player mid-dunk under arena lights. Cover lines down the side: "NIL Money: Can High Schoolers Cash In?", "Is Youth Sports Getting Too Expensive?", "The Sneaker Drops You Need This Fall" and "Train Like a Pro in 20 Minutes." Price $4.99 in the corner with a barcode graphic.

ARTICLES
1. News Piece: "High School Athletes Enter the NIL Era"
More states now allow high school athletes to earn money from their Name, Image and Likeness. This article explains what NIL is, which states allow it, how athletes are signing deals with local businesses and what rules they must follow to stay eligible.

2. Opinion Piece: "Travel Ball Is Pricing Kids Out of Sports"
Club and travel teams can cost families thousands of dollars a year. This piece argues that schools and communities need more affordable options so talented kids are not left behind just because they cannot pay.

3. Feature: "20-Minute Workouts That Actually Work"
A simple at-home training plan built with tips from athletic trainers, focused on speed, core strength and injury prevention.

4. Feature: "Sneaker Season: The Drops Worth Your Money"
A guide to upcoming releases, prices and which shoes perform best on the court versus which are just for style.

ADVERTISEMENTS

Ad 2: Velocity Sneakers. Neon green shoe floating over a black background. "Built for Your Fastest Season. 15% off with code GAMEFACE."
Ad 3: Elite Edge Training Camp. Photo of teens running drills. "Summer camp spots open now. Get recruited."

Each ad uses bold colors, a clear product image and a call to action aimed at teen athletes.

PEER REVIEW SUMMARY (Day 2)
Each member read one other article and gave feedback on clarity, facts and whether it fit our audience. Main changes: the Opinion Piece added real cost examples, and the News Piece shortened long paragraphs so it is easier to read.

PRICING STRATEGY
Print copy: $4.99, which is affordable for teens and similar to other niche magazines.
Bundle: $12 for three issues to reward loyal readers.

GOING DIGITAL: TWO WAYS TO MAKE MONEY ONLINE
1. Digital Subscriptions: Readers pay $2.99 a month for access to the full magazine online, exclusive workout videos and early sneaker news. This creates steady, monthly income.
2. Sponsored Posts and Video: Brands like sneaker companies or sports drinks pay us to create sponsored Instagram and TikTok videos, such as "Testing the new Velocity runner." Because our audience is highly targeted, brands will pay for access to them.

Traditional vs. Digital: Selling the printed copy earns money once per sale and costs money to print and bind. Digital income can repeat every month, reach readers anywhere and give us data on what readers click, so we can sell ads more precisely.

PRESENTATION OUTLINE (Day 5)
Intro and demographic (Editor-in-Chief), article highlights (Lead and Opinion Writers), ads and design choices (Art Director), pricing and digital revenue plan (whole group), questions.
