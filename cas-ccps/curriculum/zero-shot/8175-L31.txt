Survey Analysis: Data Driven Decisions

1. The Data: Three Points of Interest
The first finding that surprised me was that 62% of respondents (54 out of 87) said they found out about league games through word of mouth, while only 18% said they saw it on the league's Instagram page. I expected social media to be the main source, so this shows our online promotion is not reaching people as well as we thought.

The second interesting data point was the start time. 71% of respondents said the 4:00 p.m. weekday start time was "difficult" or "very difficult" to make, mostly because of work, practice or transportation. Only 9% said it was "easy."

The third finding was about the overall experience. Even though people had trouble with scheduling, 80% rated their enjoyment of the games as a 4 or 5 out of 5. In the open responses, many people mentioned the music, the competitive games and the halftime contests as highlights. This tells me people love the event once they get there, but the timing is the barrier.

2. The Suggestion: Action Plan
Our league should move most games from the 4:00 p.m. weekday start to 6:30 p.m. on weekdays, or to Saturday afternoons, and post the full schedule at least two weeks in advance on Instagram, the school website and the morning announcements.

3. The Justification
The survey data supports this change. Since 71% of respondents said the 4:00 p.m. start was hard to make and 80% said they enjoyed the games when they attended, the problem is access, not interest. A later start time would let more players and fans attend, which should increase attendance and participation. In the open-ended responses, 23 people specifically mentioned the time, with comments like "I would come if it was after practice" and "My parents can't drive me until after work." Also, because only 18% learned about games on Instagram, posting the schedule earlier and in more places would help more people plan ahead.

Protect the Data
I did not include any respondent's name or email in this analysis. Survey data must be kept private because people answer honestly only when they trust that their responses cannot be traced back to them, and sharing personal information without permission could embarrass or harm them.
