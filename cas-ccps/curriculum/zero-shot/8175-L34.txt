Interview Skills Practice Plan

My Practice Plan
Over the two weeks before an interview, I will practice my body language, tone and confidence every day. First, I will research common interview questions and write short answers using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Next, I will record myself on my phone answering three questions per day and watch the videos to check my posture, eye contact and whether I say "um" or "like" too often. I will practice a firm handshake and sitting up straight with my hands relaxed on the table. I will also do two mock interviews with a family member or teacher, asking for honest feedback on my tone and energy. The night before, I will lay out professional clothes, and before the interview I will take deep breaths to stay calm and confident.

Reflection

What did you learn from the video that was not expected?
I did not expect how much of a first impression is made in the first few seconds, before you even answer a question. The video explained that interviewers notice your posture, handshake, eye contact and smile right away. I was also surprised that nervous habits like tapping your foot, crossing your arms or looking down can make you seem uninterested even if your answers are good. Another surprise was that it is okay to pause and think before answering instead of rushing.

How did this experience help shape your future career plans?
This helped me realize that interview skills are something I can practice and improve, just like a sport. I want to work in sports marketing or event management, which are jobs where you constantly meet clients, fans and coworkers, so confidence and communication are essential. Now I plan to take more opportunities to speak in front of others, like presenting in class and joining DECA competitions, so I am ready for internships and job interviews.

What is something specific you could do that would help you in an interview setting?
One specific thing I can do is prepare a strong answer to "Tell me about yourself" and practice it out loud until it sounds natural, not memorized. I will also bring two or three thoughtful questions to ask the interviewer at the end, such as "What does a successful first month look like in this role?" That shows I am prepared and genuinely interested in the job.
