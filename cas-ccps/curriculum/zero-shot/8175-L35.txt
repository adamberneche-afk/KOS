The Google Interview Challenge

Question 1: Please tell me a little bit about yourself.
Final Answer: I'm a junior at Clover Hill High School with a strong interest in marketing and the business side of sports. I've taken Sports and Entertainment Marketing, where I planned a full album launch campaign and a naming rights proposal. Outside of class, I work as a crew member at Chick-fil-A, where I've learned to stay calm and friendly during busy rushes, and I'm a member of DECA, where I placed second at districts. I'm organized, I communicate well, and I'm excited to bring that energy to a team where I can keep learning and contribute to real projects.
Feedback and change: The tool showed that my first answer did not cover "lessons learned" as a talking point. I added what I learned from my job about staying calm under pressure and connected my experience to what I could bring to the team.

Question 2: Tell me about a time you had to work with someone who was difficult to work with.
Final Answer: During a group project in marketing class, one teammate wasn't completing his part of the presentation and wasn't responding to our group chat. Instead of complaining to the teacher right away, I talked to him one-on-one before class. I learned he was overwhelmed with work hours and didn't understand his section. I helped him break it into smaller tasks and we set a check-in two days before the deadline. He finished his slides, and our group earned an A. I learned that asking questions first usually solves the problem faster than assuming the worst.
Feedback and change: The tool pointed out that I used "like" and "um" often and didn't give a clear result. I removed filler words and added the outcome (we earned an A) using the STAR method.

Question 3: Describe a project you worked on that you're proud of.
Final Answer: I'm most proud of helping plan our school's Pink Out football game. I was in charge of social media promotion, so I designed graphics in Canva, scheduled posts for two weeks leading up to the game and coordinated with the student section leaders. We sold over 200 pink T-shirts and raised more than $1,200 for cancer research. The project taught me how to plan a timeline, work with a team and measure whether marketing actually worked.
Feedback and change: The tool's "job-related terms" feature showed I used few specific skills. I added terms like "designed," "scheduled," "coordinated" and real numbers to show my impact.

Question 4: What is one of your weaknesses, and how are you working on it?
Final Answer: One of my weaknesses is that I sometimes take on too many tasks at once because I want to help everyone. Last fall that made a few deadlines stressful. To fix it, I started using a planner and Google Calendar to map out my week and learned to say, "I can help with that after Thursday," instead of saying yes to everything. Now I meet my deadlines more consistently and the quality of my work has improved.
Feedback and change: My original answer described the weakness but not the improvement. The tool highlighted that I should explain the steps I took, so I added the planner, calendar and the results.

Question 5: Why are you interested in this position?
Final Answer: I'm interested in this position because it combines my two biggest interests: marketing and connecting with people. I've already practiced creating campaigns, designing social content and talking to customers, and this role would let me use those skills while learning from professionals. I also admire how your organization builds a loyal community and fan experience, and I'd love to help create promotions that bring people together. Long term, I want a career in sports and entertainment marketing, and this is the right place to start building that experience.
Feedback and change: The tool noted my first answer was short and focused only on what I wanted. I expanded it to include what I could offer the employer and why the organization specifically appeals to me.
