The Game Day Takeover: Cavalier Shop Pop-Up

Day 1

Part 1: The Run of Show (Friday home game, kickoff at 7:00 PM)
4:30 PM - Shift lead and setup crew arrive; pick up inventory, cash box and card reader from the store.
4:45 PM - Set up table, tent, signs and displays near the main gate; test card reader on the stadium Wi-Fi.
5:30 PM - Count starting cash and inventory; final check.
5:45 PM - Gates open. BUSY TIME begins.
5:45 to 6:55 PM - Peak sales before kickoff (warm-ups, student section arriving). BUSY TIME.
7:00 PM - Kickoff; sales slow down. Restock table from backup bins.
8:15 PM - Halftime. BUSY TIME (concession and bathroom traffic).
8:35 PM - Third quarter begins; half the staff takes a break.
9:15 PM - Sales stop at the start of the fourth quarter.
9:15 to 9:45 PM - Closeout: count money, run Z-out report, count remaining inventory.
9:45 PM - Pack up, clean area and return everything to the store.
My role and shift: Cashier, 5:15 PM to 8:45 PM (covering both busy times).

Part 2: The Manager's Checklist

Staffing Chart

Supply List
- Inventory: 48 Cavalier tees, 40 rally towels, 100 stickers, 24 beanies
- Starting cash: $150 in small bills and coins
- Card reader (charged) and backup phone hotspot
- Folding table, 10x10 pop-up tent, tablecloth
- Price signs and "Cavalier Shop" banner
- Bags, tape, zip ties, pens, inventory sheet
- Extension cord and portable battery

Event Goals
- Items sold: 90
- Revenue: $900
- Number of customers: 70
- Longest acceptable wait: 5 minutes

Risk and Backup Plan
Risk: The card reader won't connect to the stadium Wi-Fi.
Backup plan: Use a phone hotspot for the card reader, and if that fails, accept cash only and keep a written list of card customers who will pick up items at the store Monday. A sign will say "Cash and card accepted" so customers know ahead of time.

Part 4: The Results
Goal vs. actual:
- Items sold: goal 90, actual 104 (beat the goal)
- Revenue: goal $900, actual $1,046 (beat the goal)
- Customers: goal 70, actual 78 (beat the goal)
- Longest wait: goal 5 minutes, actual about 9 minutes at halftime (missed the goal)

My shift: I worked as cashier from 5:15 to 8:45. I greeted customers, ran the card reader and handled cash. One customer was a parent who wanted a beanie for her son but wasn't sure of the size. I let her know they were one size fits most, showed her the stretch, and she bought two. The biggest problem came at halftime, when the line got long and the card reader froze for a few minutes. The shift lead switched it to the phone hotspot while the runner took cash payments, and we got the line moving again.

Part 5: Keep and Change
Keep: Setting up at the main gate. We sold 61 items before kickoff because everyone walking in passed our table, which helped us beat our revenue goal.
Change: Add a second card reader and a second cashier at halftime. Our wait reached 9 minutes, almost double our goal, and some people left the line to get back to their seats. With two checkout spots, we could cut the wait in half and sell even more.

Key terms used: As event managers, we built a run of show and staffing chart, set goals before the event, followed the closeout steps, and completed the follow-up through recognition and evaluating our results.
