Executive Summary: Cavalier Shop Customer Survey Results

To: Cavalier Shop General Manager and CHHS Administration
From: Cavalier Shop Management Team
Subject: Survey findings and recommendation

Purpose
The Sports Management class surveyed 331 Clover Hill High School students about how they receive school news, whether supporting student activities matters to them, and what products they would like the Cavalier Shop to offer. This executive summary presents three key data points, one recommendation, and how the results support the store's marketing functions.

Part 1: Three Data Points

Data point 1: According to the survey, 68% of students selected CHHS Instagram as one of their top two sources of school news, while only 14% selected Morning Announcements. This quantitative data shows a clear trend: students get their news on social media, not from the PA system. The store currently relies heavily on announcements, which means many potential customers never hear about sales or new products.

Data point 2: When asked how important it is that their purchases fund CHHS student activities, 61% of students rated it a 4 or 5 on the 5-point scale, and the average rating was 3.8. This means that "every purchase supports student activities" is a strong selling point that the store should highlight on signs and at the register.

Data point 3: In the qualitative data from Question 3, 27% of written responses (about 89 of 331) asked for hoodies or quarter-zips in more colors and sizes, making it the most common theme. The next most common themes were snacks and drinks (18%) and custom sport-specific gear (12%). Students clearly want more cold-weather apparel options.

Part 2: One Change
I recommend that the Cavalier Shop move its main promotion to the CHHS Instagram by posting every new product and sale the morning it launches, starting with a "Hoodie Week" featuring two new hoodie colors and expanded sizes in December.

Part 3: Back It Up
This recommendation is supported by the data. Since 68% of students get school news from Instagram compared with only 14% from Morning Announcements, shifting promotion to Instagram will reach almost five times as many students. Featuring hoodies responds directly to the 27% of written suggestions that asked for them, the largest single request. Each post should also include the message "Your purchase funds CHHS student activities," since 61% of students said that matters to them. Operationally, this means a student employee would be responsible for creating and scheduling posts each week, and the store would reallocate time currently spent writing announcements to photographing products.

Part 4: One Survey, Every Function
Product/service management: Question 3 shows the store should expand hoodies and sport-specific gear, because those were the most requested items.
Promotion: Question 1 shows the store should focus its promotion on Instagram and Canvas instead of Morning Announcements.
Pricing: Our survey did not ask about price, so a future survey should ask, "What is the most you would pay for a Cavalier hoodie?" to help the store set prices students will accept.

Conclusion
These marketing functions all depend on accurate information. Rather than guessing, the Cavalier Shop can use these results to stock the products students want, reach them where they actually get their news, and emphasize the message that matters to them. Following this recommendation should increase both traffic and sales during the winter season.
