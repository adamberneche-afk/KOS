The Signature Item: The "Squirrel Stack" Nacho Helmet

Venue: The Diamond, home of the Richmond Flying Squirrels

Part 1: Product
Our team's signature item is the Squirrel Stack: a pile of tortilla chips topped with pulled pork from a local Richmond barbecue spot, queso, jalapeños and a drizzle of sweet chili sauce, served in a red mini souvenir Flying Squirrels batting helmet. What makes it special is that it is tied to Richmond (local barbecue) and to the team (the helmet in Squirrels colors with the Nutzy mascot on the side). Fans can't get it anywhere else, and the helmet becomes a keepsake they take home.

Part 2: Price
Price: $15
Estimated cost to make: chips $0.60, pulled pork $1.80, queso and toppings $0.90, souvenir helmet $1.70, for a total of $5.00.
Profit per item: $15 − $5 = $10.

Fans will pay $15 because the perceived value is higher than just a plate of nachos. It is big enough to share, it uses a local favorite, and they get a souvenir helmet that their kids can keep. Regular nachos at the ballpark cost around $9, so for $6 more fans get a bigger portion and something to take home.

Part 3: Place
The Squirrel Stack will be sold at only one stand: the "Nutzy's Kitchen" stand on the first-base concourse. We chose one spot because it makes the item feel special and a little scarce, and it keeps the kitchen focused on making it well. Fans can also pre-order it in the Flying Squirrels team app and pick it up at a fast lane at that stand, so they don't miss innings waiting in line. Ordering in the app fits our fans because many of them already use the app for mobile tickets, and it lets the team see how many are selling each game.

Part 4: Promotion
We will launch the Squirrel Stack on Opening Night. In the third inning, Nutzy will "taste test" one on the video board while the crowd votes on whether he can finish it. The first 100 fans who post a photo of their Squirrel Stack on Instagram with #SquirrelStack get a free helmet refill at their next game. This gets fans talking on social media and brings them back for a second game.

Part 5: How They Connect
If we doubled the price to $30, the other three Ps would have to change. The product would need to be bigger or more special, like a full-size helmet or a limited-edition color each month, so the perceived value matches the price. The place might need to change to a premium area, like the club seats, where fans expect to pay more. Promotion would need to be stronger, such as advertising it as a collector's item, or else most fans would skip it and we would sell fewer. That shows how the marketing mix works together: changing one P changes all of them.

Part 6: Your Part
In our team pitch, I will present Price, including the cost breakdown, the $10 profit per item and why fans will see it as worth $15.
