Fan Persona Profile: The Parkers at a Richmond Flying Squirrels Game

Part 1: The Persona

The Parkers are a family of four who live in Chesterfield: Mike (38), a project manager, Lisa (36), a nurse who works three 12-hour shifts a week, and their kids, Ella (8) and Ben (5). After paying the mortgage, groceries, gas, car payments and daycare, they have about $200 a month in discretionary income for entertainment.

A typical week: Monday through Friday are packed with work, school, homework and Ella's soccer practice on Tuesdays and Thursdays. Lisa often works weekend shifts, so free family time is rare. Friday nights are usually pizza and a movie at home because everyone is tired. Saturdays are soccer games and errands. They want to do something special together at least once or twice a month, but it has to be easy, kid-friendly and not drain the budget.

Cost of one outing to a Flying Squirrels game at The Diamond:
- 4 general admission tickets: about $12 each = $48
- Parking: $5
- Food (4 hot dogs, 4 drinks, popcorn): about $45
- Souvenir (a small toy or cap for the kids): about $15
- Total: about $113, more than half of their $200 monthly budget.

Part 2: The Motivator

Top motivator: family bonding.

The Parkers barely get time together during the week, so what they want most is a memory they all share. They don't care much about the final score, and Ben won't sit still for nine innings. What gets them off the couch is the kids running the bases after the game, Nutzy the mascot, fireworks and the chance to do something together that they can't do at home. A big TV can show the game, but it can't give their kids that experience.

Part 3: The Competition

Indirect competitor: Dave & Buster's at Short Pump.

Dave & Buster's is not baseball, but it competes for the same Friday night and the same discretionary income. It's indoors, so weather doesn't matter, it's open late and the kids love the arcade games and prizes. The parents can get dinner and the kids can play. It's a real threat because it feels like a sure thing: no rain delays, no long innings for a bored 5-year-old. A night there with food and game cards can easily cost $100 to $120, the same money the Parkers would spend at The Diamond. Unlike direct competition, such as another baseball game, this competitor wins by offering a completely different kind of fun.

Part 4: The Winning Strategy

The Squirrels Family Friday Pack: $79 for a family of four.

What's included:
- 4 reserved seats in a family section near the kids' play area
- 4 hot dogs, 4 drinks and 1 bag of popcorn
- 1 free kids' activity wristband for the inflatables and speed-pitch booth
- A post-game "Kids Run the Bases" pass and Friday fireworks
- A $5 game-day coupon for the team store

Why it wins:
- Budget: the pack saves the Parkers about $25 compared with buying everything separately. With parking, the outing costs about $84, leaving well over half their monthly budget for another outing. That's cheaper than a typical night at Dave & Buster's.
- Motivator: everything in the pack is about family bonding. The kids have something to do every inning, the whole family runs the bases together, and the fireworks give them a shared memory and photos.
- Easy decision: with food included, the Parkers know exactly what the night costs before they leave home, so there are no surprises when the kids get hungry.

How the event would promote it: posts on Chesterfield parent Facebook groups and the team's Instagram showing kids running the bases, and flyers sent home through local elementary schools and youth soccer leagues.
