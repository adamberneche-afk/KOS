The Free Agent Portfolio

Part 1: The Post-Event Evaluation

Event: Clover Hill's Homecoming football game and Spirit Week, which I attended and helped with by working a shift at the Cavalier Shop table at the game.

SWOT Analysis

Strengths (internal)
- Strong student turnout: the student section was packed, and spirit week themes like Pajama Day and Throwback Thursday got a lot of participation.
- The Cavalier Shop table sold out of rally towels before halftime, which showed our product matched what fans wanted.

Weaknesses (internal)
- Long lines at the concession stand and the shop table, with some fans waiting more than 15 minutes and missing part of the game.
- We only had one card reader, so when it lost Wi-Fi, we could only take cash for about 20 minutes.

Opportunities (external)
- Local businesses could sponsor a spirit week day or the halftime show, bringing in more revenue.
- Pre-ordering homecoming merchandise online during spirit week would reduce game-day lines.

Threats (external)
- Weather: rain or cold could lower attendance next year.
- Competing events the same night, like other schools' homecoming games or a VCU basketball game, could pull families away.

What I would measure next time (KPI): average wait time at the Cavalier Shop table and concession stand. If we add a second card reader and pre-orders, we can see whether the wait drops below 5 minutes. I would also track merchandise revenue to calculate ROI on the items we printed.

Part 4: My STAR Stories

STAR Story 1: Solving a problem at the Cavalier Shop
- Situation: During a lunch shift at the Cavalier Shop, a big line formed just as the register's receipt printer jammed.
- Task: As shift manager, I needed to keep customers happy and keep sales moving.
- Action: I asked one teammate to start a second line on the backup iPad, offered digital receipts, and told the line exactly what was happening while I cleared the jam.
- Result: We kept the line moving and lost no sales. I learned that staying calm and communicating clearly keeps customers patient.

STAR Story 2: Fixing a budget
- Situation: In our $10,000 Tip-Off Budget project, my first budget showed a small loss instead of the 15% profit margin we needed.
- Task: I had to find a way to reach the goal without cutting required expenses like security and the athletic trainer.
- Action: I tested different prices in Google Sheets and raised the team fee and concession price, while keeping the ticket price low so attendance wouldn't drop.
- Result: My final budget reached a 15.0% margin. I learned to use formulas to test "what if" scenarios quickly.

STAR Story 3: Handling rejection in a negotiation
- Situation: In our sponsorship role-play, my partner played a business owner who said, "Teenagers don't spend money at my business."
- Task: I needed to stay calm and keep the conversation going instead of giving up.
- Action: I used the Feel, Felt, Found method and explained that our home games bring hundreds of local parents and families, not just students.
- Result: My partner agreed to a trial sponsorship for the basketball season. I learned that an objection is a question, not the end of the conversation.
