The Brand Revival Pitch: Richmond Kickers

Part 1: The Bundle

The Kickers Crew Pack (for college students at VCU and the University of Richmond)

What's included and the price of each item on its own:
- 4 general admission tickets: 4 x $15 = $60
- 4 fountain drinks: 4 x $5 = $20
- 2 large orders of loaded fries to share: 2 x $9 = $18
- 4 Kickers student rally scarves: 4 x $10 = $40
- Total if bought separately: $138

Bundle price: $99 for four people (about $25 each)
Fan savings: $39

Why this group would buy it: college students have limited discretionary income and go out in groups, so the game becomes the social plan for the night. The price per person is about what they would spend on a movie and snacks, and the scarves make them feel like part of a student supporters' section, which looks great in photos and videos on social media. For the Kickers, the bundle sells food and merchandise before fans walk in the gate and builds a habit of coming with friends.

Part 2: The Brand Extension

Kickers Soccer & Fitness Summer Camp, plus a Kickers Youth Academy Clinic Series

The Kickers could launch a year-round youth and family program that has nothing to do with the pro team playing matches: weekend "Kickers Fit" family workout sessions and summer day camps for kids ages 6 to 13, taught by Kickers coaches and visited by current players.

How it uses the brand: the camp uses the Kickers name, colors and logo on camp shirts, water bottles and certificates, and players make appearances. Parents trust the Kickers name as professional and local, so they feel safe signing up their kids. Every camper goes home in a Kickers shirt, which makes them future fans.

Who would buy it: Richmond-area families with young kids who play youth soccer or need summer childcare and activities. At $225 per week per camper, with 60 campers a week for 8 weeks, the camp could bring in about $108,000 a summer.

Licensing opportunity: the Kickers could also license their logo to a local apparel company to make the camp gear and retail fan apparel, earning a royalty of around 10% on every item sold, without having to manufacture anything themselves.

Part 3: The Naming Rights

Area: the family zone, the grassy area with inflatables, small-sided goals and the kids' play area.

Business: Children's Hospital of Richmond at VCU.

New name: The Children's Hospital of Richmond at VCU Family Zone

Why it fits: the family zone is where kids play and families spend time together, and Children's Hospital of Richmond is a trusted local brand focused on keeping kids healthy and active. The hospital reaches exactly the audience it wants, local parents, and the Kickers can add health and safety features like a first aid station and hydration stations with the hospital's branding. The name would appear on tickets, stadium maps, signs, social posts and announcements, which is why naming rights are worth much more than a single banner. The sponsor fits the team's family-friendly image and has no connection to alcohol, tobacco, vaping or gambling.

Part 4: The Life Cycle

The Kickers are in the maturity stage of the product life cycle: they are one of the oldest pro soccer clubs in the country, people in Richmond know them, and ticket sales have been flat for three years. If they don't find new ways to grow, they risk sliding into decline. My three ideas grow the product mix without depending on more regular ticket sales: the Crew Pack bundle increases spending per fan on food and merchandise, the summer camp brand extension creates a new year-round business and future fans, and the naming-rights deal brings in sponsorship revenue, giving the Kickers new growth from the same loyal brand.
