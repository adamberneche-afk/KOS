The NIL Activation: Cavalier Rally Towels

Product: the Cavalier Shop's custom sublimation-printed rally towels, designed and printed by Clover Hill students, $8 each.

Part 1: The Match

Public figure: Justin Verlander, the MLB pitcher who grew up in Goochland County and played high school baseball in the Richmond area before starring at Old Dominion University.

His followers: Verlander's audience includes baseball fans nationwide, but a big, loyal part of it is Virginia and Richmond-area sports fans who are proud of a hometown athlete. They include high school and youth baseball players, their parents and coaches, the same people who go to Clover Hill games and buy school spirit gear.

Why the audience matches: our customers are local students, parents and fans who want to show school pride at games. A post from a Richmond-area native who started out playing high school ball here sends the message "I was once in your seats, cheering for my school," which connects with exactly the people who buy rally towels.

Why he's a better fit than someone with more followers: a global superstar with tens of millions of followers would reach far more people, but almost none of them live near Clover Hill or would ever shop at the Cavalier Shop. Verlander's endorsement carries hometown credibility. Fans in Richmond see him as one of their own, so they are more likely to trust and act on his recommendation of a local, student-made product.

Part 2: Macro or Micro?

Verlander is a macro-influencer, with a following in the millions across Instagram and X.

One advantage: reach and credibility. One post would put the Cavalier Shop in front of more people in a day than we could reach in a year, and his name would draw local news coverage, which is free publicity for our class.

One risk: cost and fit. A macro-influencer is expensive, and most of his followers live outside Richmond, so much of the reach is wasted on people who can't buy our product. There's also a risk the post feels like an ad to his followers if it doesn't sound like him. To reduce the risk, we would focus the post on his hometown story and use a trackable promo code so we know exactly what it earns. If the cost were too high, a local micro-influencer such as a Richmond high school coach could be a backup.

Part 3: The Post

Instagram post mockup (designed in Canva):

Image: a photo of a packed Clover Hill student section waving bright blue-and-gold Cavalier rally towels under the stadium lights, with an inset photo of Verlander holding up one of the towels and smiling. The Cavalier Shop logo is in the bottom corner.

Top banner text on the image: #ad | Paid partnership with the Cavalier Shop

Caption:
"#ad Nothing beats Friday nights in Richmond. That's where it all started for me: high school ball, the student section going crazy, the whole town showing up. Shoutout to the Clover Hill students who designed and printed these rally towels themselves. Real talent right there. Grab yours and bring the noise this season. Use code VERLANDER35 at the Cavalier Shop for $2 off. Go Cavs!"

Call to action: "Use code VERLANDER35 at the Cavalier Shop for $2 off."

FTC disclosure: #ad appears at the very start of the caption and in the banner on the image, plus Instagram's "Paid partnership" label, so followers see it immediately without tapping "more."

Label on the mockup: "For educational purposes only."

Part 4: Measure It

I will know the deal worked by tracking:
- Promo code redemptions: how many times VERLANDER35 is used at the Cavalier Shop. This is the most direct measure.
- Rally towel sales: total towels sold in the two weeks after the post compared with the two weeks before.
- Revenue and ROI: towel revenue from the code compared with what the endorsement cost.
- Engagement: likes, comments, shares and saves on the post, plus new followers on the Cavalier Shop's Instagram account.
- Website or QR code visits: clicks on the link in his bio or scans of the QR code to our online store.

If code redemptions and towel sales go up noticeably and the deal earns back more than it cost, the endorsement was a success.

Why this choice fits our values: Verlander has a professional, family-friendly public image and is not known for explicit content or for promoting alcohol, tobacco, vaping or gambling, so he fits CHHS's core values. Since 2021, NIL rules let college athletes earn from their name, image and likeness, and this deal shows how the same endorsement idea works for a professional athlete promoting a local school product.
