The Sponsorship Negotiation: Clover Hill Cavaliers Basketball Banner Sponsorship ($500)

Part 1: The CRM

Google Sheet: "CHHS Basketball Sponsorship CRM" (link shared with my teacher)

Columns: Business Name | Contact Person and Title | Contact Information | Why They're a Good Fit | Status

1. Mama J's Kitchen
- Contact: Owner and founder
- Contact information: general contact form on the restaurant's website; main line on its Google business listing
- Why a good fit: a well-known Black-owned and woman-owned family restaurant in Richmond's Jackson Ward that serves home-style Southern food. Our basketball games draw families from all over the Richmond area, and a banner would introduce them to a local favorite for a post-game family dinner. It also builds a community partnership with a business that represents Richmond's diverse communities.
- Status: Ready to pitch

2. Plaza Azteca (Midlothian location)
- Contact: General manager
- Contact information: restaurant phone number and contact form on its website
- Why a good fit: a family Mexican restaurant close to Clover Hill that serves the area's growing Hispanic and Latino community. Many CHHS families are already customers, and it is a natural spot for team dinners and post-game celebrations.
- Status: Researching

3. Sweet Frog Premium Frozen Yogurt (Midlothian location)
- Contact: Store manager
- Contact information: store phone number on its website's location page
- Why a good fit: a frozen yogurt chain founded in Richmond that is popular with students and younger kids. Game-night crowds include lots of students and families, which is exactly its customer base, and a "show your ticket for a discount" offer would be easy to track.
- Status: Ready to pitch

4. Chick-fil-A (Midlothian Turnpike location)
- Contact: Franchise owner/operator
- Contact information: restaurant phone number and the location's community-involvement request form online
- Why a good fit: locally owned and operated franchises often support schools and youth sports, and its customers are families and students who live near the school.
- Status: Pitched

5. Richmond Raceway
- Contact: Community relations manager
- Contact information: community relations request form on its website
- Why a good fit: a major Richmond-area entertainment venue looking to attract younger fans and families. A banner at our games puts the raceway in front of local families who could become future race fans.
- Status: Researching

Diverse partnerships: Mama J's Kitchen (Black- and woman-owned) and Plaza Azteca (serving Richmond's Hispanic and Latino community) meet the requirement and reflect the diversity of the CHHS fan base.

Note: this is a practice CRM. I did not contact any of these businesses.

Part 2: The Objection Script (Feel, Felt, Found)

Objection 1: "Teenagers don't spend money at my business."
- Feel: "I understand how you feel. You want your advertising to reach people who actually buy from you, and that makes sense."
- Felt: "Other local business owners felt the same way when we first approached them."
- Found: "What they found is that our home games aren't just students. Parents, grandparents, younger siblings and alumni fill the stands. Those are adults who live right around the school and make the family's dining and shopping decisions, and they see your banner at every home game all season."

Objection 2: "I already sponsor another school."
- Feel: "I completely understand, and it's great that you're already supporting local students."
- Felt: "Other sponsors felt like one school was enough at first, too."
- Found: "What they found is that each school reaches a different neighborhood of families. Sponsoring Clover Hill puts your name in front of a whole new group of customers in Midlothian who may not know you yet, for less than the cost of most local ads. And when we play that other school, your name is in front of both fan bases."

Part 3: The Reflection

Opening pitch: "Hi, I'm the director of corporate partnerships for Clover Hill basketball. For $500, your business can have a banner on the gym wall for every home game this season, plus a thank-you announcement on the PA and a shout-out on our team's Instagram, reaching hundreds of local families each night."

Objection 1: My partner, playing the owner of a family restaurant, said, "Teenagers don't spend money here." I used my Feel, Felt, Found answer and pointed out that parents and families make up most of the crowd.

Objection 2: My partner said, "$500 is too much right now." I said I understood that every dollar matters, that other small businesses felt the same way, and that what they found was that it works out to about $50 a game for a whole season of exposure. I also offered to add a coupon in the game program so they could measure results.

How it ended: my partner still wasn't ready to commit to the full $500, so I pivoted and offered a half-season banner for $300. My partner agreed, so I closed the deal: "Great, can we put you down as our banner sponsor starting with the first home game in January?"

How it felt: when my partner first said no, I felt nervous and a little discouraged because I thought my pitch was strong. I stayed calm by taking a breath, listening to what they were really worried about and reminding myself that an objection is just a concern, not a final answer. Using Feel, Felt, Found helped me respond respectfully instead of arguing, and pivoting to a smaller package showed I was focused on their needs. I closed a deal, even though it was a smaller one, and I learned that flexibility can turn a no into a yes and move a prospect forward in the sales pipeline.
