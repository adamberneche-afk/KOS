The Sales Management Playbook: River City Blades

Part 1: The Quota

Team goal: $60,000 / $750 per plan = 80 plans needed next month.

Last month the team sold 24 + 19 + 15 + 6 = 64 plans, so we need 16 more plans than last month, about a 25% increase.

Quotas:
- Jordan: 26 plans (last month 24, +2). Jordan is our top seller with the strongest track record, so a small increase is challenging but very reachable.
- Sam: 22 plans (last month 19, +3). Sam is consistent and close behind Jordan, so a stretch of three more plans is realistic with good leads.
- Alex: 18 plans (last month 15, +3). Alex has been falling behind, so I'm raising the goal only slightly and pairing it with extra support (see Part 4).
- Taylor: 14 plans (last month 6, +8). Taylor started only two weeks ago, so 6 plans in half a month works out to about 12 for a full month. Fourteen is a stretch that rewards growth without setting Taylor up to fail.

Check: 26 + 22 + 18 + 14 = 80 plans x $750 = $60,000. The quotas add up exactly to the team goal.

Each sales quota is based on the rep's experience and past results. Giving everyone 20 would be too easy for Jordan and nearly impossible for Taylor, which could discourage our newest rep.

Part 2: The Sales Contest

"Power Play Month"

How reps win:
- Individual "Hat Trick" award: every rep who hits their personal quota wins. Because quotas are different, every rep, including Taylor, has a fair shot.
- Weekly "Goal of the Week": the rep who sells the most plans each week gets recognized at Monday's team meeting.
- Team "Full Strength" bonus: if the whole team reaches 80 plans, everyone wins.

Prizes:
- Hat Trick winners: a $150 bonus on top of regular commission.
- Goal of the Week: the reserved parking spot by the arena entrance for the next week and their name on the "Power Play Board" in the office (non-financial).
- Full Strength team prize: a team lunch with the head coach and a chance to ride the Zamboni during a home game intermission (non-financial).

Why it will work: slow months drain sales motivation, so the contest adds energy and fun. Because it rewards personal quotas and the whole team, not just the top seller, Alex and Taylor won't give up after the first week. The non-financial prizes give public recognition and unique experiences that reps will talk about, and the team prize encourages reps to help each other instead of competing for leads.

Part 3: The Ethics Policy

Rule 1: Always give the full price, including taxes and fees, before taking payment.
Problem it prevents: customers feeling tricked by surprise charges at checkout, which leads to angry calls, refunds and fans who never buy again.

Rule 2: Never promise anything we can't guarantee, like player meet-and-greets, playoff tickets or specific giveaways.
Problem it prevents: broken promises that damage trust in the whole organization and could create legal problems if a customer feels they were misled.

Rule 3: Describe every seat's view honestly and never use fake deadlines or high-pressure tactics.
Problem it prevents: customers who feel pressured into buying the wrong plan or end up behind a railing they were told was a great view. Instead, we use consultative selling: learn what the fan wants and recommend the plan that fits their budget. Honest reps earn renewals and referrals, which matter more than one quick sale.

Part 4: Support a Struggling Rep

To help Alex succeed, I will provide two support activities. First, sales training through weekly one-on-one role-plays: I'll listen to a few of Alex's recorded calls, point out what's working, like a friendly approach, and coach Alex on specific weak spots, such as asking open-ended questions and handling the "it's too expensive" objection. We'll practice those steps together until they feel natural. Second, sales support through better leads and tools: I'll give Alex a list of warm leads, such as fans who bought single-game tickets three or more times last season, along with a one-page comparison of our ticket plans to share with customers. We'll meet briefly each Friday to review progress in the CRM and set a small goal for the next week. This gives Alex constructive feedback and a clear plan, not just a reminder of being behind.

As director of ticket sales, my role is to set fair goals, keep the team motivated, give them the training and tools they need, and make sure every sale is made ethically.
