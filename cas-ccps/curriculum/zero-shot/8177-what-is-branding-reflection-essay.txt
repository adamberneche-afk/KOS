Part 2: Write It

What Is Branding?

Think about the last time you saw a red can and knew it was Coca-Cola before you could even read the label. How did you know? That is branding at work. Branding is everything a business does, the same way every time, so that people recognize it and feel a certain way about it. A brand is not just a name or a picture. It is the reputation and the feeling people connect with a business. In my marketing class this quarter, I learned that branding shows up in the way something looks, what makes it different, and even how it is priced and sold.

My first example is the Brand Board I made at the start of the quarter. A brand board is one page that shows a brand's logo, colors and fonts all together. A logo is the symbol or design that identifies a brand, like the Nike swoosh. Together, the logo, colors and fonts make up the brand identity, which just means the look a brand always uses. When I made my board, I had to pick two main colors and stick with them. At first I wanted to add more colors because they looked cool, but I learned that changing your look all the time confuses people. If a brand looks different every time you see it, you never start to remember it. Keeping the same look over and over is what makes a brand easy to spot.

My second example is the company description I wrote for the Cavalier Shop, our school store. A company description is a short paragraph that explains who a business is and why it matters. The hardest part was figuring out what made the store different from Amazon or Walmart. Those stores are bigger and cheaper, so why would anyone shop at ours? The answer was our unique selling proposition, which is the one thing a business offers that nobody else does. For the Cavalier Shop, it is official Clover Hill gear made by students right here in school. This taught me that a brand is not only a look. It is also a promise about what makes you special. I also had to think about the target market, which means the people most likely to buy from you. For the Cavalier Shop, that is students, parents and teachers who want to show school pride.

My third example is the signature item my team invented using the 4 Ps. The 4 Ps are product, price, place and promotion, the four big choices a business makes about anything it sells. We created a nacho dish served in a mini souvenir helmet for a baseball team. We priced it at $15, sold it at only one stand, and launched it with the mascot eating one on the video board. I learned that every one of those choices said something about the brand. A higher price and a single stand made it feel special and fun, which matched the team's family-friendly, playful brand. If we had sold it cheap at every stand, it would have felt ordinary.

So why does branding matter? For a business, branding builds brand loyalty, which is when people keep choosing the same brand again and again, even when they could buy something cheaper somewhere else. Loyal customers come back, tell their friends and are less likely to switch. That is how small businesses survive against giant companies. Branding matters to me, too, because I am a customer every day. Now when I pick a pair of shoes or a drink, I notice when I am paying for the brand and not just the product. Understanding branding helps me make smarter choices as a shopper, and it helps me see how much thought goes into every logo, price and ad around me. Branding is really about trust: when a business keeps its promises the same way every time, people trust it, and that trust is what keeps them coming back.

Part 3: Check It

Sentence a beginner might not understand: "Together, the logo, colors and fonts make up the brand identity."
Rewritten: "When you put the logo, the colors and the fonts together, you get what marketers call brand identity, which is just the look a brand always uses so you can recognize it."

Sentence a beginner might not understand: "That is how small businesses survive against giant companies."
Rewritten: "When people keep coming back to a small business because they like it, that business can stay open even when huge stores nearby sell things for less."
