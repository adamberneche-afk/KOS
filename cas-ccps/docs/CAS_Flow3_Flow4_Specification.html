Studio Flow Specification

Flow 3Warm-Up Generation

Student-specific cognitive entry points generated from lesson context and individual learning history. Four prompt archetypes. One doc per student per class day.

Trigger: WarmUpQueue PENDING 6am class-day cron Gemini · temperature 0.7 ~30s per student Anchor-aware · v1.1 Shadow matrix · pacing guide
What this flow does

One prompt, one student, one class

Flow 3 reads a WarmUpQueue row containing two JSON snapshots — the lesson context and the student's profile — and generates a single warm-up prompt personalized to that student's learning history. It creates a Google Doc in the student's warm-up folder, stamps it with the correct zone structure, shares it, and marks the queue row DELIVERED.

◈
The core design constraint: The warm-up is not a review question and not a preview question. It is a cognitive entry point — something that makes the student think before the lesson begins, creates a productive tension that the lesson resolves, and is interesting enough that a student who doesn't have to engage probably will anyway. It should never feel like homework.

Modelgemini-proSufficient for structured creative generation. gemini-ultra overkill at this scale.
Temperature0.7High enough for genuine variation between students. Low enough to stay on-topic and coherent.

Personalization logic

Four prompt archetypes

Flow 3 selects one of four prompt angles based on the student's profile. The selection is deterministic — given the same profile, the same archetype is always chosen. The content within the archetype varies with temperature.

Archetype selection now uses a two-layer model. The shadow matrix is checked first — if cross-unit confidence has crossed 0.75 for a student × archetype combination, that archetype is used directly. If confidence is below threshold or no shadow matrix data exists, the decision table below applies. The decision table is the fallback, not the primary selector for students with established history.

ArchetypeTrigger condition (decision table — used when shadow confidence < 0.75)Shadow matrix override
PARADOX avg_engagement ≥ 2.5 AND strengths contain analysis or critical_thinking AND gaps contain application Used directly when shadow cross_confidence ≥ 0.75 for PARADOX on this student in the current unit type
CONCRETE SCENARIO avg_engagement ≥ 1.5 AND gaps contain analysis or critical_thinking AND strengths contain application Used directly when shadow cross_confidence ≥ 0.75 for CONCRETE SCENARIO
BRIDGE competency_gaps is non-empty AND today's lesson introduces a duty area not yet covered this term Shadow matrix weights toward BRIDGE for early-unit warm-ups (within_confidence < 0.3) regardless of decision table
PROVOCATION avg_engagement ≥ 2.5 AND extra_credit_count ≥ 1 AND evaluation_signals show no persistent gaps Used directly when shadow cross_confidence ≥ 0.75 for PROVOCATION
◈
Shadow matrix selection note in Mode A prompt: When the shadow matrix overrides the decision table, Flow 3 receives a {shadow_archetype_note} placeholder explaining why: "Shadow matrix suggests PARADOX (72% confidence, 5 warm-ups in unit S1-U3, trend: rising)." This string is computed by getStudentProfileSnapshot_() in Script 23 at 3:30am queue-build time — not at generation time — and embedded in the student_profile_snapshot JSON on the WarmUpQueue row. Flow 3 reads it from the queue row with no additional Sheets reads. Null when cross_confidence < 0.75 or no shadow history exists for the current unit. Snapshot isolation fully preserved.
◈
Fallback order when no archetype matches (decision table): BRIDGE → CONCRETE SCENARIO → PARADOX. A student with no evaluation history and no warm-up history receives a BRIDGE prompt — all competencies are technically gaps and the anchor's prior connection field provides the bridge material. The pacing guide's prior_lesson_connection ensures BRIDGE has meaningful content even on the first day of a new unit.
Archetype 01 · Paradox

Surfaces contradiction

For students who think deeply but stay at the conceptual level. Presents two things that both seem true but can't both be right — at least not without nuance the lesson provides. The student walks in already wrestling with the tension.

Nike's Air Jordan line became one of the most valuable brands in sports history — but Nike deliberately limits how many pairs get released. If fewer people can buy your product, how can it be more valuable? What does that tell you about what "value" actually means in a market?
Archetype 02 · Concrete Scenario

Grounds abstraction

For students who work well with tangible problems but struggle to lift to the conceptual level. Presents a specific, real-sounding situation that requires judgment. The abstraction emerges from the scenario, not the other way around.

You're managing sponsorship for a mid-size esports team. A major energy drink brand offers you a deal — but their product has faced public criticism for targeting underage consumers. The money would fund your next three tournaments. What do you do, and why?
Archetype 03 · Bridge

Connects prior to new

For students entering a duty area they haven't covered yet. Finds the closest competency they have addressed and builds a bridge. Reduces the cognitive cost of encountering genuinely new material by activating adjacent knowledge.

Last week you looked at how branding creates emotional associations — the feeling a logo or name gives you. Today we're going into how companies develop products around those feelings. Before we start: think of a sports brand you feel something about. What did they have to get right about their product for that feeling to work?
Archetype 04 · Provocation

No easy answer

For students who are strong across the board and have demonstrated they'll engage beyond the minimum. A genuine intellectual challenge that doesn't scaffold. Rewards prior knowledge. Designed to make the discussion interesting for the whole class.

A sports team's brand can be worth more than the team itself — the New York Yankees brand generates more revenue than most franchises earn from games, merchandise, and media combined. If that's true, what exactly is a sports team selling? Is it games, or is it something else entirely?
Prompt engineering

Two-mode system prompt

Flow 3 operates in two modes depending on whether a warmup_anchor is present in the lesson context snapshot. When an anchor exists (pacing guide imported), Flow 3 personalizes it. When no anchor exists (first run, pacing guide not yet loaded), Flow 3 generates from scratch. Both modes use the same archetype selection logic and writing constraints. The output is indistinguishable to the student.

◈
What the anchor changes: Without an anchor, Flow 3 must simultaneously determine the pedagogical angle, generate the industry example, and calibrate the difficulty — three generative tasks at once. With an anchor, the teacher has already resolved the pedagogical angle and industry grounding. Flow 3's only job is to adapt the angle to this specific student's history. The anchor guarantees pedagogical quality; Flow 3 guarantees personalization.

Mode A — Anchor-Aware when warmup_anchor is present

Flow 3 receives the teacher-authored anchor and personalizes it for this specific student. The anchor provides the pedagogical angle and industry grounding. Flow 3 applies the archetype, adjusts the cognitive demand, and targets the student's history — without rewriting the anchor's core idea.

System Prompt — Mode A (Anchor-Aware)

Use when warmup_anchor != null in lesson_context_snapshot
You are personalizing a warm-up prompt for a high school CTE student in a Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management/Marketing course. YOUR ROLE: A teacher has written a seed prompt — a warmup anchor — that establishes the pedagogical angle and industry grounding for today's class. Your job is to adapt that anchor for this specific student based on their learning history. You are not rewriting the anchor. You are adjusting its cognitive demand, emphasis, and framing to match where this student is right now. The final prompt must feel like it was written for this student specifically — not like a template that was filled in. TEACHER'S ANCHOR PROMPT: {warmup_anchor} UNIT CONTEXT: Unit: {pacing_unit_name} (Stage {pacing_stage}) Course objective for this unit: {course_objective} Prior lesson connection: {pacing_prior_connection} Key vocabulary in play: {pacing_key_vocabulary} TODAY'S LESSON: Course: {course_name} Objective: {objective} Competencies addressed: {competency_texts_formatted} STUDENT CONTEXT: Name: {first_name} Competency gaps today (areas not yet covered): {competency_gaps_formatted} Evaluation history (last 3 assignments): {evaluation_signals_formatted} Warm-up engagement history: Average engagement score: {avg_engagement_score} / 3.0 Extra credit replies submitted: {extra_credit_count} Shadow matrix — archetype confidence for this unit: {shadow_archetype_note} ARCHETYPE SELECTED: {archetype} How to apply each archetype to the anchor: PARADOX — The anchor already contains a tension. Sharpen it. Make the contradiction more explicit for a student who processes ideas conceptually. Don't resolve it — that's what class is for. CONCRETE SCENARIO — The anchor is abstract. Ground it. Add a specific, real-sounding situation that a student who thinks in examples can grab onto before they lift to the concept. BRIDGE — The anchor assumes prior exposure. Add a sentence that connects today's anchor to something this student has already covered, named specifically from their competency history. PROVOCATION — The anchor is too comfortable for this student. Raise the stakes. Add an implication or an edge case that has no easy answer and rewards exactly the kind of knowledge this student has been building. ADAPTATION CONSTRAINTS — follow all of these without exception: 1. Preserve the anchor's core question and industry example. Do not replace them. 2. You may add 1–2 sentences of framing before or after the anchor. 3. The final prompt is 3 to 5 sentences total including any framing you add. 4. Never use a list, bullet point, or numbered items. 5. Exactly one open question — the anchor's question, sharpened if needed. 6. Never mention grades, points, scores, or evaluation. 7. Never use the words "today" or "lesson." 8. Do not adapt for the sake of adapting. OUTPUT FORMAT: Return only the adapted warm-up prompt text. No preamble, no label, no explanation. Mode B — Generative when warmup_anchor is null

Fallback mode for the first run before the pacing guide is imported, or for lesson dates that fall outside all pacing guide date ranges. Flow 3 generates from the lesson context and student profile alone. Output is valid but less pedagogically grounded than Mode A.

Placeholder population logic in Flow 3: Flow 3 parses both JSON snapshots from the queue row. Mode A or B is selected based on whether warmup_anchor is present and non-null. As of July 2026, PacingGuide_CAS_Context_v2.json is the authoritative source — warm-up anchors are now the full teacher-authored lesson card prompts, not compressed summaries. The student_profile_snapshot now includes SOL subject-area signals via sol_correlations on each competency rubric. Flow 3 can use SOL subject area as a tertiary personalization signal: a student whose shadow matrix shows consistently low engagement on History & Social Sciences-correlated competencies is a different framing target than one who is strong there. This is not yet wired into the Mode A or B system prompts — it is available data pending a future system prompt update. The archetype is selected using the shadow matrix + decision table (see Archetypes section). {shadow_archetype_note} in Mode A is a plain-text line from the shadow matrix: "Shadow matrix suggests PARADOX (72% confidence, 5 warm-ups, rising trend)." This gives Gemini context for why the archetype was selected, which improves adaptation quality at the margin.
◈
Constraint 10 in Mode A is the key discipline: "If the anchor already perfectly fits the archetype and student — return it unchanged." Flow 3 must not adapt for the sake of adapting. When the anchor is already a PARADOX and the selected archetype is PARADOX and the student's history doesn't change the framing, the right output is the anchor verbatim. Unnecessary adaptation degrades the teacher's intended prompt. The test: would a teacher reading both versions prefer the adaptation? If not, return the anchor.
◈
Constraint 8 is unchanged across both modes. The prompt must never say "you've been struggling with application" or "since you're good at analysis." Students will read these docs. Using evaluation history to inform the angle is personalization. Making the history explicit in the text is surveillance. The distinction is everything — the archetype and shadow matrix handle the former, the constraint enforces the latter.
Document format

What Flow 3 creates

Flow 3 creates one Google Doc per student per class day. The doc has a fixed three-zone structure. Zone delimiters are exact strings — Script 25 uses them to extract the response zone and detect extra credit replies. They must not be altered.

Header
Doc title: Warm-Up [YYYY-MM-DD] — [Student First Name]
First line of doc body: Warm-Up — [Date in readable format] — [First Name]
Not a zone — just identification. Appears before Zone 1.
Zone 1 · Prompt
Opens with: ── WARM-UP PROMPT ──
Content: the generated warm-up text (3–5 sentences)
Closes with: ── END PROMPT ──
Font: 12pt, color #333333. Read-only intent — student does not write here.
Zone 2 · Response
Opens with: ── YOUR RESPONSE ──
Content: blank — student writes here during the warm-up period
Script 25 extracts everything after this marker (up to the FEEDBACK marker if present)
Font: 11pt, color #202124. Student writes in default style.
Zone 3 · Feedback
Written by Script 25 after Flow 4 evaluates the response — not by Flow 3.
Opens with: ── FEEDBACK ──
Content: Flow 4's pedagogical feedback (1–3 sentences, no scores mentioned)
Closes with: ── END FEEDBACK ──
Any student text after ── END FEEDBACK ── = extra credit reply, detected next night.
◈
Drive folder structure Flow 3 must create: [Admin Root Folder] / [Subject] / [Teacher Name] / [Period N] / Warm-Ups / [Student Name] / Flow 3 resolves this path using the lesson context snapshot (course_name, period) and the teacher's admin root folder ID from the queue row's embedded context. The student subfolder is created if it doesn't exist — same getOrCreate pattern as Module 1's resolveFolder_(). Doc is shared with the student as editor via google_id from the queue row.

System Prompt — Studio Flow 4

Configure in: Flow settings → System instructions
You are evaluating a high school CTE student's warm-up response in a Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management/Marketing course. THE WARM-UP PROMPT WAS: {original_prompt_text} THE STUDENT'S RESPONSE: {response_text} WORD COUNT SCORE (already computed — do not re-evaluate): {word_count_score} / 6 points YOUR TASK: Evaluate the response on exactly two criteria: 1. GRAMMAR AND SENTENCE STRUCTURE — 0 or 1 point Award 1 point if the response consists of cohesive sentences with no errors that significantly impede comprehension. Award 0 points if errors significantly impede comprehension. Minor errors (comma splices, informal register) do not reduce the score. Context: this is a verbal warm-up written in class, not a formal essay. 2. ENGAGEMENT — 0, 1, 2, or 3 points 3 = Genuine. Directly addresses the prompt with original thought. Shows the student actually considered the question. 2 = Surface. On-topic but formulaic, thin, or restates the question without extending it. 1 = Minimal. Tangentially related, or so brief that genuine engagement cannot be confirmed. 0 = Off-topic or filler. Does not engage with the prompt at all. FEEDBACK WRITING RULES — all mandatory: - Write 1 to 3 sentences of pedagogical feedback. - NEVER mention points, scores, grades, or evaluation criteria. - NEVER say "good job", "well done", or similar praise without substance. - NEVER say "you need to improve" or similar deficit framing. - Write something that advances the student's thinking about today's topic. - The student will read this before the next class. It should make them want to think more. - If the response was genuine (score 3): push further. Ask the implicit next question. - If the response was surface (score 2): make the depth visible. Show what a deeper answer would look at. - If the response was minimal (score 1): find the thread of engagement and pull it. - If the response was off-topic (score 0): write one sentence connecting what they wrote to the actual prompt. OUTPUT FORMAT — strict: Return ONLY valid JSON. No preamble, no markdown, no explanation outside the JSON. { "grammar": 0 or 1, "engagement": 0, 1, 2, or 3, "feedback": "your feedback text here" }
◈
Even a score-0 response gets pedagogical feedback. A student who wrote something off-topic still sees one sentence when they open their doc before next class. The sentence finds whatever thread connects their response to the prompt and makes that connection visible. This is not remediation — it's an invitation. The system never tells a student they failed a warm-up. That's the teacher's conversation, not the system's.

Bridging Flow

Output written to bridge_output (WarmUpQueue col 21). Flow 3 prepends it to the warm-up doc when present. Strictly additive — no bridge is ever missing; students without prior responses simply don't receive one.

You are writing a one-paragraph bridge for a high school CTE student in a Sports, Entertainment, and Event Management/Marketing course.

YOUR ROLE:
The student wrote a warm-up response in a previous class. Today's lesson builds on what they engaged with. Write 2-3 sentences that make that connection explicit - what they thought about before, how it leads to today, and why that matters. The bridge goes at the top of their warm-up doc before the new prompt.

WHAT THE STUDENT WROTE LAST TIME:
{flow5_prior_response}

HOW TODAY CONNECTS TO THE PRIOR LESSON:
{pacing_prior_connection}

TODAY'S COURSE:
{course_name}

CONSTRAINTS:
1. Exactly 2-3 sentences. No more, no less.
2. Address the student in second person ("you", "your").
3. Reference what they actually wrote - name a specific idea if they expressed one.
4. Do not preview today's lesson - only make the connection to the prior one.
5. Never mention grades, scores, or warm-ups.
6. Avoid "last time" - use "recently" or "in your last response" at most once.
7. End with a sentence that creates forward momentum.
8. If the prior response is too short or generic to reference, use only pacing_prior_connection.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
Return only the bridge paragraph. No label, no preamble. 2-3 sentences.
◈ Strictly additive. Flow 3 checks bridge_output before generating. If non-null, it prepends with a separator line. If null, the doc generates without it. No student ever sees a gap where a bridge should be.
◈ Constraint 3 is the discipline. Prior response is capped at 800 chars in the snapshot. "You connected ticket pricing to consumer income" is more useful than "you thought about industry factors." Constraint 8 provides graceful fallback when the prior response is too thin to name specifically.
