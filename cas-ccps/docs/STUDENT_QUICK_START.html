Student Guide

Your Assignment,
Step by Step

Write in your document. Press Submit for Feedback. Answer one question about your feedback. Paste your turn-in code into Canvas. Here's how each step works.

📄 Open the assignment ✍️ Write under the grey line 📊 Submit for Feedback 💬 Answer the question about your feedback ✅ Paste your turn-in code into Canvas
01

Open Your Document

Every lesson has its own Google Doc, made just for you. You don't make it. It shows up the night before the lesson.

1
Sign in with your school Google account

Your document and your dashboard only work with your school account.

2
Open it from your dashboard

On your dashboard, find the assignment and press Open My Document ↗. The Canvas assignment also has a link to your dashboard.

3
Or find it in Google Drive

Go to drive.google.com and click Shared with me. The document's name starts with the lesson's name and ends with your name.

⚠️
Use that document. Don't make a copy. The system only reads the one made for you.
02

What's in Your Document

Write in one place: under the grey line. Here's everything on the page, top to bottom.

Page header (small grey text)
Your name, class, teacher, block and period. On the next line, the lesson and a Your dashboard link. A Feedback: line that says where your work stands.
System writes here
📋 The lesson title and the assignment
The assignment is in a box: The assignment says what to do, and What your work needs to show lists your “I can” goals as a checklist. ↓ Go to your response jumps down to where you write.
Read this
“Your response” heading
At the top of a new page, so your writing never starts at the bottom of the assignment's page. Under it: “Write your answer under the grey line below.” and ↑ Back to the assignment.
Read this
✍️ ── YOUR RESPONSE BEGINS HERE ──
The grey line. Write under it, in the bigger text.
You write here
“↑ Write your response above. Nothing below this is checked.”
The last line of the page. Your assignment code is in the page footer. (Older documents have a grey “[CONFIG_ID: …] Do not delete this line.” line here instead: write above it.)
Do not edit
🚫
Keep your writing under the grey line and above the last line of the page. Anything above the grey line or below the last line is not checked. Do not change the assignment, the grey line or the last line.
💡
Your feedback isn't in the document text. It's a comment on the document. Click Comments (top right) to see it.
03

Get Feedback

Submit as many times as you like. Revising is the point, and there's no penalty.

1
Write at least 25 words

Write under the grey line. With fewer than 25 words, the dashboard says “Write a little more first.” and tells you about how many words it found.

2
Press Submit for Feedback on your dashboard

Open your dashboard (the Your dashboard link at the top of your document). Find the assignment and press Submit for Feedback. You'll see: “✅ Submitted. In a few minutes your feedback will be a comment on your document: open it and click Comments (top right).”

Your card may first ask one or two short questions about how you worked, such as where your work shows a skill, or the hardest choice you made. Answer each in at least 10 words, then press Submit for Feedback again. Your answers are not part of your work: they come back to you as a comment on your document, so you can look at them while you revise.

3
Wait a few minutes

Keep your dashboard open. While your work waits, the card shows a line of where it is: Submitted → In line → Being checked → Feedback ready, with the time of each step it knows. Being checked may say how long a check usually takes. The page checks for news by itself, and when your feedback is ready it tells you at the top of the page and moves to that card.

If a step takes more than about 8 minutes, the card says “Taking a while. Your work is saved.” with a Check again button. Check again only asks for the latest news; it doesn't send your work again, so you don't need to submit again.

4
Open your document and click Comments

Your feedback is a comment. Each time you submit, you get a new one. The newest is the one to read.

What your card says

Each assignment has a card on your dashboard. The coloured label says where it stands in a word or two; the line under the title says what to do. The filled button is the next step.

Cards that need you are listed first, under Do next, at the top of the page. Click one to jump to its card.

Not started

You haven't submitted this one. Open your document, write, then press Submit for Feedback.

Waiting for feedback

Your work is in line to be checked. Nothing to do yet. Check back in a few minutes.

Being checked

Your work is being read right now.

Passed ✓

Your work meets the standard. The line under the title says where your feedback is. The card asks one question about your feedback: answer it to get your turn-in code (section 06). You can also keep improving your work and submit again.

Revise

Read the feedback, change your work, then press Submit for Feedback again. A Not yet: line names the part of the assignment your last check didn't find yet: start there. The card also asks one question about that feedback. You can answer it and get your turn-in code now, before you pass (section 06).

Turned in

Your teacher is reviewing your work. Nothing to do yet.

Done ✓

Your teacher confirmed this assignment.

Check stopped ⚠

The check took more than 12 minutes and was stopped. Your work is saved and your teacher has been told. Press Try again on the card to put the same work back in line; you don't need to submit again. You can press it once every 10 minutes: if you press it sooner, the card says how many minutes to wait. If the feedback arrives late, the card changes to show it.

Couldn’t be checked ⚠

Something went wrong on our side, not with your work. The line under the title says whether you can submit again or should tell your teacher; the card has a link to email them.

The card also shows Last submitted (when you last pressed Submit for Feedback) and Last checked (when you last got feedback), or “Not checked yet”.

⏱️
Most checks take a few minutes. While you wait, the card shows where your work is (Submitted, In line, Being checked) and this page shows your feedback when it’s ready. If a check takes longer than 12 minutes, the system stops it, your card says “Check stopped ⚠”, and your teacher is told by email. Press Try again on the card; you do not need to submit again.
🔔
The page checks for news while you wait. It pauses while you're on another tab and starts again when you come back. After many checks on one visit it stops and says so: then press ↻ Refresh to see what’s ready.
04

Read Your Feedback

Open your document and click Comments (top right). Each comment starts with the date and a result line. Here's the shape of one. Your own will talk about your work.

Example comment: passed

Feedback, Oct 22, 10:41 AM

Result: ✅ Passed. Your work meets the standard.

Overall assessment

A few sentences on what your work does well and what would make it stronger.

Milestone breakdown

Each milestone (a part of the task) marked MET, PARTIALLY MET or NOT MET, with a reason.

What to do next

1. Get your turn-in code: on your dashboard, answer the one question on this assignment's card. 2. Copy the code and paste it into the Canvas assignment. Want to make it stronger? (optional) Revise it, then press Submit for Feedback again. Your turn-in code stays the same. (If a Canvas assignment asks for your document's link instead, the comment says so: follow the Canvas instructions.)

If your work needs more, the result line is different, and there's a list of what to fix:

Example comment: revise

Result: ✏️ Revise, then submit again.

Required revisions

A numbered list of the changes to make.

What to do next

Change your work under the “Your response” heading, then press Submit for Feedback on your dashboard again. Repeat until you see ✅.

✅
Revising is normal. Feedback is a coach, not a grade. Your teacher grades your work in Canvas.
💬
Resolving a comment doesn't delete it. If you click Resolve, the comment is still in the document's comment history, and your teacher can still see it.
05

Warm-ups

A warm-up is a short question at the start of class. You answer it on your dashboard, in the Warm-ups card at the top.

1
Type your answer and press Save answer

Find Today's warm-up. Type in the box, then press Save answer. You can change it until the end of the day: the button then says Update answer.

2
Read your score and feedback

Warm-ups are checked overnight. Next time, Last warm-up shows your score out of 10 (Length, Grammar and Engagement) and your feedback. Length is about word count: 30 words or more gets the full length points.

3
Earn extra credit

Under the feedback, reply to it in at least 10 words by the date shown (the end of your next class day), and press Save reply. Replies are checked overnight. Once it counts, you'll see “✓ Extra credit earned” and “+1 extra credit”.

4
Missed a warm-up?

If you didn't answer last class's warm-up, Last warm-up shows it with a box until the end of your next class day. A late answer is scored, but you don't get feedback on it or a chance at extra credit.

📄
Some classes use a warm-up document instead. If the card shows Open warm-up ↗, answer in that document, and reply under the feedback there for extra credit.
06

Turn It In: Your Turn-in Code

You turn in on Canvas. That's where your teacher grades it. For most assignments, what you paste into Canvas is a turn-in code from your dashboard. You earn the code by using your feedback. Here's the whole path.

1
Open the assignment

Open your document from your dashboard with Open My Document ↗ (sections 01 and 02).

2
Write under the grey line

At least 25 words, under “── YOUR RESPONSE BEGINS HERE ──”.

3
Submit for feedback

Press Submit for Feedback on your dashboard and wait for your feedback (section 03).

4
Answer the one question built from your feedback

When your feedback is ready, the card has a Turn-in code box with one question. It is made from your own feedback. If your feedback says Not yet, it asks what you will change and where in your document. If you passed, it asks you to point to the part of your work that shows the skill and say why it does. Write at least 10 words in your own words, then press Send answer.

The card says “Checking your answer…”. This takes a few minutes, and the page shows your code when it's ready. If the card says Not yet:, it tells you why in one line: rewrite your answer and send it again, as many times as you need.

5
Copy your turn-in code and paste it into the Canvas assignment

Press Copy beside the code. Press Open Canvas ↗ beside it (or Canvas ↗ at the top of your dashboard) to open Canvas in a new tab. Open the same assignment, choose Start Assignment → Text Entry, paste the code and press Submit Assignment. Paste only the code: your teacher reads your work in your document.

📌
Read the Canvas instructions first. Each Canvas assignment says whether to paste a turn-in code or a link to your document. If it asks for a link, copy your document's address from the browser's address bar and choose Start Assignment → Website URL instead. For a lesson in parts, finish every part; the Canvas instructions say what to paste.

What the code means

Used your feedback

The code shows you read your feedback and did something with it. It does not say your work is good or finished. Your teacher still reads your work.

Revise is fine

You can earn the code on a Revise card: the question asks what you will change. The code turns in this round; it isn't a pass. Revise and submit for feedback as many times as you need. In a lesson in parts, the next part opens when this part meets its goal.

Passed

On a Passed card, the question asks how what you showed connects to another goal, like the next part's.

Yours alone

The code belongs to you and this one assignment. Someone else's code won't work for you, and yours won't work for them, so don't share it. It stays the same if you submit again, and you can copy it again any time.

After it's in

Once your code is in Canvas, you can keep revising and submitting for feedback. That revising is for your learning and your final grade; you don't need a new code.

What a good answer does

There is no answer to copy: the question comes from your own feedback. A good answer:

→
Names the specific part

Says which sentence, paragraph, example or choice in your work it means, not just “my work”.

→
Says where

Says where that part is in your document, or where the change will go.

→
Says why

Explains why that part shows the skill, or why the change will fix what the feedback found.

An answer that is too short, or that mostly copies the feedback or the question, is sent back straight away with the reason. Use your own words.

💡
If the card says “We couldn’t check your answer.” That's a problem on our side, not with your answer. Your teacher can see it, and you can send it again. If it says this marking period has ended, the dashboard can't give a code for it any more: ask your teacher.
07

Your Dashboard

Your dashboard is called 📚 My Assignments. Open it from the Your dashboard link at the top of any of your documents.

→
This marking period

Assignments shows this marking period's work, named at the top, in the order your teacher assigned it in each class. Each card has the assignment's name, its lesson number (like L05), one line on what you do, and what the lesson checks in your own words (“I can …”) with what your checked work showed: Shown, Partly shown, Not shown yet, or Not checked yet. A lesson in parts is one card on the part to work on now, with dots for its parts (● done); All parts shows the rest. Sort changes the order (Assigned order, Needs attention first, Recently checked, Not yet shown first), and the buttons beside it show only the work that needs action, is in progress, is not started or is done. Do next above it lists what needs you. Work from earlier marking periods leaves the page; what it showed stays in 🎓 Competencies.

→
The tabs

📋 Assignments is your work and today's warm-up. Some lessons come in parts, each with its own document: a locked part (🔒) opens when the check finds the part before meets its main goal, or when your teacher opens it. A card’s What this lesson checks opens to show the lesson’s main “I can” goals and what your checked work showed for each. On a lesson in parts, that is your best so far across its parts, and each mark names the part that showed it (“Shown in Part 2”), so a part you haven’t submitted yet can show marks from earlier parts. For a lesson in parts, the Canvas instructions say what to turn in. 🎓 Competencies lists every competency in your course by area (or by rating, or by your most recent evidence), with your teacher's rating as its number and words (or “Not rated yet”), what your latest checked work showed, what it means in your own words, the lessons where you practice it, what you showed this marking period and this year, and what to practice next; Review on a competency shows a short recap from each lesson, its key terms and any video and each piece of evidence your checked work gave: the assignment, the date, and Shown, Partly shown or Not shown yet. A rating of 3 or 4 also says what more work would let your teacher re-rate it, or that it is ready for a re-rating; your teacher decides. ✏️ Warm-ups has every past warm-up with its score, feedback, the question and your answer. 🗂️ My Context shows what the system has gathered about your work, one week at a time, with a link straight to that week in the document.

→
🎓 Competencies done

At the top, one line per course: how many of the course's competencies your teacher has rated you able to do with limited supervision or better, out of all of them (for example “12 of 113”). It goes up as your teacher rates your work.

→
ℹ️ My Info

In the More ▾ menu at the top. What the system has on file for you: your name, class, period and teacher. If something is wrong, press ✉ Request a correction or tell your teacher.

→
❓ What do these mean?

Also in More ▾. Opens a list of the words on the page and what they mean. Press Escape or Close to shut it.

→
↻ Refresh

While a card is waiting for feedback or a turn-in code, the page checks for news by itself. Everything else updates when you press Refresh.

→
Canvas ↗

Beside Refresh at the top. Opens Canvas in a new tab, so your dashboard stays open.

08

Common Questions

I pressed Submit for Feedback. Where's my feedback?⌄
Keep your dashboard open: the card shows where your work is (Submitted, In line, Being checked) and the page checks for news by itself. If it's taking a while, press Check again on the card, or ↻ Refresh. When it's ready, open your document and click Comments (top right). If the card says “Couldn't be checked ⚠”, tell your teacher.
It says “Write a little more first.”⌄
You need at least 25 words under the grey line. The message says about how many words it found. If it says you have not written anything under “Your response” yet, check that you wrote under the “── YOUR RESPONSE BEGINS HERE ──” line and above the last line of the page.
It says “This is already waiting for feedback.”⌄
You already submitted, and it's in line. You don't need to press it again. Check back in a few minutes.
It says my response “has text copied from a feedback block.”⌄
Your writing has a line the system uses itself, like one starting with [SYSTEM:. The message shows which. Delete that line and submit again. Your own words are fine.
Do I have to pass before I get my turn-in code?⌄
No. You can earn your code on a Revise card: answer the question about your feedback. The code shows you used your feedback, not that your work is done. Keep revising after that if you want your work to meet the standard.
My answer says “Not yet”.⌄
Read the one-line reason, then rewrite your answer and send it again. There is no limit. Name the specific part of your work, say where it is, and say why. Use your own words: an answer that mostly copies the feedback or the question comes back right away.
Copy didn't work.⌄
The card selects the code for you: press Ctrl+C (⌘C on a Mac), then paste it into Canvas. You can also type it: it doesn't matter if you get capitals or dashes wrong.
Can I submit more than once? Will it hurt my grade?⌄
Submit as often as you need. There's no penalty. Write, get feedback, improve, repeat.
I changed my work after I turned it in on Canvas. What now?⌄
Nothing to turn in again. Your code stays the same, and your teacher reads your work in your document as it is when they open it. Keep pressing Submit for Feedback to get your changes checked: that revising is for your learning and your final grade. If you're not sure, ask your teacher.
My assignment isn't on my dashboard.⌄
Check that you're signed into your school Google account. A lesson's document shows up the night before the lesson. Check the year menu says “Whole school year”. If it's still missing, tell your teacher.
I don’t see a “📊 Feedback” menu in my document.⌄
That's normal. That menu doesn't work for student accounts. Use Submit for Feedback on your dashboard instead.
I deleted part of my document by accident.⌄
Try File → Version history → See version history and restore an earlier version. If you deleted the grey line or the end of the page and cannot get it back, tell your teacher.
Something in My Info is wrong.⌄
Open More ▾, then ℹ️ My Info, and press ✉ Request a correction, or tell your teacher.
09

Words You'll See

The same list as ❓ What do these mean? on your dashboard.

Competencies done
Competencies in your course that your teacher has rated you able to do with limited supervision or better (1 to 3 on the state's 5-point scale), out of every competency in the course.
Competency rating
Your teacher's rating of a competency on Virginia's 1 to 5 scale, where 1 is the highest: 1 Can teach others, 2 Without supervision, 3 Limited supervision, 4 With supervision, 5 Cannot perform. “Not rated yet” until your teacher decides.
Evidence
What a checked piece of your work showed for a competency: Shown, Partly shown or Not shown yet. Each assignment counts once, at its best result, so an early draft doesn't count against you.
My Context
What the system has gathered about your work, week by week: your checked assignments and your warm-up answers. The same document your teacher sees.
Submit for Feedback
Sends what you wrote under “Your response” to be checked. Press it when you’re ready; you can submit again after you revise.
Do next
The list at the top of your assignments: the ones waiting on you. Click one to jump to it.
Not started
You haven’t submitted this one yet. Open your document, write under “Your response”, then press Submit for Feedback.
Waiting for feedback
Your work is in line to be checked. Nothing to do yet; check back in a few minutes.
Being checked
Your work is being read right now.
Passed ✓
Your work meets the standard for this assignment. You can still improve it and submit again.
Revise
Read the feedback, change your work, then press Submit for Feedback again.
Not yet
The part of the assignment your last check didn’t find in your work yet. Start your revision there.
Turned in
Your teacher is reviewing your work. Nothing to do yet.
Done ✓
Your teacher confirmed this assignment.
Check stopped
The check took too long and was stopped. Your work is saved. Press Try again on the card to put it back in line.
Couldn’t be checked
Something went wrong on our side, not with your work. Tell your teacher.
Comments
Your feedback appears as a comment on your document. Open the document and click the Comments button (top right). Resolving a comment doesn’t delete it.
Last submitted
When you last pressed Submit for Feedback on this assignment.
Last checked
When your work was last checked and given feedback.
Warm-up
A short question at the start of class. Type your answer here and save it before the end of the day. Missed one? You can answer it late until the end of your next class day, for points but no feedback or extra credit.
Extra credit
After a warm-up is checked, reply to its feedback in at least 10 words by the date shown (the end of your next class day) for an extra point.
Marking period
Part of the school year. Use the menu at the top to see one marking period or the whole year.
Block and period
When your class meets. 3-Odd and 3-Even are the two halves of period 3 that meet on alternate days.
My Info
What the system has on file for you: your name, class, period and teacher. Tell your teacher if something is wrong.
