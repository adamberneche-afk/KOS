## Student Documents

Open a student's document from their row. Each document is shared with that student (they can edit) and with you (you can comment). Classmates have no access.

**What the student sees, top to bottom:**

- A small grey page header: who and where (name, class, teacher, block, period), then the lesson and a “Your dashboard” link on their own line, and a “Feedback:” line that says where the work stands.
- The lesson title, a “↓ Go to your response” link, and the assignment in a two-row box (9 pt): “The assignment” (the task, with its headings and bullets), then “What your work needs to show” (the student’s “I can” objectives as a checklist).
- A “Your response” heading, “Write your answer under the grey line below.” and “↑ Back to the assignment”.
- The grey “── YOUR RESPONSE BEGINS HERE ──” line, then the student's writing space (10 pt).
- A last line, “↑ Write your response above. Nothing below this is checked.” Only the text between the grey line and the end of the writing space is checked. The assignment code is in the page footer. (Older docs end on a grey “[CONFIG_ID: …] Do not delete this line.” line instead.)

> **Feedback is a comment, not text in the document.** With feedback as a comment (the setting is on for every course), each check adds a new comment. Click Comments (top right) to read them. A student who presses Resolve doesn't delete it.

- **Student Context**, one week at a time: **‹ Older** and **Newer ›** move between weeks, and **Open the doc ↗** is on every week.

**Showing (the lesson picker).** It opens on the **current lesson** for each course: the lesson whose start date in 📅 Lessons is the latest on or before today. Pick another lesson, or **All assignments** at the bottom.

Click a card to show only those students; **Clear filter** shows everyone again. In All assignments the first card counts assignments, since a student has one row per assignment. Under the cards, **📭 n haven't submitted yet** shows only the students with no Submit on record for the work in view (they're among the ⚪ Not checked yet); click it again to show everyone.

Rows are grouped by lesson. In each lesson, rows that need you (Couldn’t be checked ⚠, or Waiting for your review) come first, then the rest by period and name, which follows SpeedGrader. A Couldn’t be checked ⚠ row also says why and what to do next.

**Warm-up readiness strip** (under the header, when warm-ups are on). The pacing guide unit, then “x of N students have had work checked”, “y answered a warm-up”, “building a personalized learning profile” and “ready for fully personalized feedback”. Click a number to show those students.

## Understanding Status

Each student's work for a lesson is in one of four states:

- **⚪ Not checked yet**: No check has come back. The student hasn't pressed Submit for Feedback, or the first check is still running.

- **✏️ Checked · not passing yet**: One or more checks came back, and none passed. The student is working on it.

- **✅ Passed**: The latest check met the assignment's criteria. The student can turn it in on Canvas, or keep improving it.

- **↩️ Passed before · latest needs revision**: An earlier check passed, and the latest one needs revision.

A second badge shows while a check is on its way, or when it failed:

- **Waiting to be checked**: Submitted and in line for its check. The student's card says “Waiting for feedback…”.

- **Being checked now**: The check is running. The student sees the same words.

- **Couldn't be checked ⚠**: The check failed or timed out. This isn't the student's work. A check still running after 12 minutes is stopped and lands here (the student’s card then says “Taking longer than usual ⚠”). These rows are counted under Needs attention. See When Students Have Problems.

> Passing isn't required before a student turns in on Canvas. A Canvas submission from a student who is ⚪ or ✏️ here hasn't passed a check yet.

## Lessons and Start Dates

Give each lesson a start date in 📅 Lessons. To change what a lesson says, or add one, write a draft there. You don't upload rubrics or forms to make assignments.

1. **Open 📅 Lessons**: One table per course: Lesson, Status (Current, Upcoming, Taught or No date), Starts, and Last rollout. Next to each course name: whether the rollout is on, or “in shadow (changes nothing)”. **Make lesson doc** makes your teacher doc for that lesson, in a *Lesson Plans* folder: the plan, an **itinerary** (what happens when, with minutes for your blocks on Schedule A and B, drafted from the lesson; change any minutes or words there), look-fors, a section for each day it runs with room for notes, and **Class status** per period (documents made, shared, submitted, waiting for feedback, passed). Once made, it shows **Lesson doc ↗**, and the button reads **Refresh status**: it updates Class status only, so your edits stay. With the nightly rollout on, the doc is made for you before the lesson starts. **Today's agenda** (next to each course name) makes the day's Google Slides agenda to project, in an *Agendas* folder: the day and today's goals, a slide for each part of the itinerary with its mode and each period's minutes, and “Before you go”. It reads your lesson doc's itinerary, so change the minutes there and press it again to remake the deck. On a day with no class it makes the next class day's. With the nightly rollout on, tomorrow's agenda is made for you each night.
2. **Set a start date**: Type or pick a date in Starts, then press Enter or leave the box. The row says **Saved ✓**, or **Not saved** and why. A date in the next 2 days, in the past, or on the current lesson asks you to confirm first. **Clear** removes it. A lesson runs from its start date until the next one starts. **Hold** (under the status) keeps the nightly rollout from making or sharing that lesson's student documents, whatever its date, until you press **Release**; the row says **Held: no student docs**. Documents students already have stay shared, so holding a lesson that has started asks first.
3. **Edit a lesson, or add one**: **Edit** opens the lesson's fields as a draft. **+ New lesson** (next to the course name) starts an empty one: title, objective, activity, the student task or a brief for it, a warm-up anchor, vocabulary, and competencies with a tier each.
4. **Save draft, then Submit for import**: Submit only works once every required field is filled. The draft is imported that evening and comes back as a proposal.
5. **Review the proposal**: Open it under Lesson drafts. Choose **Accept the proposal**, or **Return with my note** to send it back. An accepted lesson goes live once the change is reviewed and published (the screen calls this a PR: a reviewed change to the system's files).

> **Competency tiers.** Each lesson's competencies are primary (must be shown to pass), secondary or tertiary (credited when shown). The draft won't submit unless the tiers follow those rules.

## How Students Get Their Documents

Nothing to do. You don't register students one at a time. The nightly rollout (about 9 p.m.) does it from the Canvas roster.

1. **Enroll**: For the current lesson and any lesson starting within 2 days, the rollout enrolls every student on the Canvas roster export.
2. **Make the documents**: Each student gets their own document for the lesson. Until the lesson's release night, it isn't shared yet.
3. **Share them the night before**: The night before a lesson starts, each document is shared with its student and appears on their dashboard. A student who joins late gets theirs after the next run.

> **A student is missing?** Check they're in the Canvas section. A late joiner only appears once a fresh Canvas roster export is in place; an old export shows as a ⚠ line in 📅 Lessons' Last rollout column. Tell your admin.

## Settings

**⚙ Settings** turns each feature on or off for each course, and sets the marking period dates. Each feature says what on and off do. Tick or untick a course's box and change any dates; the bar at the bottom lists what isn't saved yet and what each change does (“Turn Nightly lesson rollout on for 8175: …”). Nothing changes until you press **Save changes**, which saves them all at once; **Discard** puts them back. Closing the panel or leaving the page with changes not saved asks first. A saved change shows on your dashboard at once and reaches students and the overnight jobs within about 10 minutes. Every change is kept in the **SettingsLog** tab, and the panel lists the recent ones.

| Feature | On | Off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feedback as a comment | A checked submission's feedback goes on the student's document as a comment; new documents are made without a feedback section | Feedback goes into the document's feedback section |
| Warm-ups on the Student Dashboard | Students answer warm-ups in a text box on their dashboard | Each warm-up is a Google Doc |
| Nightly lesson rollout | Each night it makes the next lesson's documents and shares them the night before the lesson starts | It only records what it would do (📅 Lessons shows each run) |
| Reflection on Submit | Submit for Feedback asks one or two short questions about how the student worked; the answers go with the work | Submit sends the work straight away |
| Warm-up style lock (research) | Keeps the warm-up style the system is confident works for a student; its email goes out only when every course is on | The style is chosen fresh each time |
| Parent reports ready on Fridays | Each Friday at 5 a.m. the week's reports for that course's students are logged in ParentReportLog as ready, not yet copied; nothing is sent | A report is built when you open ✉ Parent Reports; only the ones you copy are logged |

**Marking periods.** The last day of each marking period, as dates. The term on both dashboards and in the reports changes the day after. Until you change one, they're the district calendar's. They save with **Save changes**.

**Activity and delay days.** A day that runs a different bell schedule from usual: an activity day, a 2-hour delay, an early release that isn't on the district calendar, or an A or B day swapped. Press **+ Add a day**, pick the date and the schedule, and save. The lesson doc and the daily agenda then use that day's minutes. An agenda already made for the day changes when you press **Today's agenda** in 📅 Lessons again. The district calendar's early release days are already known, so you don't need to add them.

**What a feedback comment says.** “Feedback, <date and time>”, then “Result: ✅ Passed. Your work meets the standard.” or “Result: ✏️ Revise, then submit again.”, then the report (overall assessment, each milestone MET / PARTIALLY MET / NOT MET, required revisions) and what to do next.

## Warm-ups and Lesson Logs

Log the lessons you teach. That's what lets warm-ups fit each student over time. This part of the system is Module 2.

**Log a lesson** (the header's **+ Log today's lesson**). Enter the date, one or more periods, the objective, what students did, vocabulary, and tick the competencies you taught. Teaching the same lesson to several periods? List them with commas: one log is made for each. Each log records which competencies you taught, for coverage reports, and feeds warm-up personalization.

> Competency ratings come from checks of student work, not from lesson logs. A lesson you don't log just isn't counted toward coverage or personalization.

**Warm-ups.** A short question at the start of class. Students answer it on their dashboard, and it's scored overnight out of 10 (length, grammar and engagement). Students can earn an extra point by replying to the feedback in at least 10 words by the date shown.

**Warm-up readiness** (the strip under the header) shows how much the system knows about each student, from checks and warm-up answers. Click a number to see those students. “Building a personalized learning profile” means the system is still learning what kind of warm-up works for them; “ready for fully personalized feedback” means it's confident.

## Parent Reports

KOS writes a weekly report for each student on your roster. It never sends one: you copy it and email it to the family from your own school account.

1. Press **✉ Parent Reports**. Each student has a Subject line and a Report. Use the Course and Student pickers to show one course or one student; one student's page on the dashboard has their report too.
2. Press **Copy subject** and paste it into a new email, then **Copy report** and paste it into the body. If the copy is refused, the text is selected for you: press Ctrl+C (⌘C on a Mac).
3. Address and send the email yourself. Read it first; you can edit it in your email before you send.

**What the report says:** each item due or turned in this week by its lesson title, with where it stands (Passed, Revising, Turned in and waiting for feedback, or Not turned in yet); a score only where you confirmed one; what the student still has to finish; and the competencies rated so far this marking period, in words, on Virginia's 1 to 5 scale where 1 is the highest. Students with nothing due this week still get a report. It ends with your name and email.

> **Who it goes to is your call.** KOS has no guardian list. Send only to the student's parents or guardians on file with the school, and not to the family of a student who is 18 or older, or whose family asked not to get these, unless the school says otherwise.

Copying a report records the time and your account, so the panel shows “Copied” next to it. It doesn't record who you sent it to, or whether you did.

## When Students Have Problems

| Student says… | What's happening | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “My card says Taking longer than usual ⚠” or “Couldn't be checked ⚠” | The check timed out, or its result could not be used. Not their fault; their work is safe | Follow the email you got. A timeout (“Feedback didn’t finish”) gives the steps to restart the check; the student does not need to do anything yet. A result that could not be used (“Feedback couldn’t be made”) asks you to have the student press Submit for Feedback again. The row on your dashboard says which. Contact your admin if it keeps happening |
| “I submitted but there's no feedback” | The check may still be in line or running | Feedback is a comment: have them open the document and click Comments. Their card says “Waiting for feedback…” or “Being checked now…” while it's on its way |
| “It says Write a little more first” | Fewer than 25 words below the grey line | Ask them to write more under “── YOUR RESPONSE BEGINS HERE ──”, above the last line of the page |
| “My assignment isn't on my dashboard” | Wrong Google account, the lesson hasn't been released, or they're not enrolled | Have them sign into their school account. Check the lesson has a start date in 📅 Lessons. If they're not on your roster either, see How Students Get Their Documents |
| “I don't see the 📊 menu in my document” | Expected: that menu doesn't run for student accounts | Send them to their dashboard to press **Submit for Feedback** |
| “I deleted part of my document” | They changed the assignment, the grey line or the bottom line | File → Version history → See version history to restore. If the grey line or the CONFIG_ID line is gone for good, contact your admin |
| “My Info is wrong” | Their name, class, period or teacher is wrong on file | They can press ✉ Request a correction in My Info, which opens an email to you. Ask your admin to fix it |
| “How do I turn it in?” | They turn in on Canvas | Copy the document's link, open the Canvas assignment, Start Assignment → Website URL, paste, Submit Assignment |

## Grading and Competency Ratings

**Grade in Canvas.** Each Canvas assignment asks for the student's document link (Website URL). The system doesn't send grades to Canvas. The AI's suggested score on a passing check, 2 (Adequate), 3 (Solid) or 4 (Exceptional), is a reference only. Higher is better.

**Rate competencies in 🎓 Competency ratings.** Each item shows the student, the competency, “Evidence so far: n met, n not met, n partially met”, and the AI-suggested rating. Press **Confirm** to accept it, or type 1–5 under “Or enter 1–5” and press **Submit**. The list shows items with a suggestion. Items with too little evidence are counted under it (“N more have too little evidence for a suggestion yet”); **Show them** lists them, and you can still enter a rating.

| Rating | Meaning (1 is the highest) |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Can teach others |
| 2 | Without supervision |
| 3 | Limited supervision |
| 4 | With supervision |
| 5 | Cannot perform |

> **The two scales run in opposite directions.** On a competency rating, 1 is the highest. On the AI's suggested score, 4 is the highest. Changing a suggested rating from 2 to 4 lowers it.

**Competencies done.** For each course, a student's competencies you have rated 1 to 3 (limited supervision or better), out of every competency in the course: 113 for 8175, 108 for 8177. Only your confirmed ratings count, not the AI's suggestions, and a later rating replaces an earlier one. It shows on the student's page and in Student Context; students see their own on their dashboard, counting every teacher's ratings.

**How the AI suggests a rating** (updated weekly). Every check records evidence for the competencies its milestones cover. One assignment counts once, at its best result, so drafts don't count against a student. With 3 or more assignments, it averages the 3 best (Met = 2, Partially met = 3, Not met = 4) and rounds. With fewer than 3, there's no suggestion yet. It only ever suggests 2, 3 or 4: 1 and 5 are yours alone.

## Marking Periods and the School Year

Use the year menu in the header. It starts on **Whole school year**. Pick a marking period, such as “Marking period 1, 2026–27”, to see only that part. A student's work belongs to the marking period it was made in. The dates each marking period ends are in ⚙ Settings.

> **Archiving is your admin's job.** At the end of a school year, the admin archives it. Archived work no longer shows on the dashboard. It's kept, not deleted, and the admin can bring a year back.

> **Before a marking period ends:** click ⚪ Not checked yet and ✏️ Checked, not passing yet, and reach out to those students.

## Words You'll See

The same list as **❓ Terms** on your dashboard.

- **Competencies done**: For each course, the competencies you have rated 1 to 3 (limited supervision or better) in Competency ratings, out of every competency in the course (the CompetencyRegistry tab). Only your own confirmed ratings count.
- **Check**: One AI evaluation of a submission against the assignment’s criteria (Flow 2). A student can be checked many times.
- **Passed / Not passing yet**: Whether the latest check met the assignment’s criteria. “Passed before” means an earlier check passed and the latest needs revision.
- **Waiting to be checked**: Submitted and in line for its check.
- **Being checked now**: The check is running.
- **Couldn’t be checked**: The check failed or timed out, not the student’s work. The timeout email says what to do.
- **AI’s suggested score**: On a passing check, the AI suggests 2 (Adequate), 3 (Solid) or 4 (Exceptional). Higher is better. It never suggests 1 or 5.
- **Competency rating (SCR)**: The Virginia CTE Student Competency Record, 1 to 5, where 1 is the highest: 1 Can teach others, 2 Without supervision, 3 Limited supervision, 4 With supervision, 5 Cannot perform. The AI suggests 2 to 4 once there are 3 pieces of evidence; 1 and 5 are yours alone.
- **Competency tiers**: Each assignment’s competencies are primary (must be shown to pass), secondary or tertiary (credited when shown).
- **Warm-up readiness**: How much the system knows about each student, from checks and warm-up answers. Click a number to see those students.
- **Personalized learning profile**: The system’s growing guess of what kind of warm-up works for a student; “ready for fully personalized feedback” means it’s confident.
- **Student Context**: One document per student that gathers their lessons, checks and warm-ups, updated weekly.
- **Current lesson**: The lesson whose start date in 📅 Lessons is the latest on or before today, per course.
- **Rollout**: The nightly job that makes and shares each lesson’s student documents from its start date (2 days ahead).
- **Feedback as a comment**: Where a student’s feedback goes: a comment on the document, or written into it. Set per course in ⚙ Settings.
- **⚙ Settings**: Turns each feature on or off per course (feedback as a comment, warm-ups on the Student Dashboard, the nightly lesson rollout, the reflection on Submit, the warm-up style lock, parent reports ready on Fridays) and sets the marking period end dates. Changes wait for Save changes, closing with unsaved changes asks first, and each saved change is kept in the SettingsLog tab.
- **3-Odd / 3-Even**: The two halves of period 3, which meet on alternate (odd or even) calendar dates.
- **Marking period**: A term code like 2627_MP1 is marking period 1 of 2026–27.
