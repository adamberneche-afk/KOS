# Rule Conflict Resolution Protocol
Governs how conflicts between rules, iterations, or directives get
detected, sized, and resolved on an ongoing basis — not just for the
hereditary documents already found, but for every future disagreement
this system produces as it keeps developing.

---

## 1. Purpose

Two purposes, not one:

1. **Catch value drift** — when a rule, pattern, or newly-inferred
   behavior quietly diverges from established precedent.
2. **Hold both sides of the partnership accountable** — this protocol
   does not only check the system for consistency. A new operator
   instruction that contradicts established precedent is exactly as much
   a "conflict" as two rules disagreeing with each other. Neither side
   gets a pass by default.

A secondary, equally real purpose: **surfacing unknown unknowns.** Most
conflicts caught by this protocol won't be "which rule wins" — they'll be
edge cases nobody had noticed yet. Treat every resolved conflict as new
context gained between the human and the harness, not just a dispute
closed.

## 2. Division of Labor (Math-Before-Muse, applied here directly)

- **GAS:** computes the divergence interval — frequency and severity of
  a given conflict pattern, tracked quantitatively over time, the same
  discipline already governing the calibration loop.
- **Auditor + Alignment (jointly):** interpret what a detected
  divergence actually *means* — whether it constitutes real drift, and
  how severe it is. This is qualitative judgment; GAS hands them a number,
  it doesn't hand them a verdict.

## 3. Severity Tiers

Every detected conflict gets sized, not treated uniformly:

| Tier | Handling |
|---|---|
| **Small** | Logged directly into context documents (PIVOTS_AND_LESSONS or equivalent) as a noted edge case. No escalation, no SMP. |
| **Medium** | Included in periodic health checks — reviewed on the same cadence as the weekly calibration loop, not immediately, but not ignored either. |
| **Large** | Addressed immediately. Warrants a full SMP and a structural change, not just a note. |

Sizing itself is a divergence-interval computation — a conflict's severity
is assessed by frequency and magnitude over time, not by a single
instance's apparent drama. This deliberately avoids the "always ask"
default that would otherwise create friction disproportionate to most
conflicts' actual stakes.

## 4. Detection

Two real paths, not one:
- **Checkable contradictions** (e.g., two classification rules producing
  different outcomes for the same input) can be GAS-detected directly.
- **Value-level or philosophical conflicts** (e.g., an authority question
  between two governing documents) require a human or Arbitrator/Alignment
  to actually notice — nothing computes this kind of tension. This
  protocol should not assume the second path can ever be fully automated.

## 5. Expected Trajectory

In response to the cognitive-debt concern the Arbitrator's own documents
raise about mechanical creep and accumulating unreconciled iterations, the
operator's direct answer: conflict frequency is expected to be higher
now, during active development and reconciliation of multiple prior
iterations, and to decrease as precedent and patterns get established.
The **cognitive debt** of holding multiple unreconciled iterations in mind
at once is a real, named cost being consciously accepted short-term — not
an oversight to fix immediately, but a cost that should visibly decline
as this protocol accumulates real precedent.

## 6. Open Questions Not Yet Resolved

Named plainly rather than silently decided:

- **Resolution outcome granularity:** when a conflict is resolved, does
  one source always retire in favor of the other, or can both survive in
  a reconciled, scoped form (e.g., "A's authority operates within B's
  execution context, not above it")? Severity tiers were resolved;
  this wasn't.
- **Relationship to Chesterton's Fence in Reverse:** should every Large
  conflict's resolution formally require "why was the superseded side
  ever adopted in the first place" as a mandatory first step, the same
  discipline already governing the hereditary documents watch list?
- **Multi-way conflicts:** with multiple real prior generations now in
  play, some conflicts may involve three or more sources, not a clean
  two-way dispute. Does sizing/resolution need a naming step first —
  explicitly listing every source actually in tension — before
  arbitration proceeds?
